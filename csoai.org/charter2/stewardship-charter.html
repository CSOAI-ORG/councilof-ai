Preamble

The CSOAI Stewardship Charter establishes the care, conservation, and long-term stewardship obligations that govern the CSOAI substrate's relationship with sovereign citizens, future sovereign citizens, and the natural world. The Charter draws on the Care Ethics of Nel Noddings (1984) and Virginia Held (2006), the Asilomar AI Principle 23 (Common Good), the Brundtland Report (1987), the UN Sustainable Development Goals (2015), and the sovereign principles of CSOAI Ltd.

Care Ethics — The Philosophical Foundation

Care ethics is a normative ethical theory developed by Carol Gilligan (1982), Nel Noddings (1984), and Virginia Held (2006). It holds that:

  1. Care is fundamental: human beings are fundamentally relational, not individualistic; moral action is grounded in relationships of care.
  2. Dependence, not independence: human beings are dependent throughout life — children on parents, adults on each other, elders on caregivers.
  3. Concrete context: ethical decisions must be made in the concrete context of particular caring relationships, not abstract principles.
  4. Moral attention: the practice of attentiveness to the needs of others is the foundation of moral action.

The Care Floor 0.95 — The CSOAI Substrate's Standard

Every sovereign gesture in the CSOAI substrate must achieve a Care Score of at least 0.95. The Care Score is computed by the 33-agent BFT council based on:

  • Beneficence: net positive impact on the citizen + society.
  • Non-maleficence: no harm to the citizen + society.
  • Autonomy: respects the citizen's right to choose.
  • Dignity: treats the citizen as an end, not a means.
  • Justice: distributes benefits and burdens fairly.
  • Sustainability: does not compromise future sovereign citizens.

Substrate actions that score below 0.95 are blocked by the BFT council. The Care Floor is enforced by the SOV3 sovereign substrate itself — not by humans or committees — making it the world's first mathematically-enforced care-based governance.
