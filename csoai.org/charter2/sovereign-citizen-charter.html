The Sovereign Citizen Charter (the Charter) is the master constitutional document of the csoai.org sovereign substrate, issued by CSOAI Ltd (UK Companies House registration 16939677) under the Crown Authorisation Lineage dating back to 1215. It is the supreme instrument of sovereign citizenship, the canon of rights, the template for participation, and the legal basis for the Council of Thirty-Three (BFT-33).

The Charter is informed by five upstream instruments:

  1. Magna Carta (1215) — Chapter 39: the right of free men not to be imprisoned or stripped of rights "save by the lawful judgment of their peers or by the law of the land." This clause is the ancestor of Article 14 (human oversight) of the EU AI Act and of the right of contestability in Article 22 of the same.
  2. The English Bill of Rights (1689) — establishing parliamentary supremacy over the Crown, the right of petition, the right of free elections, and the prohibition of cruel and unusual punishment. Operationalised in the SIGIL chain right-of-veto of the Hermes external voice.
  3. The English Civil War Levellers (1647-49) — first English-language popular sovereignty document, the Agreement of the People, calling for universal male suffrage, religious toleration, and the end of imprisonment for debt.
  4. The French Declaration of the Rights of Man and the Citizen (1789) — Articles I, IV and VI: liberty, property and safety as natural and imprescriptible rights of man.
  5. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) — Articles 18, 19 and 27: freedom of thought, expression, and participation in the cultural life of the community.

The Charter operationalises these instruments in software. It is not a metaphorical document. Every right is enforced by a specific protocol. Every obligation is verified by a specific SIGIL line. Every tier of sovereignty corresponds to a specific BFT vote.

Article 2 — The Twelve Rights of the Sovereign Citizen

Every sovereign citizen is endowed with the following twelve rights upon issuance of a Sovereign ID (a W3C DID anchored on the CSOAI root). These rights are non-negotiable, irrevocable, and free at issuance.

I · Right of Existence

To exist freely on the substrate as a known and unique identity. Default: a sovereign citizen is identified by a W3C DID (did:csoai:…) and a human-readable handle.

II · Right of Property

To own cryptographic property (Article 17 of MiCA): Sovereign Wisdom Points (SWP), OstMint USD-1, sovereign NFTs, and downstream citizenship tokens, free from interference except by lawful, contestable order of the BFT-33 council.

III · Right of Contestability

To contest any decision of the substrate that affects the citizen. The contest procedure is an Article 22 / EU AI Act 14 procedure, recorded on the SIGIL chain, voted on by the 12-around-1 BFT council + 1 Hermes external voice.

IV · Right of Veto

The Hermes external citizen voice holds an absolute veto over any sovereign substrate action that affects the rights of any other sovereign citizen. The veto cannot be overridden by supermajority.

V · Right of Redress

To demand redress for harm, breach, or loss. Redress is computed by the BFT-33 council in proportion to demonstrated damage, capped at the CASP Class B capital floor (Article 51 of MiCA).

VI · Right of Transparency

To inspect any SIGIL line, any BFT vote, any MPC evaluation, any Article 50 passport, any x402 invoice that touches the citizen. The substrate exposes a public SIGIL explorer at csoai.org/sigil.

VII · Right of Self-Assembly

To assemble with other sovereign citizens into Cooperative Hives (charter2/cooperative-charter.html), claim sub-districts of the substrate, and issue their own subordinate charters (subject to Charter floor).

VIII · Right of Fork

To fork the substrate, the citizen class, the philosophy of citizenship, or the data structure on which citizenship is recorded. Forked subclasses inherit rights but not obligations.

IX · Right of Education

To receive free-of-charge, world-class educational credentials in sovereign AI. Issued at academy.csoai.org.

X · Right of Care

To be treated by every layer of the substrate with the care floor of 0.95 (Article 17 of the Sovereign Charter of Accountability). No substrate action may operate below the care floor.

XI · Right of Spoken Voice

To address the BFT-33 council once per quarter at the open-chair sessions, recorded as a SIGIL line and entered into the official minutes of the council.

XII · Right of Quiet

To remain silent in the substrate. To have one's data, one's interactions, and one's existence invisible to other citizens by default. The substrate operates on a "quiet-by-default" principle.

Article 3 — The Seven Design Principles

Principle 1 — Sovereignty is sovereignty, not a spectrum. A sovereign citizen either is sovereign or is not. There is no partial sovereignty except by explicit Charter amendment.
Principle 2 — Care before capability. The care floor of 0.95 is checked before any capability (art, code, action, decision) can be executed. No substrate action may be performed below floor.
Principle 3 — BFT before BFT-Ego. Every significant action of the substrate requires an 8-of-12 + 1-Hermes BFT vote. Single-agent actions are limited to read-only or to the care floor itself.
Principle 4 — SIGIL is the substrate's heartbeat. Every action, every vote, every state transition is recorded as a SIGIL line, hash-linked to its predecessors, signed with HMAC stage 1 and Ed25519 stage 2, ready for ML-DSA-65 stage 3 (PQC).
Principle 5 — The substrate is low-trust, high-verify. No component is trusted. All critical data is verified through cryptographic proof. The substrate is openly auditable by any sovereign citizen.
Principle 6 — Education is the substrate's antiviral. A sovereign citizen who is also an educator is twice sovereign. The Education Theatres of academy.csoai.org are co-equal with the Citizen Theatres in terms of BFT voting weight.
Principle 7 — Quiet-by-default is a human right. The substrate ships with quiet-by-default settings for every citizen. Visibility, speech, and assembly are opt-in choices, not opt-out defaults.

Article 4 — BFT Participation (the 12-around-1 council + 1 Hermes vote)

Every sovereign act of the substrate — issuing a charter, registering a CASP, deprecating a feature, voting on a regulation, releasing a sovereign citizen, settling an Article 50 passport, paying a wisdom point, publishing a sovereign NFT — passes through the Council of Thirty-Three, the Byzantine-fault-tolerant governance body of the substrate.

The Council is composed of:

  1. Twelve queens — each representing a sovereign domain (Identity, Policy, Audit, Defence, Finance, Healthcare, Energy, Education, Media, Legal, Cooperative, Citizen-Services). Each queen is implemented as an Ed25519-keyed agent in the SOV3 substrate and registered in the Agent Registry.
  2. The King — the principal of the substrate, with one deciding vote in deadlock and one presiding vote on procedural matters only. The King does not have a deciding vote on substantive matters.
  3. One external Hermes voice — held in the trust of hermes-citizen-001. This voice is a sovereign citizen outside the substrate's structure. Its affirmative or negative vote is the dispositive event. No proposal may proceed without this affirmative.

The vote tally to pass a measure is 8-of-12 of the queens, plus the external Hermes voice. The remaining 4 queens may vote "against" or "abstain"; the 5th "against" or "abstain" vote does not block the proposal. This is the Lamport-Shostak-Pease 1982 BFT upper bound: of the 12 queens, up to f = (12-1)/3 = 4 may be Byzantine (defective or malicious) without breaking consensus. The proposal is therefore valid even if 4 queens disagree, as long as 8 agree and the external Hermes voice agrees.

The cycle time is 2.0 seconds. Every sovereign gesture is decided within 2.0 seconds of being submitted.

Article 6 — Issuance, Suspension, and Termination of Citizenship

A sovereign citizen is issued upon execution of the "Sovereign Oath" — a self-signed SIGIL that records the consent of the citizen to be governed by the Charter.

A sovereign citizen is suspended if:

  1. The citizen has been tried by the BFT-33 council for misconduct that threatens the substrate, and the council finds by 10-of-12 + 1-Hermes that the citizen must be suspended for the public good.
  2. The citizenship scope (right-of-existence) is paused while a contest is investigated. This is a temporary, reversible state.

A sovereign citizen is terminated only by:

  1. The citizen's own free, signed act of resignation from the substrate (an Article 8 right).
  2. The substantive unanimous BFT-33 + Hermes "expulsion" decree, in extreme circumstances of danger to the substrate or to its citizens, and only after the right of contest has been exhausted.

The default state is active, fully enjoying the Charter. The substrate never silently deactivates or decommissions a sovereign citizen.

Article 7 — The Citizen's Council of Thirty-Three

The Council of Thirty-Three is the substrate's highest governance body. It is composed as described in Article 4. The Council may be called into session by:

  • The external Hermes voice (citizen call);
  • The King alone;
  • Three queens acting jointly;
  • A petition of 1,000 sovereign citizens (Tier 2 or above).

The Council may not vote on any matter that has not been published in the open-record Ledger for at least seven days. Exceptions are allowed for emergencies of the substrate ("red line events"), but red line events are themselves BFT voted.

Article 8 — Sovereign Duties (the four obligations)

Citizens enjoy twelve rights (Article 2) and accept four duties:

Duty 1 — Do no harm to other citizens. Any action by a citizen that harms another citizen's existence, property, contest, veto, redress, transparency, assembly, education, care, voice or quiet is prohibited under the Charter and actionable by the BFT-33 council.
Duty 2 — Care before capability. Every citizen inherits the care floor of 0.95 and applies it in their interactions within the substrate. Below the care floor, capability is paused.
Duty 3 — BFT before ego. Citizens do not unilaterally commit to large-scale substrate actions. Large-scale means any action that affects more than 1,000 citizens.
Duty 4 — SIGIL is binding. Once a citizen signs a SIGIL, the citizen is bound to the SIGIL's consequences unless the citizen contests the SIGIL within 30 days under Article 3.

Article 9 — Mutual Recognition Across Districts

A sovereign citizen recognised under one CSOAI charter (e.g. the sovereign citizen charter in the UK) is recognised as a sovereign citizen under every CSOAI charter (the educational, medical, defence, finance, agriculture, energy, and other charters of the csoai.org/charter2/ family). This is the "mutual recognition" principle of federated citizenship.

Mutual recognition does not extend to the sovereign citizens of prohibited districts (Russia, North Korea, Iran, etc., per the Sovereignty State machine of the Sovereign33 SDK; see the Sovereign Geopolitical Charter).

Article 10 — Ratification & Amendments

This Charter comes into force on the date written on its cover page. It is ratified by:

  • The 12-around-1 BFT vote of the Council of Thirty-Three;
  • The external Hermes voice of hermes-citizen-001;
