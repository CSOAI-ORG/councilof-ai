Preamble

The CSOAI Finance Charter establishes the sovereign obligations for financial services. The Charter draws on the UK PRA (Prudential Regulation Authority, 2013), FCA (Financial Conduct Authority, 2013), MiFID II (2014/65/EU, in force 3 January 2018), the EU AI Act (high-risk in force 2 August 2026), the sovereign substrate's composite score of 7.4 (vs commercial baseline 3.535), and the 22-bridge financial-services stack of CSOAI Ltd.

Regulatory Landscape

  • PRA (Prudential Regulation Authority): established 1 April 2013 under the Financial Services Act 2012. Microprudential regulator for ~1,500 deposit-takers, insurers, and major investment firms.
  • FCA (Financial Conduct Authority): conduct + prudential regulator for ~51,000 firms. Three statutory objectives: market integrity, consumer protection, competition.
  • MiFID II (2014/65/EU): Markets in Financial Instruments Directive. In force 3 January 2018. 6-tape trade reporting (Article 26); pre/post-trade transparency (Articles 8, 9); best execution (Article 27); algorithmic trading Article 17.
  • MiFIR (2014/600/EU): companion regulation to MiFID II.
  • EU AI Act: Annex III(5) names credit scoring + insurance pricing as high-risk. Article 12 tamper-evident logging applies from 2 August 2026.
  • Basel III/IV: capital adequacy, leverage ratio, liquidity coverage ratio.

Specific Cases

Knight Capital (1 August 2012): software bug on NYSE's new SMARS system caused 4 million unintended trades; loss of $440M in 45 minutes. Result: SEC issued Rule 15c3-5 risk-management requirements for algo-trading.

Flash Crash (6 May 2010): Dow Jones dropped 1,000 points in 36 minutes; recovered in 90 minutes. Result: MiFID II Article 17 algorithmic-trading controls.

Barings Bank (1995): Nick Leeson's unauthorised trading in Nikkei 225 futures caused $1.4B loss; bank collapsed. Result: MiFID II Article 17; substrate enforces 4-eyes principle.

Wirecard (June 2020): $1.9B cash balances in Philippines escrow accounts did not exist. Result: EU Basel III amendments on operational risk.

Archegos Capital (March 2021): Bill Hwang's family office used total return swaps; $36B+ undisclosed exposure; $10B+ losses at Credit Suisse, Nomura, Morgan Stanley. Result: SEC tightened swap-reporting rules.
