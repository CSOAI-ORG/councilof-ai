Preamble

The CSOAI Transparency Charter establishes the sovereign citizen's right to know what the substrate does with their data, why every AI decision is made, and how every sovereign action is processed. The Charter draws on GDPR Articles 13–14 (transparency obligations), GDPR Article 22 (right to explanation), EU AI Act Article 50 (transparency obligations for AI systems), the sovereign Compliance Passport (W3C VC + Ed25519), and the sovereign substrate principles of CSOAI Ltd.

GDPR Articles 13–14 — Information to be Provided to the Data Subject

  • Article 13(1): identity and contact details of the controller; contact details of the DPO; purposes of processing; legal basis; recipients; transfers to third countries.
  • Article 13(2): retention period; legitimate interests; right to access, rectify, erase, restrict, object, portability; right to lodge complaint; existence of automated decision-making including profiling.
  • Article 13(3): if controller intends further processing, the citizen has the right to be informed of that intention.
  • Article 14: same information as Article 13 but for data not collected from the data subject (e.g. third-party data).

GDPR Article 22 — Right Not to Be Subject to Automated Decision-Making

The data subject shall have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing, including profiling, which produces legal effects concerning him or her or similarly significantly affects him or her, unless: (a) it is necessary for entering into a contract; (b) it is authorised by Union or Member State law; (c) it is based on the data subject's explicit consent.

EU AI Act Article 50 — Transparency Obligations (verbatim)

1. Providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed in such a way that the natural persons concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious to a reasonably well-informed natural person taking into account the circumstances and context of use. This obligation does not apply where it would be obvious to a reasonably well-informed natural person that they are interacting with an AI system in the circumstances and context of use, including for the purpose of detecting AI-generated content.
2. Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. Providers shall ensure that their technical solutions are effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as this is technically feasible, taking into account the specific circumstances and limitations of the use case type and the nature of the content.

Specific Cases

Bavarian Lager v UK ICO (ECJ 28 July 2008, then-CJEU; UK High Court on remand 2010): ICO refused to disclose names of recipients of an FOI request. Bavarian Lager argued Article 8(2) transparency. UK High Court held the ICO was correct to refuse — but the case established that transparency does not override privacy of third parties.

Google Spain v AEPD (CJEU 13 May 2014): established the right to be forgotten as a transparency obligation.

UBER v Aslam (UK Employment Appeal Tribunal 2017): drivers are "workers", not self-employed. Result: uber's automated decision-making systems must respect worker transparency rights.

ACLU v Clearview AI (US, ongoing): Clearview AI's face recognition database of 30 billion images scraped from social media. Result: GDPR Article 9 + Article 22 transparency challenges.

Italy's ChatGPT ban (March 2023): Italian Garante banned ChatGPT after data breach. Result: GDPR Article 13 transparency requirements for AI chatbots clarified.

Modern Application (2026)

  1. Sovereign Compliance Passport: every sovereign action issues a W3C VC + Ed25519 + PQC ML-DSA-65 signed passport with the basis + logic + alternatives + model version.
