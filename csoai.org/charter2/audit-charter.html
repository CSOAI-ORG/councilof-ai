Preamble

The CSOAI Audit Charter establishes the immutable, post-quantum-signed audit trail for every action in the sovereign substrate. The Charter draws on SOC 2 Type II (AICPA, 2024 revised), ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (AI Management Systems), the EU AI Act Article 12 (tamper-evident logging), the EU AI Act Article 19 (Quality Management System for high-risk AI), and the sovereign SIGIL chain of CSOAI Ltd.

Foundational Documents

  • SOC 2 (AICPA Trust Service Criteria, 2017, revised 2022): five criteria — Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy. SOC 2 Type II reports on operating effectiveness over a period (typically 6-12 months).
  • ISO/IEC 42001:2023: AI Management System standard. 10 clauses (4–13), 47 controls in Annex A. Specifies requirements for AI governance, risk management, and continuous improvement.
  • ISO/IEC 27001:2022: Information Security Management System standard. 93 controls in Annex A. Specifies requirements for ISMS.
  • EU AI Act Article 12: "High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events ('logs') over the lifetime of the system."
  • EU AI Act Article 19: AI providers shall put a quality management system in place ensuring compliance with this Regulation.
  • EU AI Act Article 72: Post-market monitoring by AI providers; ongoing assessment of compliance.

The SIGIL Chain Architecture

Every sovereign action is appended to the SIGIL chain with:

  1. Timestamp: ISO 8601 + millisecond precision + UTC offset.
  2. Actor: sovereign citizen ID (Ed25519 public key).
  3. Action: type of sovereign gesture (data access, AI decision, consent grant, etc).
  4. Basis: GDPR Article 6 lawful basis, EU AI Act Article 12/14/22 reference, sovereign policy reference.
  5. Hash chain: each record includes sha256(previous_record). Breaking the chain invalidates all subsequent records.
  6. Ed25519 signature: each record signed by sovereign citizen + sovereign substrate.
  7. PQC ML-DSA-65 backup signature: post-quantum backup signature to survive quantum attack.
  8. Rekor transparency log: Sigstore-style transparency log entry to prevent backdating.

Specific Cases

Wirecard scandal (June 2020): Wirecard AG, the German fintech, collapsed when €1.9B of cash balances in Philippines escrow accounts were found not to exist. EY's audit failed to detect the fraud. Result: enhanced requirements for audit trail immutability in EU financial services.

Facebook–Cambridge Analytica (March 2018): 87 million users' personal data harvested without consent for political microtargeting. Result: ICO fine £500K, FTC fine $5B.

Cambridge Analytica whistleblower testimony (April 2018): Christopher Wylie revealed that personal data harvested via a personality quiz app was used for political profiling. Result: GDPR Article 7 strengthened, explicit consent requirements clarified.

SolarWinds post-mortem (2021): investigation revealed that SolarWinds's audit logs were altered by attackers to hide malicious activity. Result: substrate requires SIGIL chain + Rekor transparency log to prevent log tampering.

Modern Application (2026)

The CSOAI Audit Charter is implemented in the sovereign substrate through:

  1. SIGIL chain: every action immutable, signed Ed25519 + PQC ML-DSA-65, hash-chained.
  2. Rekor transparency log: every SIGIL entry mirrored to Sigstore Rekor public log.
  3. Compliance Passport: every sovereign action issues a signed W3C VC + Ed25519 passport.
  4. Auditor access: any auditor (CSOAI internal, regulator, customer) can verify the SIGIL chain offline in their browser.
  5. AI Act Article 12 compliance: every high-risk AI decision is logged with full input + output + basis + model version.
