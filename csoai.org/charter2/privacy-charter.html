Preamble

The CSOAI Privacy Charter establishes the sovereign citizen's right to privacy in the age of AI, cloud extraction, and ubiquitous surveillance. The Charter draws on the 99 Articles of the GDPR, the UK Data Protection Act 2018, the Council of Europe Convention 108+ (2018), the EU AI Act (2024/1689), and the sovereign substrate principles of CSOAI Ltd.

The 99 Articles of GDPR — Summary by Chapter

  • Chapter I (Arts. 1–4): General provisions — subject matter, material scope, territorial scope, definitions.
  • Chapter II (Arts. 5–11): Principles — lawfulness, fairness, transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, accountability; special categories of personal data.
  • Chapter III (Arts. 12–23): Rights of the data subject — transparent information, communication, right of access, right to rectification, right to erasure, right to restriction, right to data portability, right to object, automated decision-making.
  • Chapter IV (Arts. 24–43): Controller and processor — responsibility, data protection by design and by default, security of processing, breach notification, DPIAs, prior consultation, DPO.
  • Chapter V (Arts. 44–50): Cross-border transfers — general principles, adequacy decisions, SCCs, BCRs, derogations.
  • Chapter VI (Arts. 51–59): Supervisory authorities — independent status, powers, tasks.
  • Chapter VII (Arts. 60–76): Cooperation and consistency — mutual assistance, joint operations, dispute resolution, EDPB.
  • Chapter VIII (Arts. 77–84): Remedies, liability, penalties — right to lodge complaint, judicial remedies, compensation.
  • Chapter IX (Arts. 85–91): Specific processing situations — employment, public authorities, public interest, health, research, archiving.
  • Chapter X (Arts. 92–93): Delegated and implementing acts.
  • Chapter XI (Arts. 94–99): Final provisions — repeals, relationship to Directive 2016/680, evaluation, entry into force.

Specific Cases

Google Spain v AEPD and Mario Costeja González (CJEU C-131/12, 13 May 2014): "Right to be forgotten" established; Google must delist inadequate, irrelevant, or no-longer-relevant search results.

Breach at Marriott International (November 2018): 500 million customer records exposed (names, addresses, passport numbers, payment cards). ICO fine £18.4M (reduced from £99M).

Equifax data breach (2017): 147 million Americans' personal data exposed. Settlement $700M.

Belgian DPA v Facebook (October 2022): €2.8M fine for cookie-tracking violations under GDPR ePrivacy.

TikTok €345M fine (September 2023): Dutch DPA fined TikTok for failing to obtain parental consent for children's accounts.

Meta €1.2B fine (May 2023): Irish DPC fined Meta for transferring EU user data to the US in violation of Schrems II.

UK DPA 2018 — Divergences from GDPR

  • Section 10: lawful bases (mirrors GDPR Article 6).
  • Section 14: special categories (mirrors GDPR Article 9).
  • Section 15: substantial public interest conditions (Schedule 1 Part 2 contains 23 conditions, more specific than GDPR Art. 9(2)(g)).
  • Section 170: intelligence services processing — different regime from GDPR Article 23.
  • Schedule 21: immigration exemption.
