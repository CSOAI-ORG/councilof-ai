Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT) — The Mathematical Foundation

Byzantine Fault Tolerance was introduced by Leslie Lamport, Robert Shostak, and Marshall Pease in their 1982 paper "The Byzantine Generals Problem." The problem: a group of generals surrounding a city must agree on a common plan of action (attack or retreat) via messages, but some generals may be traitors (Byzantine) who send conflicting messages. The solution: BFT consensus algorithms can tolerate up to f = (n-1)/3 Byzantine nodes and still reach consensus.

Castro-Liskow PBFT (1999): Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance — the first practical BFT algorithm for asynchronous networks. O(n²) message complexity. Used in Hyperledger Fabric, JPMorgan Quorum.

HotStuff (Yin et al. 2019): 3-phase BFT with linear message complexity. Used in Facebook's Libra/Diem.

HotStuff-2 (Nausberg et al. 2024): 2-phase BFT; further reduced message complexity.

Knight Capital Group (August 1, 2012): software bug in NYSE's new trading system caused Knight to execute unintended trades for 45 minutes; loss of $440M in 30 minutes. Result: SEC now requires continuous accountability + human oversight for high-frequency trading.
