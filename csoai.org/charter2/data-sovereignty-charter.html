Preamble

The CSOAI Data Sovereignty Charter establishes the principle that every sovereign citizen retains ownership and control of their personal data, and that no sovereign actor — public, private, or foreign — may extract, process, or transfer that data without the citizen's explicit, informed, and revocable consent. The Charter draws on the GDPR (2016/679), the Schrems II decision (CJEU C-311/18, 16 July 2020), the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (adopted 10 July 2023), the UK DPA 2018, the CLOUD Act (US, 2018), and the sovereign substrate principles of CSOAI Ltd.

Foundational Documents

  • GDPR Article 8(1): "Processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies..." enumerates six lawful bases (consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, legitimate interests).
  • GDPR Article 17: Right to erasure ("right to be forgotten") — controller must erase personal data without undue delay when consent is withdrawn, data is no longer necessary, or processing is unlawful.
  • GDPR Article 44: "Any transfer of personal data which is undergoing processing or is intended for processing after transfer to a third country... shall take place only if... the conditions in this chapter are complied with by the controller and processor."
  • GDPR Article 45: Adequacy decisions — the Commission may decide that a third country ensures a level of protection essentially equivalent to the EU.
  • Schrems II (CJEU 16 July 2020): invalidated the EU-US Privacy Shield; required SCCs to be assessed for each transfer; established that US surveillance (Section 702 FISA, EO 12333) does not provide adequate protection.
  • UK DPA 2018 Section 8: equivalent of GDPR Article 6 lawful bases for the UK after Brexit.
  • CLOUD Act 2018 (US, 18 USC §2713): US authorities can compel US providers to disclose data stored anywhere in the world, including on EU servers — directly conflicting with GDPR Article 48 (no third-country governmental access without international agreement).

Specific Cases

Schrems v Facebook Ireland (Schrems II, CJEU C-311/18, 16 July 2020): Max Schrems, an Austrian privacy activist, challenged Facebook Ireland's transfer of his personal data to the US under Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs). The Court held that US surveillance laws do not provide protection essentially equivalent to EU law. Result: 1,500+ companies had to revise their transfer mechanisms.

La Quadrature du Net v CNIL (CJEU C-511/18, 6 October 2020): French data protection authority's anti-Trump injunction against Google Analytics held lawful; expanded Schrems II's reach to all transfer tools.

Belgian DPA v IAB Europe (CJEU C-604/22, 7 March 2024): Real-Time Bidding consent framework held inadequate under GDPR; consent must be specific, informed, and freely revocable per Article 4(11).

Max Schrems v Meta Platforms (CJEU C-446/21, 4 July 2023): Meta's "contract" lawful basis for behavioural advertising rejected; "consent" is the only lawful basis for non-essential processing.
