Preamble

The Geneva Conventions are four treaties + three additional protocols that establish the standards of international humanitarian law (IHL). The original four conventions were adopted in 1949; the three protocols were added in 1977 + 2005. The conventions are the foundation of IHL + are ratified by 196 states.

Convention I — Wounded and Sick in Armed Forces (1949)

Convention I protects wounded + sick soldiers on land. Requires: (a) respect + protection of wounded + sick; (b) medical care for the wounded; (c) protection of medical personnel + facilities; (d) the Red Cross + Red Crescent emblems.

Convention II — Wounded, Sick, and Shipwrecked at Sea (1949)

Convention II extends Convention I to naval warfare. Same protections + Red Crystal emblem.

Convention III — Prisoners of War (1949)

Convention III protects prisoners of war (POWs). POWs must be: (a) treated humanely; (b) not tortured or coerced; (c) provided adequate food + clothing + shelter; (d) allowed to communicate with family; (e) repatriated after hostilities end.

Convention IV — Civilians in Time of War (1949)

Convention IV protects civilians in occupied territory + civilian internees. Civilians must be: (a) treated humanely; (b) protected from violence; (c) not deported; (d) not used as human shields.

Article 36 — Weapons Review (Additional Protocol I)

Article 36 of Additional Protocol I requires states to determine whether new weapons would comply with IHL. This includes AI-enabled autonomous weapons. The CSOAI substrate implements Article 36 weapons review for every sovereign defence action.

Common Article 3 — Non-International Armed Conflict

Common Article 3 applies to all armed conflicts including non-international ones. It prohibits: (a) violence to life + person; (b) taking of hostages; (c) outrages upon personal dignity; (d) summary justice.
