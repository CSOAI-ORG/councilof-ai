Article 2 — The History of the MIT License

The MIT License was developed at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in the late 1980s. It became one of the most widely-used permissive free software licenses, alongside the BSD licenses. The license was originally designed for X Window System (X11) at MIT in the 1980s. The X Consortium adopted the MIT License as the standard license for X11 software. Since then, it has been adopted by countless projects including jQuery, Ruby on Rails, Node.js, React, Vue.js, Angular, Swift, Kotlin, TensorFlow, PyTorch, Kubernetes, Docker, Istio, and many more.

Article 3 — The MIT License + Sovereign AI

The MIT License is the natural choice for the CSOAI Layer-0 substrate. Article 5 — The 1 Obligation of the MIT License

The MIT License has only one obligation: include the copyright notice + the permission notice in all copies or substantial portions of the software. That's it. No copyleft. No "share-alike". No derivative-source-disclosure. No patent grant. The MIT License is the most permissive of the major open-source licenses.
