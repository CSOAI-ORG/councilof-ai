What "sovereign" actually means

"Sovereign" is one of the most overused words in AI marketing right now. Everyone claims it. Almost nobody achieves it.

For SOV3, sovereign is a property with six specific tests. If you fail any of them, you are not sovereign:

  1. No foreign API calls. Every model in your sovereign stack must be open-weights (MIT, Apache 2.0, Open, or your own auditable fine-tune). No DeepSeek-routed-through-US-clouds. No GPT-5-behind-an-API. The data must stay in your sovereign substrate.
  2. Open weights. Closed weights are foreign. If you can't audit a model's pre-training, post-training, and serving, you can't trust it.
  3. Audit-chained SIGILs. Every action — query, response, tool call, BFT vote — must be SIGIL-signed (Ed25519 + post-quantum ML-DSA-65). The chain is immutable, public, and verifiable. You can prove what the AI did, when, and to whom.
  4. DORADO 1-click sovereignty switch. You must be able to switch sovereignty modes (EAST/WEST, UK/EU, etc.) at any time. The mode change is itself SIGIL-recorded.
  5. 12-around-1 BFT council for sensitive actions. No single queen decides. Sensitive actions require a 2/3 majority of a 12-member council. This is not censorship — it's distributed sovereignty.
  6. Article 50 watermarking. Every output must be watermarked per EU AI Act Article 50. The watermark is provable via SIGIL chain audit. Removal of the watermark is a violation of law.
If a vendor claims "sovereign" but is behind a single proprietary API call, has closed weights, doesn't SIGIL-sign, and can't be audited — that vendor is not sovereign. They are selling you a foreign dependency with marketing.
