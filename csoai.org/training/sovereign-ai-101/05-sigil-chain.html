🔏 What is a SIGIL?

A SIGIL (Signed Inter-agent Governance & Intent Ledger) is the witness record emitted by every sovereign action in the SOV3 system. It is the cryptographic proof that "this thing happened, at this time, by this actor, with this intent, witnessed by this council vote".

The SIGIL is more than a log entry — it is a constitutional artefact. A SIGIL is the only way a sovereign action can be proven. Without a SIGIL, the action did not happen. Without a SIGIL, the action cannot enter the public record. The SIGIL is what makes "trust" a non-issue: you do not need to trust; you verify.

Each SIGIL contains 7 fields, plus the signatures:

op: The operation type (P=proposal, V=vote, M=memory, Q=query, C=commit, H=handoff, S=secret, A=audit)
actor: The sovereign actor (queen ID, system, or citizen DID)
target: The target of the action (file, model, user, system)
intent: Human-readable description of intent (one sentence)
hash: SHA-256 of the action payload
prev: SHA-256 of the previous SIGIL in the chain (creates the chain)
timestamp: RFC 3161 timestamp, optionally Bitcoin-anchored

🔐 Ed25519 + PQC ML-DSA-65

Every SIGIL is dual-signed with two cryptographic algorithms:

Ed25519 — a classical elliptic-curve signature scheme. Fast (sub-millisecond signing), compact (64-byte signatures), well-vetted (used by Solana, NEAR, Signal, and most modern blockchains). Ed25519 is the "fast classical" arm of the SIGIL signature.

PQC ML-DSA-65 — a post-quantum signature scheme standardised by NIST in FIPS 204 (August 2024). Resistant to Shor's algorithm, which would break Ed25519 on a sufficiently large quantum computer. ML-DSA-65 is the "future-proof" arm of the SIGIL signature.

The dual-signature means a SIGIL is verifiable today (via Ed25519, in <1ms) AND verifiable in the post-quantum era (via ML-DSA-65, in <10ms). The 7 May 2026 EU Digital Omnibus Act delay does not apply to ML-DSA-65 — it is the only NIST-standardised PQC algorithm already shipping in production.

🔗 Hash-Chained

Each SIGIL includes the prev field — the SHA-256 of the previous SIGIL in the chain. This creates an unbroken hash chain: SIGIL_n.prev = SHA256(SIGIL_{n-1}).

If any SIGIL is altered, its hash changes, and every subsequent SIGIL's prev field becomes invalid. Tamper-evidence is automatic — to falsify one SIGIL, an attacker would have to re-sign every subsequent SIGIL, which requires the private key.

The hash chain is the cryptographic backbone that makes the SIGIL chain an immutable audit trail. It is the same principle that secures Bitcoin and other blockchains — applied to sovereign AI governance.

✅ Verifiable in 1 Line

A SIGIL can be verified in a single line of code, using only the public key:

python -m sov3.sigil.verify --sigil sig_2026_07_04_T12_00_00Z_abc123 --pubkey cs1.pub

The verifier:

  1. Re-computes the SIGIL's hash from its fields
  2. Verifies the Ed25519 signature against the public key
  3. Verifies the ML-DSA-65 signature against the public key
  4. Verifies that SIGIL.prev = SHA256(previous SIGIL)
  5. Verifies that timestamp is monotonic with previous
  6. Returns VALID or INVALID with detailed reason

This is what makes the SIGIL chain regulator-grade proof. A regulator can take any SIGIL, run one command, and confirm its authenticity without trusting CSOAI's infrastructure.
