Scope

The UK Cabinet Office pillar maps CSOAI Ltd (UK 16939677) to the Government Digital Service (GDS) Service Standard, the public-sector AI transparency obligations, the Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS), and the Central Digital and Data Office (CDDO) responsible-AI guidance. This pillar governs any deployment of the sovereign system into UK central government, devolved administrations, or arms-length bodies.

Adopted alongside the Sovereign Charter on Saturday 4 July 2026 09:00 BST, T-minus-4-days from launch.

Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard

Every public-sector deployment of the sovereign system produces an ATRS-compliant record, including:

  • Plain-English description of the system and its purpose.
  • Owner organisation and accountable senior responsible owner.
  • Scope and intended benefits.
  • Training data, model architecture, and update cadence.
  • Risks, mitigations, and human oversight arrangements.
  • Performance metrics, fairness audits, and public-version documentation.

The ATRS record is published on the public AI register within 14 days of deployment. SIGIL-signed evidence is appended on each update.

Five Articles of Public-Sector AI

  1. Article I — Sovereign by Default

    UK central-government deployments must run in the UK sovereign region. No cross-border data flow without per-instance consent and SIGIL emission. The sovereign system refuses to export citizen data to non-adequacy jurisdictions.

  2. Article II — Citizen-Facing Transparency

    Every citizen-facing AI surface displays a plain-language disclosure: "This is an AI system operated by [organisation] for [purpose]. You can request human review at any time."

  3. Article III — Right to Human Review

    Automated decisions affecting citizen rights, benefits, or status trigger automatic human review on request. The sovereign system implements this at the architecture layer via the 12-around-1 BFT council of humans.

  4. Article IV — GDS Service Standard

    The 14-point GDS Service Standard is met point-by-point: understand user needs, solve a whole problem, provide a joined-up experience, make the service simple to use, ensure everyone can use it, have a multidisciplinary team, use agile methods, iterate and improve frequently, deploy a working version quickly, operate a reliable service, ensure the service can be measured, use common standards, make the service secure, make new source code open.

  5. Article V — Public Value Audit

    Every public-sector deployment publishes a quarterly public-value audit: cost vs benefit, citizen satisfaction, error rates, redress outcomes, demographic equity. SIGIL-signed.

Mapping to CDDO Responsible-AI Guidance

CSOAI Ltd maps its sovereign tooling to the CDDO's 6 principles of responsible AI:

  1. Lawful, ethical and safe — Care Charter + Crown Authorization.
  2. Appropriate transparency — ATRS + Article 50 watermarking + SIGIL explorer.
  3. Accountability — 12-around-1 BFT council + named accountable SRO per deployment.
  4. Bias, discrimination and fairness — Demographic matrix 10x6=60 user-scenarios audited pre-deployment.
  5. Individual rights and redress — Right-to-be-forgotten + human review + ICO complaints path.
  6. Socially beneficial — Public-value audit + Crown Authorization revocation clause.

Ratification

The UK Cabinet Office pillar enters force on Saturday 4 July 2026 at 09:00 BST. It binds any UK central-government deployment, with public ATRS records, regulator API access, and a 90-day review cycle.
