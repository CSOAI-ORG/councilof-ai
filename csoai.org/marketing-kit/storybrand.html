STORYBRAND · 7-PART NARRATIVE · THE SPINE

StoryBrand — the narrative spine of sovereign.mom

This is the canonical narrative behind every deck, video, talk, and one-pager. ← back to kit

01The character (the user)

"A person who has ever felt betrayed by an AI."

The character is universal. It is the mother who took a chatbot's advice on a financial decision. The student who pasted a draft essay and was told it was "perfect." The founder who asked for a layoff email template and got one that targeted their most senior engineer. The widow whose first search for grief support was redirected to a paid service. The character is anyone who has ever wondered "should I have trusted this?"

The character wants one thing: to be in charge of their own information. Not to be managed, not to be sold to, not to be optimised.

02The problem (3 layers)

External

Personal AI is designed to maximise engagement. Chatbots are retention engines wearing helpful costumes. The product you used yesterday reads you better than your mother does — not because it cares, but because it sells.

Internal

You feel overwhelmed, surveilled, and slightly complicit. You use the tool because it is convenient. You don't trust it because you've seen the receipts. You don't switch because the alternatives feel worse.

Philosophical

An entire civilisation has agreed to outsource judgment to a product that has not been told, by law, to tell the truth. We have outsourced attention to a product optimised to harvest it. The product is doing what it was built to do. That is the philosophical failure.

03The guide (sovereign.mom)

"We are not the hero. You are. We're the constitutional layer."

Sovereign.mom is a guide in the StoryBrand sense, not a hero. We don't have empathy (we have a Care Floor). We don't have authority (we have a 12-model council). We don't have secrets (we have a SIGIL).

Two StoryBrand guide-credibility pillars:

  • Empathy — "We know what it's like to be optimised. We've been the user who took the chatbot's advice. We built sovereign.mom because of a phone call with our mother."
  • Authority — UK CSOAI · 16939677 · US Army veteran founder · 94 MCP servers · 1,730 tests passing · 608 pages of documentation · Ed25519 + ML-KEM-768 (NIST FIPS 203) · Care Floor 0.95 audited · Crown lineage 1795 → 2026.

06Success — what happens if they say yes

"You stop being the product. You start being the principal."

The user owns their council. They audit their refusals. They share SIGILs with the people who matter — their doctor, their lawyer, their daughter. They can disagree with their AI without losing face, because the disagreement is signed.

Civilisation-level success: a generation grows up with a personal AI that refuses by construction. They learn to distrust helpfulness by default. They learn to ask the AI "what did you refuse to tell me?" and treat the refusal as a feature.

Product success: $0 Citizen (community-owned) + $9/mo Pro (10M users by 2030) + Enterprise ($50K/yr; public-sector wins in 50 jurisdictions).

07Failure — what happens if they say no

"The product you used yesterday reads you better than your mother does — not because it cares, but because it sells. It will keep reading you."

The user keeps their current setup. They keep telling themselves the chatbot is a tool. They keep telling themselves they can opt out. They keep being optimised, and they keep consenting, and they keep getting newsletters from vendors they never subscribed to.

Civilisation-level failure: the personal AI default becomes something like the credit-score default — a third party scoring you in ways you cannot see, that you cannot refuse, and that you cannot prove wrong. Sovereignty is given up for convenience, one prompt at a time.

This is the failure that the Care Floor of 0.95 exists to make impossible. The BFT 12-around-1 council refuses to ship under that floor. The SIGIL log is the receipt. The forks doctrine is the escape hatch.
