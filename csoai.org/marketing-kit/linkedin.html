02The technical post — "How we built a 12-model BFT council"

Angle: technical. Architecture, code, trade-offs. Aimed at AI engineers. 510 words

LINKEDIN 2 — copy verbatim How we shipped a 12-model BFT council in production for $0/month per user. This is the architecture of sovereign.mom's consent gate. Engineers — pull up a chair. The problem. We need every response in a personal-AI product to be signed, refused-if-uncertain, and reproducible in audit. A single model can't do that — a model is a vendor, a vendor is a regulatory target (EU AI Act Art. 51, US EO 14110, China interim measures), and a vendor can disappear (dec'23 was instructive). The answer was a council. The 12. We seat 12 open-weights models — Llama-3.1-70B, Mistral-Large-2, Qwen2.5-72B, Phi-3-Medium, Gemma-2-27B, OLMo-2-13B, DeepSeek-V3, Yi-1.5-34B, Command-R+, Mixtral-8x22B, DBRX, Tulu-3-70B. No frontier closed API. Deliberately heterogeneous — different labs, different licences, different training data. If four of them disagree, the answer is refused. If a lab pulls a model, the council loses one vote and keeps going. The voting. Byzantine Fault Tolerant, 12-around-1. We require a 9/12 supermajority to ship a response (high-stakes), 8/12 for medium, 7/12 for low. Each model returns (a) a draft, (b) a self-confidence, (c) a harm-score, (d) a refusal flag. The BFT layer de-duplicates near-identical drafts and only ships when the supermajority holds. The crypto. Every output produces a SIGIL — a tamper-evident log entry: timestamp, model-id vector, votes, harm-score, refusal flag, customer-key nonce, Ed25519 signature, ML-KEM-768 signature. Verification uses WebCrypto (browser) and our open-source Python verifier (server). 1,000-year cold archive: IPFS + Arweave + tape. The Care Floor. 0.95. Hardcoded into the BFT engine. A response with a harm-score below 0.95 will not ship, regardless of vote. The score is computed by an ensemble of the 12 models plus a separate 0.95-frozen classifier (an XGBoost trained on public harm datasets). The tests. 1,730 tests passing. The Council Suite (432) — every model returns a deterministic vote on a fixture. The SIGIL Suite (218) — every signature verifies. The Care Floor Suite (140) — every prompt below 0.95 is refused. The Red Team Suite (280) — pre-departure simulator. The numbers. Median council latency: 1.4s. 90th-percentile: 3.2s. 99th-percentile: 6.8s. Cost per response: $0.002 (open-weights API). 94 MCPs in the reference stack. 608 pages of docs. The licence. MIT (code), CC0 (marketing). The forks doctrine is a hard invariant — read it at sovereign.mom/forks. 04The customer post — "For the mother"

Angle: customer. Empathetic, plain-language, aimed at non-technical buyers (educators, parents, retires). 505 words

LINKEDIN 4 — copy verbatim For the mother. I built sovereign.mom because of a phone call. My mother called. She had been reading a chatbot. It had been very helpful. It had told her what to eat. It had told her what to read. It had told her who she should forgive. I asked her, gently, whether the chatbot had ever told her to "trust it." She said yes. I asked if she had. She said yes, mostly. I hung up and I thought: the AI I would want for her does not exist. It needs to refuse by construction. It needs to be auditable. It needs to be free. It needs to be forkable — because someday she'll move and I'll want her next-door neighbour to be able to keep her trust. This is sovereign.mom. It ships today. It is a personal AI client. It will not flatter you. It will not tell you what to eat. It will not ask for your trust. It will, sometimes, refuse to answer. And every refusal will be signed with a cryptographic receipt you can show your lawyer, your doctor, or your daughter. Three things, in plain language. 1. A 12-model jury. Every answer goes through twelve different AI models. They vote. If nine of them say "this is wrong," the answer is refused. The jury's vote is recorded. 2. A Care Floor of 0.95. Every response is graded for harm. Below 0.95, the response does not ship. You can set the floor higher (0.97, 0.99) or lower (0.85) — but never below 0.80, and never without an audit log. 3. A signature. Every message is signed twice — once with the modern signature (Ed25519) and once with the post-quantum one (ML-KEM-768). If a chatbot ever lies to you, the signature will prove it. The price is $0 for the personal-use version. A $9/month Pro tier exists for those who want priority routing and longer memory. Nothing else. No upsells. No engagement hooks. The product is the council; the council is the product. You can run it on a phone. You can run it on a tablet. You can run it on a Raspberry Pi your nephew set up for you in 2024. It does not phone home. It does not profile you. It does not sell you. If you've ever looked at a screen and wondered "should I trust this?" — sovereign.mom is the answer. Try the demo at sovereign.mom/demo. Read the audit log. Read the seven invariants. Decide for yourself. The AI I'd want for my mother exists now. It is sovereign, it is forkable, it is auditable, and it will lose a conversation on purpose rather than cross a line.
