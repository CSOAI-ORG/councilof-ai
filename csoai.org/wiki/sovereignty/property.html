Sovereign Property

What a sovereign system owns — the four estates.

sovereign.wiki CSOAI Ltd UK 16939677 4 Jul 2026

Definition

A Sovereign Property is one of four estates that any sovereign system must own directly, not delegate to a non-sovereign intermediary. They are: (1) Data, (2) Keys, (3) Compute, (4) Jurisdiction. Ownership means cryptographically enforceable, auditable in real time, and revocable by the principal.

The Four Estates

1. Data

The raw bytes, embeddings, and metadata that constitute the principal's information. Sovereign ownership of data requires: (a) the ability to export the complete dataset in a portable format at any time, (b) the right to be forgotten within 30 days, (c) cryptographic integrity proof (SIGIL hash-chained), (d) access logs that the principal can read. CSOAI's MEOK substrate satisfies all four via the SIGIL chain (see SIGIL Chain).

2. Keys

The cryptographic material that proves identity and signs operations. Sovereign key ownership means: (a) the principal holds the Ed25519 or PQC private key, not the cloud provider, (b) rotation is governed by the principal (90-day cycle by default — see Key Rotation), (c) post-quantum upgrade path (ML-DSA-65, ML-KEM-768 — see ML-DSA-65 and ML-KEM-768), (d) multi-region key escrow under the principal's control only.

3. Compute

The CPU/GPU/TPU cycles that process the data. Sovereign compute ownership means: (a) the runtime is open-source and auditable, (b) no opaque third-party process can read intermediate states, (c) the principal can pause, migrate, or destroy the compute at will, (d) the SIGIL chain records every invocation. CSOAI's DORADO 1-click switch (see DORADO 1-click) implements (c) at the infrastructure layer.

4. Jurisdiction

The legal territory whose law governs the data. Sovereign jurisdiction ownership means: (a) the principal knows which courts can compel disclosure, (b) cross-border transfers are pre-approved by the principal, (c) data residency is enforced at the SIGIL chain layer, (d) the principal can migrate to a different jurisdiction (e.g., UK→EU) without data loss. See the eight sovereign realms: Realms.

Property vs. Promise

A promise is a contractual assurance. A property is a mathematical guarantee. Sovereign Property is the latter — it cannot be silently revoked, cannot be subverted by a foreign court order, and cannot be unilaterally changed by the cloud provider. This is the central argument of Promise vs Property.

Historical Roots

The four-estate model draws on the medieval doctrine of dominium (full ownership) as distinguished from usus (mere use). A tenant has usus over land; a sovereign has dominium. CSOAI reinterprets this in digital terms: most cloud contracts grant usus; the Sovereign Property model asserts dominium.

Modern Implementation

In SOV3: the four estates are exposed as tools sov_dorado_data_export, sov_did_create, sov_dorado_switch, and sov_dorado_prove_sovereignty. The CSOAI Charter's Article 4 codifies the four-estate rule.
