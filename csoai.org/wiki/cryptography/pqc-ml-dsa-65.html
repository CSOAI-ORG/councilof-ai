ML-DSA-65 (Dilithium)

NIST post-quantum signature standard — CSOAI's Ed25519 successor.

sovereign.wiki CSOAI Ltd UK 16939677 4 Jul 2026

Definition

ML-DSA-65 is the NIST-standardised post-quantum signature algorithm formerly known as Dilithium-3 (CRYSTALS-Dilithium). It was standardised as FIPS 204 in August 2024 and is the quantum-safe successor to Ed25519 in CSOAI's stack.

Why Post-Quantum?

Ed25519 (see Ed25519) is vulnerable to Shor's algorithm on a sufficiently large quantum computer. Projected CRQC window: 2030–2035. CSOAI rotates keys to ML-DSA-65 to ensure that signatures emitted today remain unforgeable in 2035.

Properties

  • Public key: 1,952 bytes
  • Signature: 3,293 bytes
  • Security: NIST Level 3 (~128-bit post-quantum security)
  • Performance: ~80µs sign, ~50µs verify on x86-64
  • Based on: Module-LWE (Learning With Errors) over module lattices

Why ML-DSA-65 Specifically?

NIST standardises three parameter sets: ML-DSA-44 (Level 2), ML-DSA-65 (Level 3), ML-DSA-87 (Level 5). CSOAI picked ML-DSA-65 because Level 3 matches Ed25519's classical security level (~128-bit), giving a clean drop-in replacement. ML-DSA-87 is overkill for our use case; ML-DSA-44 is too weak.

Usage in CSOAI

ML-DSA-65 is deployed in: (1) the SIGIL chain for new customer deployments (since Q4 2025), (2) the Article 50 passport (Pro tier) since EU AI Act requirements, (3) hybrid mode alongside Ed25519 for defence-in-depth.

Mathematical Sketch

ML-DSA-65 signs by: (1) sampling a random vector y from a small distribution, (2) computing the challenge c = H(μ || Ay_high) where Ay_high is the high bits of Ay, (3) computing z = y + c·s where s is the secret key, (4) rejecting if z or the low bits of Ay − c·s reveal too much. Verification recomputes c and checks Ay − c·s = Ay_high · 2^d + Ay_low against the public key.

Historical Context

CRYSTALS-Dilithium was submitted to NIST's PQC standardisation process in 2017. It was selected as a winner in 2022 and standardised as FIPS 204 in August 2024. CSOAI deployed it in production within 4 months of standardisation — one of the earliest production deployments globally.

Migration Path

Existing Ed25519 keys are rotated to ML-DSA-65 via the standard rotation cycle (see Key Rotation). Hybrid mode is supported: each SIGIL event carries both Ed25519 and ML-DSA-65 signatures, enabling verification by either. After 2027, hybrid mode will be phased out in favour of ML-DSA-65-only.
