Quantum Ready

Post-quantum cryptography + quantum-accelerated AI = sovereign by 2030 and beyond.

sovereign.wiki CSOAI Ltd UK 16939677 4 Jul 2026

Definition

Quantum Ready is CSOAI's posture toward quantum computing: the architecture is built today to withstand tomorrow's cryptographically-relevant quantum computer (CRQC). This has two dimensions: (1) post-quantum cryptography (PQC) for classical protection, and (2) quantum-accelerated AI for new capabilities.

Post-Quantum Cryptography

CSOAI deploys NIST-approved PQC algorithms: ML-DSA-65 (Dilithium, signatures) and ML-KEM-768 (Kyber, key encapsulation). See ML-DSA-65 and ML-KEM-768. The Ed25519 keys issued today can be rotated to PQC keys without changing the SIGIL chain format (the chain only stores signatures, not algorithm identities).

Quantum-Accelerated AI

CSOAI's sovereign stack includes three quantum-accelerated primitives: (1) QAOA for care-weight optimisation, (2) VQE for memory importance scoring, and (3) Grover for federated search. These are exposed via the sov_run_quantum_batch tool and run on the Sovereign Quantum backend (IBM Quantum + IonQ integration).

Harvest Now, Decrypt Later

The most pressing quantum threat is "harvest now, decrypt later" (HNDL): adversaries capture encrypted traffic today, store it, and decrypt it when a CRQC is available. CSOAI addresses this by: (1) defaulting to PQC for all new connections, (2) re-encrypting stored SIGIL chains in PQC at every rotation, and (3) the public SIGIL chain already uses PQC signatures.

Historical Context

Shor's algorithm (1994) showed that a sufficiently large quantum computer could break RSA and elliptic curve cryptography. NIST began the PQC standardisation process in 2016; ML-DSA-65 and ML-KEM-768 were standardised in August 2024. CSOAI adopted them in production by Q4 2024, ahead of the EU and UK mandates.
