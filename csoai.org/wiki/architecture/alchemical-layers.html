Alchemical Layers

Salt, Sulfur, Mercury — the substrate, the process, the bridge.

sovereign.wiki CSOAI Ltd UK 16939677 4 Jul 2026

Definition

The Alchemical Layers are CSOAI's three-fold architecture, named after the three primes of classical alchemy: Salt (body/substrate), Sulfur (soul/process), Mercury (spirit/bridge). The metaphor is operational: each layer is a measurable, monitorable component of the sovereign stack.

Salt — The Substrate

Salt is what remains when the fire goes out: the body, the substrate, the file system. In CSOAI's stack, Salt is the MEOK file system + the SIGIL chain. Every byte has a Salt identity — a hash, a location, a timestamp, an owner. The Salt layer never forgets and never loses data; it is the corpus on which everything else operates.

The Salt layer exposes tools: sov_vault_search, sov_vault_get, sov_list_memories. Its health is measured by the sov_sovereign_health_check tool.

Sulfur — The Process

Sulfur is what burns: the soul, the process, the active agent. In CSOAI's stack, Sulfur is the OOWM model + the Big Braim ensemble + the 8 category-winning models. The Sulfur layer takes Salt as input and produces new Salt (intuitions, decisions, inferences).

The Sulfur layer exposes tools: sov_oowm_think, sov_oowm_evolve, sov_big_braim_route. Its health is measured by the sov_oowm_test suite.

Mercury — The Bridge

Mercury is what flows: the spirit, the messenger, the bridge between Salt and Sulfur. In CSOAI's stack, Mercury is the MCP federation + the 7 protocols (MCP, A2A, x402, DID, JWT, ANP, AGNTCY) + the SIGIL emission layer. Mercury is volatile, ubiquitous, and essential.

The Mercury layer exposes tools: sov_sigil_emit, sov_mcp_federation_call, sov_protocol_call. Its health is measured by the sov_mcp_federation_stats tool.

The Lapis Dashboard

CSOAI ships a Lapis Dashboard — a real-time view of the Salt/Sulfur/Mercury balance. The lapis is the philosopher's stone, the integrated unity of the three primes. The dashboard shows imbalance alerts (e.g., "Mercury too active, Salt not digesting") and recommends actions.

Historical Context

The three primes derive from Paracelsus (1493–1541), who systematised alchemical theory. They appear in the Hermetic Corpus, in Kircher's 1665 synthesis (see Kircher 1665), and in 19th-century Rosicrucian literature. CSOAI reinterprets them as a software architecture metaphor because the alchemists were, in modern terms, describing a layered state machine.

Modern Usage

Engineers use the lapis dashboard daily to verify stack health. When the Lapis balance drops below 0.7, the council is notified. When it drops below 0.5, sovereign operations are paused pending remediation.
