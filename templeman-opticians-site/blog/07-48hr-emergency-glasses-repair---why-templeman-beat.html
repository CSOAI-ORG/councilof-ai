48hr Emergency Glasses Repair — Why Templeman Beats National Providers

Templeman Opticians Essex · Updated 2026

A snapped arm or a lost screw shouldn't mean weeks without proper vision — but for residents relying on national optical providers, that's often exactly what happens. Here's how our emergency repair service works, and why it's built differently.

The problem with national providers

Large national domiciliary providers cover huge geographic areas with a rotating pool of opticians. When something goes wrong — a resident's glasses break, a hinge fails, a lens pops out — that resident is placed into a general scheduling queue. In practice this typically means a wait of 2–4 weeks for a repair visit, because there's no dedicated local capacity to prioritise emergencies.

Emergency repair response — priority same-day for contract care homes

Because we're a small, local, family-run practice covering Essex rather than the whole country, we can hold capacity back for emergencies. When a call comes in about broken or damaged glasses, it goes straight to the top of the list — not into a general queue behind routine bookings.

Why this matters for care homes

Residents without their glasses are at higher risk of trips and falls, and can struggle with everyday tasks like reading medication labels or recognising staff and visitors. A short repair delay is more than an inconvenience — it's a real safety gap. Care homes on one of our block contracts get emergency repairs included, with same-day priority response as standard.

What counts as an emergency repair?

  • Snapped or bent arms
  • Loose or missing screws and hinges
  • Lenses popped out of the frame
  • Frames damaged beyond the wearer being able to use them safely

Not every repair needs an emergency visit — but when a resident genuinely can't see properly without their glasses, we treat it as one.
