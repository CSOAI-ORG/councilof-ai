5 Ways Better Eyesight Prevents Falls in Care Homes

Templeman Opticians Essex · Updated 2026

Falls are one of the biggest safety concerns in care settings, and poor vision is a well-recognised contributing factor. Regular eye care is a simple, practical part of any falls-prevention approach. Here's how it helps.

1. Spotting hazards clearly

Steps, changes in flooring, trailing cables, and furniture edges are much easier to misjudge with blurred or outdated vision. An up-to-date prescription makes everyday hazards easier to see and avoid in the first place.

2. Better depth perception

Judging the height of a step or the distance to a chair relies on depth perception, which is affected by uncorrected vision problems. Clear sight in both eyes helps residents judge these distances accurately.

3. Catching conditions that affect balance

Some eye conditions, including cataracts and glaucoma, can affect more than sharpness of vision — they can also affect peripheral awareness and adjustment to changes in light, both of which play a role in stability while moving around. Regular tests catch these conditions early.

4. Glasses that actually fit

Glasses that have slipped out of adjustment, or are the wrong prescription, can be worse than not wearing them at all — causing double vision or distorted depth judgement. Part of our home visit service includes returning to properly fit and adjust glasses, not just prescribing them.

5. Confidence to move independently

Beyond the physical safety factors, residents who can see clearly are generally more confident moving around independently, which supports both their wellbeing and their mobility — rather than becoming hesitant or overly reliant on staff for fear of missing a step.

Making it easy to stay on top of
