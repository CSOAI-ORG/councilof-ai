Headline: CSOAI Ltd launches world's first independent AI governance measurement body — 22 axes, signed, Bitcoin-anchored

Dateline: LONDON, UK — September 13, 2026

CSOAI Ltd (UK 16939677), an independent AI-governance measurement body, today announced the public release of its 22-axis signed measurement instrument. The instrument has already produced 545+ measurement cards covering frontier AI models, all of which are Ed25519-signed and Bitcoin-anchored via OpenTimestamps.

Unlike existing AI safety frameworks — including Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy, Google DeepMind's Frontier Safety Framework, OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, the UK AI Safety Institute, and NIST's AI RMF — CSOAI is structurally independent of any AI builder. Every measurement is signed, every receipt is verifiable, and the merkle roots anchoring each batch are publicly confirmable on the Bitcoin blockchain.

The company has also published the architectural blueprint for a three-layer governance stack:
- CSOAI (councilof.ai) — the independent measurement layer
- AGISafe (agisafe.ai) — the decision layer (allow/restrict/require-human/stop)
- ASISecurity (asisecurity.ai) — the enforcement layer (sandboxing, permissions, revocation)
- meope.ai — the symbiosis/intelligence layer (model selection and routing)

"The biggest shift in the history of wealth transfer is happening," said Nicholas Templeman, CEO of CSOAI Ltd. "CLARITY Act. GENIUS Act. EU AI Act. MiCA. DORA. Every one of these requires measurement. We're not asking permission — we already have signed, anchored, verifiable measurement infrastructure. We're the first ones there."

The company is submitting its measurement framework as an evaluation extension to the UK AI Security Institute's Inspect framework, is a C2PA Contributor Member at the Linux Foundation, and is applying for the BSI ART/1 standards committee seat (the UK mirror of ISO/IEC SC 42). The framework is already integrated with 22 of the EU AI Act's risk categories and has crosswalks to DORA, NIS2, CRA, ISO 42001, GDPR, MiCA, and SOC2.
