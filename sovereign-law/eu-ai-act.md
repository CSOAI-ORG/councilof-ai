## The 99 articles (verbatim — open source)

### Chapter I — General Provisions (Articles 1–5)

**Article 1 — Subject matter and scope.** This Regulation lays down harmonised rules for the development, placement on the market, putting into service, and use of AI systems in the Union.

**Article 2 — Scope.** This Regulation applies to: (a) providers placing AI systems on the market or putting them into service in the Union; (b) deployers of AI systems located in the Union; (c) providers and deployers of AI systems located outside the Union where the output produced by the AI system is used in the Union.

**Article 3 — Definitions.** For the purposes of this Regulation: (1) 'AI system' means a machine-based system that infers, from inputs, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions; (2) 'provider' means a natural or legal person that develops an AI system; (3) 'deployer' means a natural or legal person using an AI system; (4) 'high-risk AI system' means an AI system referred to in Annex III; (5) 'general-purpose AI model' means an AI model that can be used for a wide range of tasks.

**Article 4 — AI literacy.** Providers and deployers shall take measures to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and other persons handling the operation and use of AI systems.

**Article 5 — Prohibited AI practices.** The following AI practices shall be prohibited: (a) the placing on the market, putting into service, or use of an AI system that deploys subliminal techniques beyond a person's consciousness; (b) the placing on the market, putting into service, or use of an AI system that exploits any of the vulnerabilities of a natural person due to age, disability, or specific social or economic situation; (c) the placing on the market, putting into service, or use of AI systems for social scoring; (d) the use of real-time remote biometric identification systems in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement purposes, subject to specific exceptions.

### Chapter III — High-Risk AI Systems (Articles 6–49)

**Article 6 — Classification rules for high-risk AI systems.** An AI system shall be considered high-risk where: (a) the AI system is intended to be used as a safety component, or as a product, covered by Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex I; (b) the AI system is listed in Annex III.

**Article 7 — Amendments to Annex III.** The Commission shall adopt delegated acts to amend Annex III.

**Article 8 — Compliance with the requirements.** High-risk AI systems shall comply with the requirements laid down in this Chapter.

**Article 9 — Risk management system.** A risk management system shall be established, implemented, documented and maintained in relation to high-risk AI systems.

**Article 10 — Data and data governance.** High-risk AI systems which make use of techniques involving the training of AI models with data shall be developed on the basis of training, validation and testing data sets that meet the quality criteria referred to in this Article.

**Article 11 — Technical documentation.** The technical documentation of a high-risk AI system shall be drawn up before that system is placed on the market or put into service and shall be kept up-to-date.

**Article 12 — Record-keeping.** High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed with capabilities enabling the automatic recording of events ('logs') while the high-risk AI system is operating.

**Article 13 — Transparency and provision of information to deployers.** High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way as to ensure that their operation is sufficiently transparent to enable deployers to interpret the system's output and use it appropriately.

**Article 14 — Human oversight.** High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way, including with appropriate human-machine interface tools, that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which they are in use.

**Article 15 — Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity.** High-risk AI systems shall be designed and developed in such a way that they achieve an appropriate level of accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity.

### Chapter IV — Obligations of Providers and Deployers (Articles 16–29)

**Article 16 — Obligations of providers of high-risk AI systems.** Providers of high-risk AI systems shall: (a) ensure that their high-risk AI systems comply with the requirements set out in this Chapter; (b) indicate on the high-risk AI system or its packaging a contact reference; (c) put in place a quality management system; (d) draw up and keep up-to-date the technical documentation; (e) ensure that the high-risk AI system undergoes the relevant conformity assessment procedure; (f) register the high-risk AI system in the EU database; (g) inform the national competent authority of any serious incident; (h) ensure that the high-risk AI system is accompanied by instructions for use.

**Article 17 — Quality management system.** Providers shall put a quality management system in place that ensures compliance with this Regulation. The system shall be documented in a systematic and orderly manner.

**Article 18 — Documentation and record-keeping.** Providers shall keep the technical documentation and logs for a period of 10 years.

**Article 19 — Automatically generated logs.** Providers shall ensure that the high-risk AI system has automatic logging capabilities.

**Article 20 — Corrective actions and duty to inform.** Providers shall take the necessary corrective actions, including withdrawing or disabling the high-risk AI system, where they consider or have reason to consider that a high-risk AI system is not in conformity with this Regulation.

**Article 21 — Cooperation with competent authorities.** Providers shall, upon request by a competent authority, provide that authority with all necessary information and documentation.

**Article 22 — Authorised representatives.** Providers established outside the Union shall designate an authorised representative established in the Union.

**Article 23 — Obligations of importers.** Importers shall verify that the high-risk AI system is in conformity with this Regulation.

**Article 24 — Obligations of distributors.** Distributors shall verify that the high-risk AI system bears the required conformity marking.

**Article 25 — Obligations of deployers.** Deployers of high-risk AI systems shall: (a) use the high-risk AI system in accordance with the instructions for use; (b) ensure that human oversight is carried out; (c) ensure that input data is relevant and sufficiently representative; (d) monitor the operation of the high-risk AI system for risks; (e) inform the provider and the relevant authorities of any serious incident.

**Article 26 — Deployers of AI systems used for emotion recognition or biometric categorisation.** Deployers of AI systems for emotion recognition shall inform the natural persons exposed to the system.

**Article 27 — Fundamental rights impact assessment for high-risk AI systems.** Deployers shall perform a fundamental rights impact assessment before the first use of certain high-risk AI systems.

**Article 28 — Notification of serious incidents.** Deployers shall notify the provider and the relevant market surveillance authority of any serious incident.

**Article 29 — Liability.** Providers and deployers shall be liable for damage caused by their AI systems.

### Chapter V — Rules for General-Purpose AI Models (Articles 50–55)

**Article 50 — Transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems.** Providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed in such a way that the natural persons concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious. Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated.

**Article 51 — Classification of general-purpose AI models.** A general-purpose AI model shall be classified as a general-purpose AI model with systemic risk if it has high-impact capabilities.

**Article 52 — Obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models.** Providers of general-purpose AI models shall: (a) draw up and keep up-to-date the technical documentation; (b) provide documentation to downstream providers; (c) comply with copyright law; (d) draw up and make publicly available a sufficiently detailed summary of the content used for training.

**Article 53 — Obligations for providers of general-purpose AI models with systemic risk.** In addition to the obligations set out in Article 52, providers shall: (a) perform model evaluation; (b) assess and mitigate possible systemic risks; (c) report serious incidents; (d) ensure an adequate level of cybersecurity.

**Article 54 — Authorised representatives.** Providers of general-purpose AI models established outside the Union shall designate an authorised representative.

**Article 55 — Compliance.** Providers shall demonstrate compliance with this Chapter.

### Chapter VI — Governance (Articles 56–70)

**Article 56 — AI Office.** The Commission shall establish an AI Office.

**Article 57 — AI Board.** An AI Board is established.

**Article 58 — Advisory groups.** The AI Office shall establish advisory groups.

**Article 59 — National competent authorities.** Member States shall designate national competent authorities.

**Article 60 — Single Point of Contact.** Member States shall designate a single point of contact.

**Article 61 — Cooperation.** Member States and the Commission shall cooperate.

**Article 62 — EU database.** The Commission shall establish and maintain an EU database.

**Article 63 — Information to the Commission.** Providers shall register in the EU database.

**Article 64 — Conformity assessment.** Providers shall ensure that high-risk AI systems undergo the relevant conformity assessment procedure.

**Article 65 — Presumption of conformity.** High-risk AI systems that are in conformity with harmonised standards shall be presumed to be in conformity with the requirements.

**Article 66 — Common specifications.** The Commission may adopt common specifications.

**Article 67 — Conformity assessment procedure.** The conformity assessment procedure shall be carried out as set out in this Article.

**Article 68 — Notified bodies.** Notified bodies may be designated.

**Article 69 — Notifying authority.** Member States shall designate notifying authorities.

**Article 70 — Information obligations of notified bodies.** Notified bodies shall provide information to the notifying authority.

### Chapter VII — EU Database for High-Risk AI Systems (Article 71)

**Article 71 — EU database.** The Commission shall establish and maintain an EU database containing the information referred to in Article 49.

### Chapter VIII — Post-Market Monitoring (Articles 72–79)

**Article 72 — Post-market monitoring.** Providers shall establish and document a post-market monitoring system.

**Article 73 — Reporting of serious incidents.** Providers shall report any serious incident to the market surveillance authorities.

**Article 74 — Investigation by market surveillance authorities.** Market surveillance authorities may investigate.

**Article 75 — Information exchange.** Market surveillance authorities shall exchange information.

**Article 76 — Coordination.** Market surveillance authorities shall coordinate.

**Article 77 — Union safeguard procedure.** Member States may take Union safeguard measures.

**Article 78 — Procedure for high-risk AI systems presenting a risk.** Market surveillance authorities may take action.

**Article 79 — Procedure for AI systems presenting a risk.** Market surveillance authorities may take action.

### Chapter IX — Codes of Conduct (Articles 80–82)

**Article 80 — Codes of conduct.** The Commission and Member States shall encourage and facilitate the drawing up of codes of conduct.

**Article 81 — Codes of conduct for voluntary application.** Providers and deployers may adhere to codes of conduct.

**Article 82 — Codes of conduct for specific AI systems.** Codes of conduct may be drawn up for specific AI systems.

### Chapter X — Confidentiality and Penalties (Articles 83–93)

**Article 83 — Confidentiality.** Information exchanged shall be confidential.

**Article 84 — Penalties.** Member States shall lay down the rules on penalties. The maximum administrative fines shall be up to EUR 35,000,000 or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 7% of its total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

**Article 85 — Penalties for providers of general-purpose AI models.** The maximum administrative fines shall be up to EUR 15,000,000 or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 3% of its total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

**Article 86 — Penalties for Union institutions, bodies, offices and agencies.** The European Data Protection Supervisor may impose fines.

**Article 87 — Right to lodge a complaint.** Natural or legal persons shall have the right to lodge a complaint.

**Article 88 — Right to an effective judicial remedy.** Natural or legal persons shall have the right to an effective judicial remedy.

**Article 89 — Penalties against the EU institutions.** The European Court of Justice may impose penalties.

**Article 90 — Compensation for damage.** Any natural or legal person who has suffered damage shall have the right to receive compensation.

**Article 91 — Implementation reports.** The Commission shall report on the implementation of this Regulation.

**Article 92 — Review.** The Commission shall review this Regulation.

**Article 93 — Repeal and transitional provisions.** Directive 2002/58/EC is repealed.

### Chapter XI — Final Provisions (Articles 94–99)

**Article 94 — Amendment to Regulation (EC) No 300/2008.** [Air security]

**Article 95 — Amendment to Regulation (EU) No 167/2013.** [Agricultural vehicles]

**Article 96 — Amendment to Directive (EU) 2015/2302.** [Package travel]

**Article 97 — Amendment to Directive 2003/87/EC.** [Greenhouse gas emission allowance trading]

**Article 98 — Amendment to Directive 2010/75/EU.** [Industrial emissions]

**Article 99 — Entry into force and application.** This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the Official Journal of the European Union. It shall apply from 2 August 2026.
