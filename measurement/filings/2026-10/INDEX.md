## What each one is actually, in CSOAI terms

**O5 — the one worth doing first.** The comment channel is an open rolling email with no
registration, no fee and no deadline, and the announced next milestone (a draft project description)
means a comment sent now can inform the document rather than react to it. The draft argues one
narrow point, grounded in our own published failure: the concept paper's standards list is entirely
made of expression standards and contains nothing that observes conformance from outside the
deployment, and a criterion that cannot fail is not a criterion. The evidence is our own withdrawal
of 44 signed results on 14 September 2026, every one of which carried an accuracy of exactly 1
because the grading configuration permitted only one answer.

**O2 — a partner route, not a submission route.** There is no way to submit a measurement into the
Cyber Index; Artificial Analysis conducts the evaluations itself. The join channel is an email and
the ask is expert input on the Index's design, with the option of contributing datasets and
external research. That is a genuine fit for CSOAI — but it is a *contribution*, not a listing, and
it must not be reported as either a membership or a measurement being included. The separately
available opportunity is to publish an *independent* measurement of the Index and its harness,
which is what CSOAI does.

**O3 — ineligible, and the honest move is to measure rather than sell.** CSOAI Ltd is UK-registered
and the closed beta is restricted to US-based sellers and buyers, so the beta application is not
open to us and should not be attempted. The interesting consequence is that CSOAI is already on the
same rail: the Monetization Gateway uses x402 for payment authorisation in-request, and CSOAI's own
MCP server already exposes five metered x402 tools and a published x402 trust snapshot. Measuring the
402-challenge behaviour of deployed Monetization Gateway endpoints is something CSOAI can do from
outside, without a beta seat and without eligibility.

**O1 — no channel, by design, and that is the finding.** The accord's third layer calls for an
independent external auditor or evaluator. It does not define independence, register auditors, set
a deadline, require findings to be published, or attach a consequence. EO 14434 orders the executive
branch to use "Super Intelligence" and directs the APST to propose a federal definition of it; no
definition has been issued, and the accord's operative commitments are framed around "frontier
models" rather than the term in its title. There is nothing to submit to, and the absence of
criteria is the measurable gap.

**O4 — one live, dated, documented channel, plus a set of standing forums.** PRA CP14/26 is open
now and closes 22 January 2027, and it reaches AI explicitly: the PRA records that "operational
risks, including risks arising from artificial intelligence and cyber incidents, have increased the
importance of effective operational oversight." Bailey's own framing is that "rigorous model
testing, conducted before and after deployment… should also form an important part of appropriate
standards for deploying these models", and that the UK's AI Security Institute "should help form part
of a set of standards". Note that the FPC's September 2026 record cites the frontier-AI
test-environment incidents — including the Meta Muse Spark 1.1 evaluation breach — as an input into
its own agenda, which makes this the one opening where CSOAI's incident record and the
standard-setting route meet.
