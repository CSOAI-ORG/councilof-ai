### 0.1 Certifications Required

#### Cyber Essentials (MANDATORY)
The baseline cybersecurity certification required for ALL MOD contracts. You cannot bid for MOD work without it.

| Level | Cost | Timeline | Validity |
|-------|------|----------|----------|
| **Cyber Essentials (Basic)** | £320-£600 + VAT (micro to large) | 1-2 weeks | 12 months |
| **Cyber Essentials Plus** | £1,500-£4,250 + VAT depending on size | 6-8 weeks | 12 months |

**Process:**
1. Register via IASME (the NCSC delivery partner): [iasme.co.uk](https://iasme.co.uk)
2. Complete self-assessment questionnaire (SAQ)
3. For Plus: External auditor conducts technical audit (vulnerability scans, malware tests, configuration review)
4. Certificate issued, valid 12 months

**Pro Tips:**
- Download the question set in advance and prepare answers before applying
- Basic Cyber Essentials takes ~1 hour to complete if prepared
- Most assessors return results within 3 days
- You have 2 working days to fix issues if you fail first attempt
- FREE cyber liability insurance included for UK orgs with turnover under £20m

**Action:** Get Cyber Essentials Basic immediately. Upgrade to Plus when you have your first serious contract opportunity.

---

#### JOSCAR (Joint Supply Chain Accreditation Register)
Required if you want to work with major primes (BAE Systems, QinetiQ, Leonardo, Raytheon UK, etc.). Not strictly required for direct MOD work, but primes will ask for it.

| Stage | Cost | Timeline |
|-------|------|----------|
| **Stage 1** (basic company details) | FREE | Under 60 minutes |
| **Stage 2** (full compliance questionnaire) | FREE for SMEs / £275-£725 + VAT for larger orgs | 1-2 weeks |

**Process:**
1. Register at [joscar.com](https://joscar.com) (operated by Hellios)
2. Complete Stage 1 (company basics, under 60 mins)
3. System auto-determines if Stage 2 needed (based on risk of what you supply)
4. Stage 2: Detailed compliance questionnaire with supporting evidence
5. Participating primes review and approve

**Pro Tips:**
- SMEs get free Stage 2; ADS & Make UK members get reduced rate (£275)
- You don't need pricing or commercial info — purely compliance/accreditation
- More primes are joining — this is becoming the industry standard

**Action:** Register for JOSCAR Stage 1 immediately. Complete Stage 2 if you plan to subcontract through primes.

---

#### ISO 27001 (Information Security Management)

| Item | Cost | Timeline |

**Action:** Target for Month 6-12. Not needed for initial DASA/SBRI applications.

The official MOD e-sourcing platform. ALL MOD contract opportunities over £10,000 are advertised here.

- **Cost:** FREE
- **Timeline:** 1-2 hours to register
- **URL:** [contracts.mod.uk](https://contracts.mod.uk)

**Process:**

**Pro Tips:**

- You can view opportunities WITHOUT an account (but need one to bid)
- Also register on Contracts Finder and Find a Tender
- The portal also publishes subcontracting opportunities

#### R-Cloud (DSTL Research Cloud) — HIGHLY RECOMMENDED
Dstl's marketplace for defence science and technology research. Over £164M in contracts awarded since 2018. 70%+ of suppliers are SMEs. IP stays with you.

- **Cost:** FREE to join
- **URL:** [rcloud.dstl.gov.uk](https://rcloud.dstl.gov.uk)

**What you get:**
- Access to research contracts (TRL 1-6)
- Direct contracting with MOD
- You retain IP (MOD gets full user rights)

- Targeted invitations based on your capabilities

**Process:**
1. Create account at [rcloud.dstl.gov.uk](https://rcloud.dstl.gov.uk)

3. Complete company details and financial data

**Pro Tips:**

- If you're a startup with limited financials, explain in supporting info
- You don't need a separate narrative for each research area
- Only matched opportunities are pushed to you — no noise

### 0.3 Security Clearances

> **CRITICAL RULE:** You CANNOT apply for security clearances speculatively. You must be sponsored by a government department or prime contractor for a specific contract.

However, you MUST understand the requirements and plan for them.

| Clearance Level | Access | Timeline | Cost | Validity |
|----------------|--------|----------|------|----------|
| **BPSS** (Baseline Personnel Security Standard) | OFFICIAL data | 5-10 working days | £80-£150/person | Ongoing |
| **SC** (Security Check) | SECRET data | 6-12 weeks | £500-£1,000/person | 7-10 years |
| **DV** (Developed Vetting) | TOP SECRET/codeword | 6-9 months | £2,000-£5,000/person | 7 years |

#### BPSS (Get This First)
- NOT a formal security clearance — it's pre-employment screening
- Required for ALL government work
- You can do this yourself (no sponsorship needed)
- Checks: identity, right to work, 3-year employment history, unspent convictions

#### SC (Most Common)
- Required for most MOD software/AI/cyber contracts
- Builds on BPSS + detailed security questionnaire
- UKSV checks: departmental records, criminal records (spent & unspent), credit/financial, MI5 records
- Requires sponsorship from government department or prime contractor
- **For AI startups:** Most AI contracts will require at least SC for core team members

#### DV (For Classified/Sensitive Work)
- Required for TOP SECRET codeword material
- Deep dive into personal life: finances, lifestyle, relationships
- Interview with trained Investigating Officer + referee interviews
- Plan 6-9 months minimum

#### Facility Security Clearance (FSC)
- Required if you process SECRET data on your own premises/IT systems
- Separate from individual clearances
- Your office/network must meet security standards

#### The "No Clearance, No Work" Trap
This is the #1 barrier for startups. Here's how to break it:

1. **Start with unclassified work** — DASA, SBRI, and many R-Cloud tasks don't require clearance
2. **Get sponsorship through your first contract** — MOD will sponsor clearances for contract staff
3. **The Defence Tech Scaler** (new MOD initiative) provides clearance sponsorship for new suppliers
4. **Hire already-cleared staff** — advertise for SC-cleared developers/data scientists
5. **Use MOD facilities** — some work can be done on MOD sites without FSC
6. **Work as subcontractor** — primes can sponsor your clearances

**Pro Tip:** In your bids, include a mobilisation plan that shows you understand the clearance timeline. Don't claim to have clearances you don't have — but demonstrate you know how to get them.

### 0.4 Company Structure

**Recommended: Limited Company (Ltd)**

Defence buyers expect a Ltd structure. It provides:
- Limited liability protection
- Credibility with MOD buyers
- Ability to hold FSC and clearances
- Standard for all defence contracting

**Alternative: CIC (Community Interest Company)**
- Only if your mission aligns strongly with public benefit
- Some defence innovation funds prioritise CICs
- Generally not recommended for scaling to £50M

**Required:**
- UK registered company
- Company registration number
- DUNS number
- VAT registration (if turnover > £85,000)

### 0.5 Insurance Requirements

| Insurance Type | Minimum Cover | Typical Cost | When Required |
|---------------|---------------|--------------|---------------|
| **Professional Indemnity (PI)** | £1M-£2M | £500-£2,000/year | Almost all MOD contracts |
| **Public Liability** | £5M-£10M | £300-£1,000/year | If working on MOD sites |
| **Employer's Liability** | £10M (legal minimum) | £200-£800/year | If you have employees |
| **Cyber Insurance** | £1M-£5M | £500-£3,000/year | Data-handling contracts |
| **Directors' & Officers'** | £1M-£2M | £500-£2,000/year | Recommended |

**Key Points:**
- PI is the big one — MOD will specify minimum cover in contract
- Standard MOD requirement: £1M-£2M PI, £5M+ Public Liability
- Some contracts require £5M+ PI for high-risk work
- Get specialist defence contractor insurance (MFL Insurance Group, Kingsbridge, Markel offer specialist policies)
- Free cyber insurance (up to £25,000) comes with Cyber Essentials via IASME

### 1.3 How to Get Into DASA

#### The DASA Open Call (Primary Route)

DASA runs 4 competition cycles per year. The Open Call is ALWAYS open.

**Current Cycle Schedule (CY2025):**
| Cycle | Closes | Decisions | Feedback |
|-------|--------|-----------|----------|
| Cycle 1 | 29 April 2025 | 18 July 2025 | 25 July 2025 |
| Cycle 2 | 1 July 2025 | 10 October 2025 | 17 October 2025 |
| Cycle 3 | 23 September 2025 | 12 December 2025 | 19 December 2025 |
| Cycle 4 | 16 December 2025 | 27 March 2026 | 3 April 2026 |

**Funding:** £100,000 — £350,000 per project (no equity taken)

**Requirements:**
- TRL 6-7 at project conclusion (demonstrable prototype in relevant environment)
- Maximum 24 months duration
- Must include exploitation plan beyond the project
- Written final report required
- Must demonstrate value for money

**The DASA "Pre-Sift" Criteria (MUST PASS):**
1. Within scope of competition
2. Explains how it meets DASA criteria (Desirability, Feasibility, Viability)
3. Explains exploitation beyond the project plan
4. Contains financial plan, project plan, and resourcing plan
5. Maximum value £350,000
6. Duration ≤ 24 months
7. Will deliver TRL 6-7

**Application Process:**
1. Register on [DASA Submission Service](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/defence-and-security-accelerator-dasa-accessing-funding) (free)
2. Select relevant competition
3. Complete online form (Desirability, Feasibility, Viability sections)
4. Submit by midday deadline
5. Wait ~10-12 weeks for decision

**Innovation Focus Areas (IFAs):**
DASA also runs themed competitions. Check their website for current IFAs. Recent AI-related IFAs include:
- AI for defence decision-making
- AI for autonomous systems
- AI for cyber defence
- AI for ISR processing

### 1.5 What Demo to Show

**For AI companies — the demo that MOD wants to see:**

1. **Real data, real problem** — Not a generic AI demo. Show YOUR AI working on defence-relevant data or scenarios.

2. **End-user focused** — Show how a warfighter/analyst would USE it. Make it tangible.

3. **Explainability** — MOD cares about responsible AI. Show HOW your AI makes decisions, not just THAT it works.

4. **Edge/offline capability** — Defence AI often needs to work in contested environments without cloud connectivity. Demonstrate edge deployment.

5. **Integration potential** — Show how your AI integrates with existing MOD systems (C2 platforms, data feeds, etc.)

**Demo checklist:**
- [ ] Runs on a laptop (no cloud dependency for demo)
- [ ] Uses realistic defence scenario data (unclassified)
- [ ] Shows clear before/after (problem → AI solution → outcome)
- [ ] Includes explainability/interpretability features
- [ ] Shows real-time processing capability
- [ ] Can be shown in 10 minutes or less
- [ ] Has a "wow factor" that senior officers will remember

### 1.6 The Pitch Deck Structure

**Slide 1: Problem** (Defence context — specific capability gap)
**Slide 2: Your Solution** (What your AI does — one sentence)
**Slide 3: Demo/Proof** (Screenshot, video, or live demo)
**Slide 4: Defence Use Cases** (3 specific scenarios with named units/commands if possible)
**Slide 5: TRL & Maturity** (Where you are now, what you need to reach)
**Slide 6: Team** (Why YOU can deliver this — credentials, clearance status)
**Slide 7: Path to Contract** (How to buy from you — frameworks, DASA, direct)
**Slide 8: Ask** (What you want — meeting, trial, DASA application, intro)

**Rules:**
- Maximum 10 slides
- No jargon. If a non-technical brigadier can't understand it, rewrite it.
- Lead with the OPERATIONAL IMPACT, not the technology
- Show you understand the defence context

### 1.7 What MOD Buyers Want to Hear

✅ **DO SAY:**
- "This solves [specific capability gap] for [specific command/unit]"
- "We've tested this with real [data/scenarios]"
- "We understand the defence procurement process"
- "We're Cyber Essentials certified and can work within MOD security frameworks"
- "Here's how we've engaged with defence stakeholders already"
- "This is dual-use — works in civilian and defence contexts"
- "We can deliver a deployable prototype in [X months]"
- "Here's our exploitation plan beyond the initial trial"
- "We're familiar with JSP 936 [Dependable AI in Defence]"
- "We can integrate with your existing C2/[relevant system]"

❌ **DON'T SAY:**
- "Our AI will revolutionise warfare" (too grandiose)
- "We just need access to classified data to make this work" (you won't get it)
- "The MOD procurement process is too slow" (even if true, don't say it)
- "We're the only company that can do this" (probably not true, sounds arrogant)
- "We don't understand the defence market but our tech is amazing" (shows naivety)
- "Defence is just one vertical for us" (suggests you're not committed)
- "We need £50M and 3 years to build this" (too much, too long for first contact)
- "Our AI is too complex to explain" (red flag for responsible AI)

### 2.1 Fastest Paths to First Revenue

#### PATH A: DASA Open Call (Fastest — 3-6 months to contract)
- **Timeline:** 3-6 months from application to contract award
- **Value:** £100K-£350K
- **Probability:** ~10-20% success rate (highly competitive)
- **Why it works:** Designed for non-traditional suppliers. No clearance required initially. IP stays with you.

**Step-by-step:**
1. Submit application (see Phase 1)
2. If shortlisted: DASA may invite you to present (15-min pitch + Q&A)
3. Assessment panel reviews (mix of technical, military, commercial experts)
4. Decision notification
5. Contract negotiation (standard DASA terms)
6. Project kickoff

**Success factors:**
- Clear alignment with specific defence need
- Strong end-user engagement (have you talked to the military users?)
- Realistic project plan and budget
- Clear exploitation pathway beyond the project
- Good value for money

---

#### PATH B: R-Cloud Tasking (Fast — 2-4 months)
- **Timeline:** 2-4 months from task publication to award
- **Value:** £10K-£500K (typical range)
- **Probability:** Higher than DASA if you have relevant capability
- **Why it works:** Only matched suppliers see each task — less competition

**Process:**
1. Join R-Cloud (if not already)
2. Receive email notification when tasks match your capabilities
3. Review tasking form (statement of requirement)
4. Submit response via R-Cloud portal
5. Evaluation against published criteria
6. Award notification
7. Task order issued

**Pro Tips:**
- Respond quickly to task notifications
- Ask clarification questions via the portal
- Tailor each response to the specific requirement
- Price competitively — R-Cloud has rate caps

---

#### PATH C: CommercialX Fast-Track (Medium — 3-6 months)
- **Timeline:** 3-6 months
- **Value:** Up to £4M (recent awards)
- **Why it works:** Designed specifically to cut through procurement red tape

Recent example: 13 startups awarded contracts up to £4M each through CommercialX (2025), including:
- Aquark Technologies (quantum)
- Kraken Technology Group (autonomous systems)
- Helyx Secure Information Systems (cyber)
- SimCentric (simulation)

**How to access:**
- Monitor DSP for CommercialX opportunities
- Contact your DASA Innovation Partner for introduction
- Attend CommercialX Dragons' Den events
- Submit capability brief to CommercialX team

---

#### PATH D: SBRI (Small Business Research Initiative)
- **Timeline:** 3-6 months per phase
- **Value:** Phase 1: £50K-£120K / Phase 2: £1M-£2.5M
- **Why it works:** Designed for SMEs. Procurement (not grant) — so it's a contract.

**Process:**
1. Find open SBRI competitions on [apply-for-innovation-funding.service.gov.uk](https://apply-for-innovation-funding.service.gov.uk)
2. Check defence-relevant competitions
3. Submit application (two-stage: outline + full)
4. If successful: Phase 1 contract
5. If Phase 1 successful: invited to Phase 2

#### PATH E: Direct Engagement with Programme Offices (Slowest but highest value)
- **Timeline:** 6-18 months
- **Value:** £500K-£10M+
- **Why it works:** Direct relationship with the buyer

**How:**

2. Attend market engagement events
3. Submit RFI (Request for Information) response
4. Bid when formal opportunity arises
5. Or: get direct award if you're the only supplier (rare for first contract)

### 2.2 How to Price the First Contract

**DASA/R-Cloud Pricing:**
- DASA: Cost your project plan realistically. Include:
  - Staff costs (day rates £400-£1,200 for AI specialists)
  - Equipment/cloud costs
  - Travel and subsistence
  - Overheads (typically 20-30%)
  - Profit margin (10-20% for research contracts)

- R-Cloud: Day rates are capped. Typical rates:
  - Graduate/Junior: £300-£500/day
  - Consultant/Senior: £600-£900/day
  - Principal/Expert: £800-£1,200/day
  - Programme Manager: £700-£1,000/day

**Rules:**
- Always price for value, not cost
- Defence buyers understand that good AI talent is expensive
- Don't underprice — it signals low quality
- Include contingency (10-15%) for research risk
- Be transparent about pricing — MOD hates hidden costs

### 2.3 What to Deliver (and How to Exceed Expectations)

#### The Minimum:
- Deliverables specified in contract
- Final report
- Any agreed prototypes/demonstrations
- Monthly progress reports
- Financial reports

#### How to Exceed:
1. **Over-communicate** — Weekly short updates, not monthly long ones
2. **Bring the end user in early** — Invite military stakeholders to demos throughout, not just at the end
3. **Document everything** — MOD loves good documentation. Create user guides, technical manuals, integration guides.
4. **Think beyond the contract** — Show how this scales, what Phase 2 looks like, how it integrates with other systems
5. **Solve problems they didn't know they had** — If you discover adjacent issues, flag them with potential solutions
6. **Deliver early** — If you can deliver a milestone early, do it
7. **Make them look good** — Help your MOD sponsor show value to their chain of command

### 3.5 Handling Classified Environments

#### Option 1: Work On-Site at MOD Facilities
- No FSC needed for your premises

- Takes 3-6 months to obtain
- Need for larger/scaled contracts

- MOD has approved cloud services for classified data
- Good middle ground — no FSC needed, but data stays in approved environment

### 4.1 How to Sell to NATO

#### NATO DIANA (Defence Innovation Accelerator for the North Atlantic)

NATO's flagship accelerator for dual-use deep tech.

**What you get:**
- Phase 1: Up to €100,000 grant + 6-month accelerator
- Phase 2: Up to €300,000 follow-on grant + 6-month tailored support
- Access to 30+ test centres across NATO countries
- Connections to military end-users and procurement
- Non-dilutive — you keep 100% of your IP

**Eligibility:**
- Headquartered in a NATO member nation (UK qualifies)
- Technology at TRL 4+ (ideally 3-4, but 5-6 accepted)
- Early-stage startup or SME
- Dual-use technology (civilian + defence)

**Application Process:**
1. Annual call opens: Early June
2. Deadline: Mid-July (e.g., 2026: 2 June — 11 July)
3. Submit online application (5 pages — short)
4. If shortlisted: full technical application + pitch
5. Panel assessment (military, business, technology, investment experts)
6. Phase 1 selection
7. After Phase 1: selection for Phase 2

**2026 Challenge Themes:**
- Energy and Power
- Data and Information Security
- Sensing and Surveillance
- Human Health and Performance
- Critical Infrastructure and Logistics

**Focus Areas:** AI, autonomy, quantum, biotech, energy, novel materials

**Application:** [diana.nato.int/challenges.html](https://www.diana.nato.int/challenges.html)

**Pro Tips:**
- Emphasise DUAL-USE — civilian applications are essential
- Show investor readiness and scale-up strategy
- No previous defence experience required (they actively want non-traditional)
- Participation in Phase 1 is MANDATORY to reach Phase 2

### 4.3 Export Control Compliance

If you're selling AI/software to allied nations, you MUST understand export controls.

#### Key Regulations:

**UK Strategic Export Controls**
- Governed by Export Control Act 2002
- Controlled by Export Control Joint Unit (ECJU)
- Some AI/ML software is controlled under "dual-use" regulations
- Check the UK Strategic Export Control Lists

**EU Dual-Use Regulation** (if exporting to EU)
- Controls technology with both civilian and military applications
- Some AI/quantum technologies are specifically controlled

**US ITAR (International Traffic in Arms Regulations)**
- Applies if you're working with US defence companies or AUKUS partners
- Very strict — violations carry criminal penalties
- Need to register with US State Department if handling ITAR data

**Wassenaar Arrangement**
- Multilateral export control regime
- Covers some intrusion software, surveillance technologies

**Action Steps:**
1. Check if your technology is on export control lists
2. Apply for Open General Export Licences (OGELs) where available
3. Register with ECJU for Standard Individual Export Licences (SIELs) if needed
4. Get legal advice if dealing with US ITAR
5. Consider hiring export control compliance specialist

## SALES TECHNIQUES THAT WORK IN DEFENCE

---

### The "Lunch and Learn"
- **What:** 60-minute session over lunch. 20-min presentation + 40-min discussion.
- **Best for:** Introducing your capability to a new team or unit
- **How to organise:** Offer through your DASA Innovation Partner or MOD contact. Provide lunch (or virtual equivalent).
- **Cost:** £200-£500 for lunch
- **Conversion rate:** 30-40% lead to follow-up meetings
- **Best for:** AI startups (low pressure, educational format)

---

### The "Demo Day"
- **What:** Live demonstration of your technology to multiple stakeholders
- **Best for:** Visual/tangible AI capabilities (computer vision, autonomous systems)
- **How to organise:** Book through DAIC or DASA. Can be at your office or MOD site.
- **Cost:** £500-£2,000 (setup, travel, materials)
- **Conversion rate:** 40-50% if demo is compelling
- **Best for:** AI startups with impressive visual demos

---

### The "Pilot Programme"
- **What:** Small-scale trial of your technology in a real operational setting
- **Best for:** Proving value before major commitment
- **Typical structure:** 3-6 month trial, funded by MOD, with defined success criteria
- **Cost to run:** £50K-£200K (your costs)
- **Conversion rate:** 60-70% to full contract if pilot succeeds
- **Best for:** ALL AI startups (this is the #1 path to big contracts)

**Structure:**
1. Define the problem and success metrics WITH the customer
2. Run 90-day sprint
3. Weekly check-ins
4. Final demonstration with metrics
5. Go/no-go decision for scale

---

### The "Reference Site Visit"
- **What:** Take prospective customers to see your technology in use at another MOD site
- **Best for:** When you have a successful deployment to showcase
- **How to organise:** Your existing customer hosts. You facilitate.
- **Cost:** £500-£1,000 (travel, coordination)
- **Conversion rate:** 70-80% (most powerful technique)
- **Best for:** Stage 2-3 (when you have reference customers)

---

### The "White Paper"
- **What:** Authoritative document on your technology's defence application
- **Best for:** Establishing thought leadership, getting on the radar of senior officers
- **How to publish:** Submit to DASA, DAIC, or defence journals (RUSI, WIRED Defence, etc.)
- **Cost:** £2K-£5K (your time + design)
- **Conversion rate:** 10-20% direct, but builds brand awareness
- **Best for:** All stages — especially effective for getting initial meetings

---

### The "Conference Speaking Slot"
- **What:** Present at defence conferences (DSEI, Cyber UK, DAIC Connect, etc.)
- **Best for:** Building credibility, reaching multiple buyers at once
- **How to get:** Submit speaker proposals 6-9 months in advance
- **Cost:** Free (if accepted) to £5K+ (if sponsored)
- **Conversion rate:** 20-30% of attendees request follow-up
- **Best for:** Stage 2-3 (when you have results to share)

---

### Which Works Best for AI Startups?

**Ranked by effectiveness for AI startups:**

1. **Pilot Programme** (60-70% conversion) — Essential. Defence buyers need to see your AI working in their environment before committing big budgets.

2. **Reference Site Visit** (70-80%) — Most powerful, but requires existing customers.

3. **Demo Day** (40-50%) — Great for AI because you can show, not tell.

4. **Lunch and Learn** (30-40%) — Best entry-level technique. Low pressure, educational.

5. **Conference Speaking** (20-30%) — Good for credibility building.

6. **White Paper** (10-20%) — Long-term brand building.

**Recommended combination for AI startups:**
- **Months 1-6:** Lunch and Learn + White Paper (get on radar)
- **Months 6-12:** Demo Day + Pilot Programme (prove value)
- **Months 12-18:** Reference Site Visit + Conference Speaking (scale)

## FINAL NOTES

### The Mindset Shift

Defence is NOT like selling to other industries. Key differences:

1. **The buyer is not the user.** The person who signs the contract is not the person who uses your AI. You need to satisfy both.

2. **Security is non-negotiable.** You can't "move fast and break things" in defence. Security, safety, and ethics come first.

3. **Sales cycles are long.** 6-18 months is normal. Budget for it.

4. **Relationships matter.** Defence is a relationship-driven market. Invest time in building trust.

5. **The MOD WANTS you to succeed.** They are actively trying to engage non-traditional suppliers. Take advantage of it.

6. **Start small, think big.** Your first £100K DASA contract can lead to £10M+ follow-on work.

7. **Dual-use is your friend.** Technologies with civilian AND military applications are easier to fund and scale.

8. **Document everything.** MOD loves audit trails, progress reports, and clear deliverables.

9. **Be patient but persistent.** Follow up, but don't be pushy. Defence buyers are busy and security-conscious.

10. **Deliver on your promises.** Reputation is everything. One failed delivery can close doors. One exceptional delivery opens many.
