## 6. AI GOVERNANCE & AUTONOMOUS WEAPONS REGULATION

### 6.1 The Global Regulatory Landscape (2026)

AI governance for defense is the most complex regulatory challenge facing DEFONEOS. Multiple overlapping frameworks are developing simultaneously across jurisdictions, and compliance failure could result in market exclusion, criminal liability, or operational restrictions.

**Key Insight**: The window to influence these frameworks is closing. The UN Secretary-General has called for a legally binding instrument on autonomous weapons by **end of 2026**.

### 6.2 Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS): The UN Process

**The CCW Group of Governmental Experts (GGE):**
- Operating since 2014 under the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons
- Developed a "rolling text" with elements of prohibitions and regulations
- Two-tier approach gaining consensus: (1) prohibit unacceptable systems, (2) regulate acceptable ones

**November 2025 UN General Assembly Resolution:**
- **156 states in favor**, 5 against (Russia, Belarus, North Korea + allies), 8 abstentions
- Calls for urgent measures based on two-tier approach
- Informal consultations held May 2025 with 96 states participating

**Key Provisions Under Discussion (Rolling Text):**

| Element | Status |
|---------|--------|
| Prohibition on systems without "context-appropriate human control" | Emerging consensus |
| Predictability, reliability, traceability, explainability requirements | Broad support |
| Geographic, temporal, and target-type restrictions | Under negotiation |
| Self-destruct/deactivation mechanisms | Broad support |
| Bias mitigation in AI decision-making | Under negotiation |
| Anti-personnel targeting prohibition | Some support; contested |
| Meaningful human control definition | Key sticking point |

**What This Means for DEFONEOS:**
- Any autonomous system with lethal capability will require demonstrable human oversight
- Explainability and traceability must be built into system design from inception
- Systems deployed before treaty ratification may face retroactive compliance requirements
- The definition of "meaningful human control" will determine operational boundaries

### 6.3 EU AI Act: Defense Implications

**Key Facts:**
- Full enforcement timeline: **2027** for high-risk systems
- **Article 2 exemption**: National defense and military systems are EXEMPT from the AI Act
- However: defense contractors serving dual-use markets must comply for non-military applications
- High-risk system requirements affect any AI used in: critical infrastructure, law enforcement, border control, administration of justice

**DEFONEOS Implications:**
- Pure defense/military systems → Exempt
- Dual-use systems (e.g., border security, critical infrastructure protection) → Must comply
- Any EU market presence requires full AI Act compliance for non-exempt applications
- Penalties: Up to EUR 35 million or 7% of global turnover

**EU AI Act Risk Categories:**

| Category | Requirements | Defense Relevance |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Prohibited | Social scoring, subliminal manipulation | Indirect |
| High-Risk | Conformity assessment, risk management, human oversight | **HIGH** — dual-use systems |
| Limited Risk | Transparency obligations | **MEDIUM** — chatbots, deepfakes |
| Minimal Risk | Voluntary codes | Low |

### 6.4 UK AI Regulatory Framework

The UK has adopted a sector-led approach rather than comprehensive AI legislation:

**Active Regulations (2026):**
- Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 — AI-friendly data reforms
- Crime and Policing Act 2026 — Criminalizes AI-generated CSAM
- Online Safety Act 2023 — Platform duties for AI chatbots
- AI Security Institute (rebranded from Safety Institute) — Evaluation powers

**Expected 2026-2027:**
- ICO statutory code of practice on AI (by August 2027)
- Potential comprehensive AI Bill (delayed from 2025)
- Regulating for Growth Bill — AI testing sandbox framework

**Defense Relevance:** UK defense procurement increasingly requires AI ethics and safety documentation. Ministry of Defence AI Strategy requires human-machine teaming principles.

### 6.5 US AI Executive Orders & Defense-Specific Policy

**Current Status (2026):**
- Biden's EO 14144 (Jan 16, 2025) on AI security partially rescinded by Trump EO 14306 (Jun 6, 2025)
- EO 14306 removed procurement mandates but maintained support for PQC migration
- DoD Responsible AI Strategy and Implementation Pathway remains active
- NSA guidance on AI security for national security systems

**Key DoD AI Requirements:**
- Responsible AI (RAI) principles must be documented
- Algorithmic transparency and explainability
- Human-AI teaming guidelines
- Test and evaluation for AI-enabled systems

### 6.6 AI Liability Frameworks

**EU AI Liability Directive** (proposed): Would create strict liability for AI-caused harm, with burden of proof shifted to AI system operators.

**Key Risk**: If an autonomous defense system causes unintended harm (civilian casualties, friendly fire), liability could extend to:
- System developers
- Operators
- Command chain
- Nation-state deploying the system

**Mitigation**: Build traceability, explainability, and human override into every autonomous system from day one.

## 7. SYNTHETIC BIOLOGY FOR DEFENSE

### 7.1 The Biosecurity Threat Landscape

Synthetic biology is a dual-use technology with enormous potential for both threat and defense. The ability to engineer biological systems is becoming cheaper, faster, and more accessible — including to malicious actors.

**Threat Vectors:**
- Engineered pathogens with enhanced transmissibility or lethality
- Novel biotoxins designed to evade detection
- Bioweapons targeting specific genetic populations
- Agricultural bioweapons (crop/livestock targeting)

### 7.2 DARPA Programs Leading the Defense Response

**DARPA Biological Technologies Office (BTO) Active Programs:**

| Program | Objective | Timeline |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| **Detect It** | Rapid detection of novel biological threats | Ongoing — 2026+ |
| **Ag x BTO** | Agricultural defense: early warning, rapid countermeasures, crop engineering | FY2025-FY2026 |
| **Friend or Foe** | Identify engineered vs. natural organisms | 2025-2028 |
| **PREEMPT** | Pandemic prevention via engineered countermeasures | 2025-2029 |
| **SIGMA+** | Real-time biological threat sensing networks | Deployed + expanding |

**Ag x BTO Technical Areas (2025):**
1. Early warning systems for chemical/biological threats (autonomous, real-time)
2. Rapid-response agricultural countermeasures
3. Accelerated crop engineering for threat defeat
4. Predictive modeling of threat pathogenicity
5. Attribution of human-made vs. natural biological events

### 7.3 Bio-Surveillance AI

The intersection of AI and synthetic biology creates both risks and opportunities:

**AI for Biodefense:**
- Machine learning for rapid pathogen identification from genomic sequences
- AI-driven epidemiological modeling for outbreak prediction
- Automated synthesis screening (detecting dangerous DNA orders)
- Wastewater surveillance networks with AI analysis
- Metagenomic sequencing + AI classification for environmental monitoring

**Timeline to Practical Defense Applications:**
- **2026-2028**: Enhanced detection systems for known threats
- **2028-2030**: AI systems capable of identifying novel engineered pathogens
- **2030+**: Predictive biodefense (anticipating threats before they emerge)

### 7.4 DEFONEOS Synthetic Biology Strategy

Given DEFONEOS's AI focus, the synthetic biology opportunity is primarily at the intersection:

**2028-2030: MONITOR**
- Track DARPA BTO program outputs for AI integration opportunities
- Develop AI models for rapid biological threat classification
- Partner with biosecurity firms on sensor data analysis

**2030-2031: INTEGRATE**
- Bio-surveillance AI module for DEFONEOS platforms
- Genomic sequence analysis at the edge (neuromorphic + AI)
- Early warning system integration with CBRN defense networks

## 8. ENERGY & POWER SYSTEMS

### 8.1 The Defense Energy Challenge

Modern military operations are increasingly power-hungry: AI data centers, directed energy weapons, autonomous platforms, advanced communications, and radar systems all demand enormous energy. The Defense Science Board identified energy as a critical vulnerability as early as 2016.

**The Problem**: The DoD spends approximately **$10 billion annually on diesel**. Fuel convoys in Iraq/Afghanistan accounted for up to 50% of logistics and represented the highest casualty risk.

### 8.2 Nuclear Microreactors: Project Pele & Beyond

**Project Pele** (DoD Strategic Capabilities Office):

| Specification | Value |
|--------------|-------|
| **Power output** | 1.5 MWe (demonstration); 1-5 MWe (target) |
| **Reactor type** | High-Temperature Gas Reactor (HTGR) |
| **Fuel** | TRISO particles with HALEU |
| **Transport** | 4 standard 20-foot shipping containers |
| **Setup time** | <72 hours from arrival to full power |
| **Operational life** | 3+ years without refueling |
| **Contractor** | BWX Technologies (BWXT) |
| **Prime partners** | Rolls-Royce (power conversion), Northrop Grumman (controls) |

**Timeline:**
- **Sep 2024**: Groundbreaking at Idaho National Laboratory
- **Jul 2025**: BWXT began fabrication of reactor core
- **Dec 2025**: TRISO fuel delivered to INL
- **2026**: Full assembly transport to INL
- **2027**: System testing begins
- **2028**: Electricity generation demonstration (per EO 14299 deadline)

**Janus Program** (US Army):
- Deploy commercial nuclear microreactors on military installations by **2028**
- Nine sites pre-selected for possible deployment
- Builds on lessons from Project Pele
- Goal: Resilient, grid-independent base power

**ANPI (Advanced Nuclear Power for Installations)**:
- Launched 2024
- Aim: Two microreactors operational on military bases by **2030**
- Eight companies eligible for Other Transaction Awards

**Implications for DEFONEOS:**
- Microreactors enable forward-deployed AI compute centers (no grid dependency)
- Power for directed energy weapons, advanced radar, quantum computers
- 10-year+ fuel cycle eliminates logistics vulnerability
- By 2030, microreactors will be standard for strategic military installations

### 8.3 Solid-State Batteries

**Status (2026):**
- Toyota: Solid-state battery production targeted for 2027-2028
- QuantumScape: Layered manufacturing improvements; production samples to automotive partners
- Samsung SDI: Prototype solid-state cells with 900 Wh/L energy density

**Defense Advantages:**
- 2-3x energy density vs. lithium-ion (longer range/endurance)
- Non-flammable (transport and storage safety)
- Wider operating temperature range
- Faster charging

**Timeline to Defense Integration:** 2028-2030

### 8.4 Wireless Power Transfer

- Microwave beaming for UAV charging (DARPA investigations)
- Inductive charging for underwater vehicles
- Laser power beaming for extended drone endurance
- **Status**: Experimental; limited defense applications 2028+

### 8.5 DEFONEOS Energy Strategy

**2028-2030: PREPARE**
- Design AI compute nodes for microreactor compatibility
- Evaluate solid-state batteries for edge platforms
- Plan microreactor-powered edge AI deployments

**2030+: DEPLOY**
- Microreactor-powered AI training centers at strategic bases
- Solid-state battery packs for all autonomous platforms
- Wireless charging infrastructure for drone fleets
