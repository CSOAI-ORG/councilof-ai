# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The global defense AI market represents a $9.31B industry (2024) projected to reach $19.29B by 2030 (CAGR 13.0%). Five companies dominate the landscape — Anduril, Palantir, Helsing, Shield AI, and Applied Intuition/EpiSci — but **no company has yet reached "Dominant Innovator" status** (per Information Matters analysis). The market is structurally fragmented, with each player excelling in distinct sub-segments while leaving critical gaps unaddressed.

**The DEFONEOS opportunity is clear**: There is no open, modular, UK/EU-sovereign "Defense AI Operating System" that integrates across the autonomous systems ecosystem. Every major player is either US-centric (Palantir, Anduril, Shield AI), vertically integrated and closed (Anduril, Helsing), or focused on narrow autonomy slices (Shield AI, Epirus, Merlin). Small nations, SMEs, and non-US allies lack access to affordable, interoperable defense AI infrastructure.

# PART 5: OPEN SOURCE DEFENSE AI LANDSCAPE

## Current State: Extremely Limited

The open source defense AI ecosystem is in its infancy. Unlike commercial AI which has rich open source tooling (PyTorch, TensorFlow, etc.), military-grade defense AI has almost no open source equivalent.

### What Exists

| Project/Resource | Description | Limitations |
|-----------------|-------------|-------------|
| **DoD GitHub Repositories** | ~184 repos; mostly utilities, workshops, tutorials | Not operational defense AI tools |
| **ROS/ROS2** | Robot Operating System (widely used, not defense-specific) | General-purpose robotics, not military |
| **MOOS-IvP** | Marine autonomy framework (MIT) | Maritime only, research-focused |
| **PX4/ArduPilot** | Open source autopilot (used in military drones) | Flight control only, not AI decision-making |
| **aerial-autonomy-stack** | ROS2 framework for aerial autonomy | Research-focused, limited domains |
| **Project Red Sword** | Intelligence community red team framework | Cybersecurity only, not operational |
| **Awesome AI Security** (GitHub) | Collection of AI security tools | Defensive cybersecurity, not military operations |

### What's Missing (Critical Gaps)

1. **No open source defense AI OS** — nothing like Linux for defense AI
2. **No open defense AI middleware** — no standard way for military systems to communicate
3. **No open C2 (command and control) framework** — every nation builds proprietary
4. **No open sensor fusion platform** — each vendor implements independently
5. **No open autonomy framework for multi-domain operations**
6. **No open military data standards** — proprietary formats dominate
7. **No open edge AI deployment framework for contested environments**

### Implications
- Every nation/vendor reinvents the wheel
- No interoperability between allied systems
- Small nations cannot afford custom development
- Innovation bottlenecked by closed ecosystems
- Massive duplication of effort

# PART 6: PRICING & BUSINESS MODELS IN DEFENSE AI

## Model Comparison

| Model | Description | Examples | Pros | Cons |

| **Cost-Plus** | Government pays R&D cost + profit margin | Traditional primes (Lockheed, BAE) | Low risk for contractor | Slow, expensive, no innovation incentive |
