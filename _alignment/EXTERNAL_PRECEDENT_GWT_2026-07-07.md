## 1. What the primary source actually says (verified)

- **Paper:** "Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models,"
  Anthropic (Transformer Circuits), published **6 July 2026**, 16 authors.
- **Finding:** a new Jacobian-based method (**J-lens**) reads a small, privileged
  internal region (**J-space**) where the model holds concepts it can report on,
  reason with, and steer — surrounded by a much larger body of automatic processing
  it cannot access. Swapping a J-space concept changes downstream deliberate answers
  (e.g. author-naming) but not automatic behaviour (e.g. continuing fluent Spanish).
- **Explicit limit:** Anthropic states the result does **not** establish whether
  Claude is conscious or has subjective experience. The defensible claim is a
  *functional* mechanism resembling **conscious access**, not phenomenal consciousness.
- **External validation:** Stanislas Dehaene & Lionel Naccache (architects of global
  neuronal workspace theory) gave invited commentary; Neel Nanda (DeepMind) replicated
  on open weights. J-lens is an approximation — it only captures single-token concepts
  and is not a complete detector.
- **Access:** J-space inspection needs model-internal access + interpretability
  tooling. Ordinary chats do not expose it.
