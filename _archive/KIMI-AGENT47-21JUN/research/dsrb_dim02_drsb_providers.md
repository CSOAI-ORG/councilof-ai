## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The search for "DSRB" reveals that the acronym primarily refers to the **Defence, Security and Resilience Bank** Development Group — a proposed multilateral institution for defense financing, not a distinct category of commercial digital resilience services for banks [^852^]. However, the broader concept of **Digital Resilience Services for Banks** represents a massive and rapidly growing ecosystem encompassing consulting firms, technology vendors, cybersecurity providers, regulatory technology platforms, and financial infrastructure companies that collectively help banks achieve operational resilience.

The market is driven primarily by the EU's **Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)**, which became fully enforceable on **January 17, 2025**, and equivalent regulations globally. DORA covers **22,000+ financial entities** and mandates five pillars: ICT risk management, incident reporting, resilience testing, third-party risk management, and information sharing [^917^][^987^].

**Key Finding**: There is no single category called "DSRB" — instead, digital resilience services for banks are delivered through a multi-layered ecosystem of consulting firms, technology platforms, specialized testing providers, and core banking vendors. This brief maps that ecosystem comprehensively.

## 1. WHAT IS DSRB? CLARIFYING THE DEFINITION

### 1.1 Primary Meaning: Defence, Security and Resilience Bank

The acronym "DSRB" (or "DSR Bank") refers to a **proposed multilateral development bank** focused on defense, security, and resilience financing across allied nations. It is designed to:
- Provide affordable sovereign financing for defense
- Support faster financing for defense acquisition programs
- Mobilize private capital for defense, security, and resilience firms [^852^]

**This is NOT a commercial service offering for banks.**

### 1.2 The Actual Ecosystem: Digital Resilience Services for Banks

The functional equivalent — the ecosystem of services banks use to build digital operational resilience — comprises **five distinct provider layers**:

| Layer | Description | Example Providers |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Strategic Consulting** | Framework design, gap analysis, regulatory compliance | Big 4 (Deloitte, KPMG, EY, PwC), Accenture |
| **Technology Platforms** | Cloud infrastructure, core banking, GRC platforms | IBM, Microsoft Azure, AWS, Google Cloud, ServiceNow |
| **Specialized Testing** | Penetration testing, TLPT, red teaming | Bishop Fox, NCC Group, Mandiant, Bureau Veritas |
| **RegTech / GRC Software** | Compliance automation, Register of Information, TPRM | OneTrust, 3rdRisk, ProcessUnity, SAI360, ServiceNow |
| **Core Banking & Infrastructure** | Resilient core systems, payment infrastructure | Temenos, SAP Fioneer, FIS, Finacle, Mambu, Broadridge |

### 1.3 Key Insight: Banks Are Both Consumers AND Providers of Resilience Services

Major banks like **JPMorgan Chase** and **Barclays** have developed internal resilience capabilities so sophisticated that they effectively operate as benchmarks — and in some cases, service providers — for the broader industry. JPMorgan's **$1.5 trillion Security and Resiliency Initiative** [^864^] and Barclays' **Security Shield** framework [^846^] represent industry-leading approaches.

## 10. STRATEGIC FINDINGS & RECOMMENDATIONS

### 10.1 Critical Insight: "DSRB" as a Category Does Not Exist

There is no established market category called "DSRB" or "Digital Resilience Services Banks." The acronym DSRB refers to a proposed defense financing institution. However, the **functional need** — banks requiring external support for digital operational resilience — represents one of the largest and fastest-growing markets in financial services technology.
