## 5. Partnership Strategy — Who to Approach & How

DORA compliance is not a product any single vendor can deliver alone. A significant institution needs governance frameworks, threat intelligence, incident reporting, resilience testing, third-party risk management, and information sharing — across five regulatory pillars and thousands of contractual relationships. The institutions that solve this fastest will assemble the right coalition, not build every component internally.

CSOAI's strategic imperative is therefore partnership-first. The platform brings AI-native governance, biological-inspired threat detection, and multi-agent consensus that no DORA competitor offers. But to reach 22,000 financial entities, CSOAI must embed itself inside channels banks already trust: Big Four consultancies that write compliance roadmaps, cloud providers that host infrastructure, GRC platforms that manage workflows, and innovation labs that introduce technology to the largest institutions.

This chapter maps a three-tier partnership strategy — Immediate (30 days), Short-Term (90 days), and Medium-Term (180 days) — anchored around a single commercial construct: **"Digital Resilience in a Box."** This is a coalition architecture combining CSOAI's AI governance engine with compliance, infrastructure, and consulting layers banks already buy, creating a turnkey solution a 200-person regional bank deploys in eight weeks rather than eighteen months.

The selection criteria for each tier are deliberate. Tier 1 partners are chosen because they have established DORA practices, formal partner programs with clear application paths, and gaps that CSOAI fills precisely. Tier 2 partners offer broader distribution but require longer sales or development cycles. Tier 3 partners provide ecosystem validation and long-term strategic positioning. Together, they create a partner mesh that covers every route to market in the DORA compliance value chain.

### 5.1 Tier 1 Partners (Immediate — 30 Days)

Tier 1 targets three partners that are (a) actively seeking DORA-capable technology, (b) already inside target institutions, and (c) open to commercial conversations on a 30-day horizon. Each addresses a different gap: GRC workflow (OneTrust), infrastructure hosting (IBM), and consulting implementation (Deloitte).

#### 5.1.1 OneTrust: 14,000 Customers, Zero AI Governance

OneTrust leads the GRC software market with **14,000 customers** globally and a platform covering privacy, security, ethics, and compliance workflows. The company has invested heavily in DORA readiness: its GRC module maps to all five pillars, supports Register of Information workflows, and provides policy management and control testing. What OneTrust does not have — and has shown no roadmap to build — is an AI governance layer connecting DORA operational resilience to the EU AI Act's risk classification, algorithmic accountability, and high-risk system documentation requirements [^854^].

CSOAI's pitch is precise: **"We are the AI governance layer for your DORA platform."** The BFT Council maps to EU AI Act governance requirements. Pheromone's nine-signal classification engine provides the real-time monitoring that AI Act Article 15 (accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity) demands. The MCP server catalog with attestation chains delivers the documentation trail Article 12 (record-keeping) requires. No other vendor combines biological-inspired threat detection with formal governance consensus for dual-framework compliance.

Apply through partner.onetrust.com with a **pre-built integration prototype** — a working API connection between Pheromone signals and OneTrust's incident management module — not a deck. OneTrust structures technology partnerships as **revenue-sharing arrangements in the 70/30 to 80/20 range** (vendor/OneTrust), with co-sell access to the enterprise customer base once integration is certified [^907^].

#### 5.1.2 IBM Partner Plus: Designated CTPP, 100+ FS Ecosystem Partners

IBM holds a unique position: the ESAs designated it a **Critical Third-Party Provider (CTPP)** under DORA Article 31 in January 2025 [^890^][^896^]. This means IBM is directly supervised by a Lead Overseer, must comply with the CTPP oversight framework, and is building compliance solutions at scale. IBM's Promontory unit is actively supporting EU financial institutions with DORA readiness assessments and implementation roadmaps [^897^].

Simultaneously, **IBM Cloud for Financial Services** has grown to **100+ ecosystem partners** including SAP, EY, and Tata Consultancy Services, serving BNP Paribas, Luminor Bank, and MUFG [^876^]. IBM provides the infrastructure; what it lacks is a native AI governance and operational resilience testing engine.

The pitch: **"We provide the resilience monitoring and AI governance layer for your financial services cloud ecosystem."** Register at partner.ibm.com and position CSOAI as the DORA resilience testing component IBM's 100+ bank clients need. IBM's ecosystem model involves **co-selling through marketplace listings with 15-20% revenue share** and joint solution development for flagship clients [^876^]. The CTPP designation gives IBM both the regulatory imperative and client urgency to move quickly.

#### 5.1.3 Deloitte Alliance: Largest DORA Advisory, Seeking RegTech

Deloitte has positioned itself as the leading DORA advisory practice, publishing the "RegTech Universe" map covering 80+ RegTech firms and developing **RegMiner**, an AI-powered regulatory compliance solution [^852^][^854^]. Deloitte's research shows 87% of financial institutions view RegTech as significant to strategic initiatives, and the firm is actively seeking technology partners to complete its DORA stack [^852^].

RegMiner covers regulatory change management. It does not cover biological-inspired threat detection, multi-agent governance consensus, or the intersection of AI governance with operational resilience. Deloitte's DORA clients need a platform connecting regulatory compliance (what DORA says you must do) with AI governance (how you ensure your AI systems remain resilient while doing it).

The pitch: **"We are the only AI governance + DORA dual-compliance engine. White-label our platform under your brand, add your consulting, and deliver a complete solution in 90 days."** Apply through deloitte.com/en/services/consulting-risk. Deloitte typically takes a **30-40% margin on technology implementations**, but the volume multiplier is substantial: the firm's financial services practice touches hundreds of banks annually, and a DORA offering with AI governance differentiation has no equivalent across the other Big Four [^852^][^907^].

These should be pursued in parallel with Tier 1, with agreements expected within 90 days.

#### 5.3.2 AWS Marketplace D-CAT Program

AWS is also a likely CTPP candidate, which will create additional urgency for banks on AWS to demonstrate compliance [^850^]. List CSOAI as SaaS under "DORA Compliance & Digital Resilience" — 31% of enterprises buy software through cloud marketplaces, and AWS takes only **3%**, meaning CSOAI retains 97% of marketplace revenue while eliminating the 6-12 month vendor onboarding cycle that typically blocks adoption [^910^].

#### 5.3.3 FS-ISAC Critical Providers Program

FS-ISAC is the only global cyber intelligence sharing community focused on financial services, with **7,000+ members** across 75 countries [^957^]. The Critical Providers Program, launched January 2022 with Akamai Technologies as founding member, is specifically designed for service providers serving the financial sector. Membership provides access to threat intelligence feeds, incident response coordination, and distribution to 7,000+ institutions already committed to information sharing as a regulatory requirement. Pitch **Pheromone-based threat signal distribution** enriching FS-ISAC feeds with AI-classified, attestation-backed anomaly detection that meets Article 45's trusted-community requirements.
