## Current workflow (what David actually does today)

1. **08:00–09:00** — Read overnight emails (founders pitch overnight from SF/NYC). Average: 50-80 inbound pitches/week per partner.
2. **09:00–10:00** — Investment Committee prep. Read memos for the week's IC meetings.
3. **10:00–12:00** — 2-3 founder meetings per day. 30 min each. 5-7% conversion to second meeting.
4. **12:00–14:00** — Lunch with portfolio founders / LPs.
5. **14:00–17:00** — Diligence on active deals. Reference calls, customer references, technical DD.
6. **17:00–19:00** — Networking events, dinner with co-investors.
7. **19:00–22:00** — Reading memos, replying to founders, weekend partner calls.

**Tools used:** Affinity (CRM), Carta (cap table), DocSend (data rooms), Notion (IC memos), Slack (portfolio comms), LinkedIn Sales Navigator (sourcing), Pitchbook / Crunchbase (deal flow).

## Top 3 pain points (with real complaints)

### 1. Signal-to-noise in deep-tech deal flow
> "Every AI company says they have 'defensible IP'. 95% don't. We have to do the technical DD ourselves to figure out which 5% are real."
> — paraphrased from a16z partner Martin Casado, Sequoia AI Ascent 2023

UK deep-tech VC's biggest pain: filtering the 5% of AI pitches with real defensibility from the 95% with marketing-grade claims.

### 2. Pre-revenue valuation anxiety
> "Pre-revenue AI companies with no customers but 4 patent applications want £20M pre-money. We need to see at least one of: paying customer, design partner, or live production deployment."
> — typical LP feedback at BVCA panel, September 2024

UK VCs are skeptical of pre-revenue valuations, especially in AI where many companies raised on hype.

### 3. EU AI Act timing — opportunity and risk
> "Every AI deal we look at now has to factor in EU AI Act compliance. If a company can't answer 'are you Article 50 compliant?' in 30 seconds, they're not ready for EU customers."
> — paraphrased from LocalGlobe partner Saul Klein, AI safety panel, November 2024

UK VCs (especially those targeting EU customers) are increasingly asking about EU AI Act readiness as a Series A diligence criterion.

## Buying trigger (what makes David take a meeting)

- **Warm intro from a trusted portfolio founder** (70%+ conversion to meeting)
- **Pattern match to recent winning deal** (e.g. "this is the X of Y")
- **Live product demonstration** (signals technical execution)
- **Strong IP story** (patents filed, defensibility narrative)
- **UK deep-tech narrative** (national interest, sovereign AI, post-Brexit competitiveness)
- **Inbound from a competitor VC saying "you should look at this"** (FOMO effect)

**What DOESN'T work for David:**
- Cold email without personalization
- "We're the X of Y" without proof
- Pitch decks without live product
- Generic AI hype without technical depth

## Decision criteria (what makes David say YES to IC)

1. **Live product** — not "we're building this", but "this is the URL"
2. **Real customer usage** — even free tier users count
3. **Defensible IP** — filed patents, not just "we plan to file"
4. **Regulatory tailwind** — EU AI Act is the biggest 2026 AI regulatory catalyst
5. **Capital efficiency** — what does the £3M buy? How many months runway?
6. **Founder technical depth** — can the founder answer technical DD questions?
7. **Market size** — TAM, SAM, SOM with credible bottom-up math

## Objections (what makes David say NO)

- "Pre-revenue with no design partners" → "Come back with paying customers"
- "We have 47 patents pending" → "How many are granted?"
- "Our TAM is $50B" → "What's your realistic bottom-up SAM?"
- "We're going to be the EU leader" → "Show me one enterprise customer"
- "Our AI is safer than OpenAI's" → "How is that verifiable?"
