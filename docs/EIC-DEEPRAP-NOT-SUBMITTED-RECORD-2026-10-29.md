## 2. Eligibility ruling — settled

**This ruling is made by this record, on the call text and the public register. It is not a determination by the
European Commission, and no Commission officer has reviewed it.**

**The single-applicant route was ELIGIBLE IN PRINCIPLE.** EIC WP 2026 p. 33 states: *"The EIC Pathfinder Challenges
can support projects from consortia or from single legal entities… If the proposal is from one single legal entity,
this entity must be established in a Member State or an Associated Country (unless stated otherwise in the specific
Challenge chapter)."* CSOAI LTD is established in the United Kingdom, which appears in the Annex 2 list of *"countries
associated to Horizon Europe – Pillar III"* (p. 176), with footnote 286 recording that *"The United Kingdom is associated
to the entire Horizon Europe Programme, with the only exception of the investment component of the EIC Accelerator
managed by the EIC Fund … for award procedures implementing Union budget for the year 2024 and onwards."* This call is a
2026-work-programme grant-only call, so that exception does not bite: a UK applicant is a **Beneficiary** (not an
Associated Partner) and is funded directly by the Commission. The Horizon Europe **Guarantee** scheme covers the
2021–2022–2023 work-programme calls and is **not** the route for this one.

**No consortium was needed, and none was required to be assembled.** A consortium was an *alternative* route, not a
precondition: two entities must be independent legal entities from two different Member States or Associated Countries;
three or more must include at least one entity established in a Member State and at least two other independent legal
entities each established in a different Member State or Associated Country. Taking the single-applicant route meant no
partner had to be found, named, or signed — which is one of the reasons the application-side facts were tractable at
all. The 2026 DeepRAP Challenge chapter (pp. 44–47) contains **no eligibility carve-out** of its own, so the p. 33
general conditions apply unchanged.

**The mid-cap / large-company restriction was addressed and is satisfied on the public record, subject to the applicant's
own declaration.** p. 33: *"In the case of single beneficiary projects, mid-caps and larger companies will not be
permitted."* p. 24 defines *"Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs) … enterprises that employ fewer than 250 persons
and have either an annual turnover not exceeding EUR 50 million, or an annual balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 43
million"* per Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC, and *"Small mid-cap means an enterprise employing up to 499
employees."* **What the public record establishes:** CSOAI LTD has exactly **1 officer / 0 resignations** and **1 active
PSC** holding **75% or more** of shares (Companies House, read live 2026-10-29); a statement of capital of **GBP 1**;
incorporation on 2 Jan 2026 with **no accounts filed** (first accounts to 31 Jan 2027, due 2 Oct 2027); and the estate's
own records show **FTE 1**. On headcount, the applicant is nowhere near the mid-cap or large-company thresholds on any
reading of the public record. **What the public record does not establish:** because **no accounts have been filed**,
there is **no published turnover and no published balance sheet**, so the financial half of the SME test cannot be
verified from the register at all — and the establishment of the applicant as an SME remains the applicant's own
declaration to make and to certify in the portal at submission. **SME status must be confirmed and declared by the
applicant at submission.** Nothing in this record certifies it.

The reason is a **combination**, not a single cause. Stated precisely, with owners:

**(a) The deadline passed before a compliant submission could be made.** The window closed at **2026-10-28T16:00Z**
(28 Oct 2026, 17:00 Brussels; p. 34: *"The call deadline for submitting your proposal is 28 October 2026 at 17h00
Brussels local time"*). The call is single-stage, so there is no second round and no late or partial submission path.

**(b) The portal organisation registration and the PIC are human-held and cannot be created by an agent.** The route to
a PIC requires an EU Login, a Funding & Tenders Portal organisation registration for CSOAI LTD, and the person in
charge of that profile requesting the PIC from the Commission. The estate's own records put this to the owner in the

Portal yourself"* (`_alignment/drafts/DEEPRAP-EVALUATION-PARTNER-OUTLINE-2026-09-28.md:3`). As of this record the
registration and the PIC **do not exist**, so no submission vehicle existed. Owner: **the owner (human, sole
director).**

**(c) Key-staff CVs and staff-effort budget arithmetic are human facts that are not present in the estate and must
not be invented.** No CV exists for any proposed key person (§1); the only rate model in the estate belongs to a
different programme and has every salary line marked *"OWNER TO CONFIRM"*. The packet deliberately leaves these out
and says so (S12 §8 items 3–4). A proposal that states CV and rate facts the estate does not hold would be a false
statement to the Commission, so these could not be filled in. Owner: **the owner (human).**

**(d) Legal declarations must be signed by a human.** The F&T legal declarations, and the ethics, data-protection and
conflict-of-interest declarations, require a human signature; none exists (§1). An agent must not sign or submit on
CSOAI LTD's behalf — the packet states this in terms: *"Nothing will be submitted under CSOAI LTD's name, and no legal
declaration will be signed, by an agent"* (S12 §9). Owner: **the owner (human).**

the science and remains the estate's asset. It does **not** say the deadline was missed by a scheduling error, an
oversight, or a failure of diligence; the estate's own 7 Oct go/no-go record shows the decision was taken deliberately
