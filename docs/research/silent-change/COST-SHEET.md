## Cheapest design that still gives valid rates

- One batch per model per day, 600 calls. The test needs both batches to have the same K and the same items; K = 30 is the owner's number and the permutation test is exact for it. Fewer repeats would need a new calibration, because the rates belong to the method as registered.
- Calibration is paid for once, not per model. Its rates are the credentials for every watched model, with the stated caveat that an open model served locally is a different arm from a hosted endpoint.
- Not adopted, because it would be a different method needing its own calibration: a cheap daily K = 10 screen with a K = 30 confirmation only when the screen moves. It could cut routine spend to about a third. Listed as a protocol 2 candidate.
- Provider batch endpoints may halve price; whether each provider offers one and whether it routes to the same replicas as the live endpoint: unverified. Not used.
- Rate limits: 600 calls a model a day is small, but running public items 30 times can meet a provider's anti-abuse rules. That is part of what the person confirming the terms must read.

## What the open-model half needs

Superseded by addendum 2 of the protocol: the model is served on the runner itself (no GPU, no hosted route, no rented compute), in about a dozen jobs of an hour or less. Disk on the runner is under 3 GB per job; nothing is downloaded to the M4 except the one-off hash checks and the rehearsal, which were deleted.
