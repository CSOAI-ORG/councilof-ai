# CSOAI evidence-review pilot

**Private working proposal — not sent, not a contract, no public price.**

## The buying problem
A buyer, risk owner or procurement team needs to decide whether a specific AI vendor or system claim has usable supporting evidence. A long policy document, model benchmark or registry entry alone does not answer that question.

## The first scope to sell
Review **one named product or system version, one declared use context, and one agreed claim set**. Start with materials the organisation owns or is permitted to supply and public documents that may lawfully be inspected. Lock the source list and dates before the work begins.

The customer receives an evidence inventory, a claim-to-source table, explicit gaps and contradictions, a prioritised request for missing evidence, and a portable inspection packet. A readable cover sheet explains what was checked, the limitations and the next decisions. The existing private demonstration already exports and checks retained source-consistency evidence; it does not demonstrate completion of a customer-specific organisational-control audit.

## Acceptance criteria to agree before quoting
1. Every in-scope claim identifies the exact subject, version, context, date and source or records that the evidence is missing.
2. Another reviewer can retrieve the agreed evidence packet and reproduce the stated integrity and source-consistency checks without access to CSOAI's internal machines.
3. Unsupported, disputed and uncheckable items remain distinct. No benchmark score is converted to legal compliance or a fine estimate.
4. The customer can raise a specific discrepancy. Any correction preserves the original observation and links the superseding finding.
5. Delivery and customer acknowledgement are recorded separately from payment, signing and optional witnessing.

## Explicit exclusions
No blanket certification, legal opinion, regulator approval, investment view or guarantee of safety. No production access, adversarial testing or third-party scanning without separately agreed scope and authority. No inference that the absence of public evidence proves misconduct. No new model evaluation, human study, reserve verification or chain-finality claim is included merely because the packet can be verified.

## Written discovery questions
What exact decision must this review support, and who makes it? Which product version and deployment context matter? Which five claims cause the most uncertainty? What materials may be shared, with whom, and for how long? Which evidence must remain private? What would the recipient need to see to accept the work? Are any regulator-facing or legal determinations expected, requiring a qualified professional outside this scope?

## Delivery workflow
Written discovery → agreed claim/source scope → authority and handling rules → private quote and acceptance criteria → source capture and checks → qualified review → packet delivery → recipient's acceptance or documented discrepancy → correction or follow-on scope.

Fees, timing and resource ceilings are set privately after the scope is known. Payment cannot purchase a favourable finding. Free verification is separate from paid evidence-production work.

## Commercial evidence to retain
Track the origin of the enquiry, qualified scope, accepted quote, direct compute and storage costs, reviewer hours, delivery acknowledgement, outside cash collected, refunds, margin and follow-on demand. Internal demonstrations, self-funded transactions, downloads, email drafts and anonymous page views are not customer revenue or adoption.
