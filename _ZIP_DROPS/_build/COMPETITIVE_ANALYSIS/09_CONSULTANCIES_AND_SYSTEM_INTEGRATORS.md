# 09 — Consultancies & System Integrators

These are services companies, not software products. They compete for the **AI governance services budget** and are also a major sales channel for CSOAI.

---

## 9.1 Big 4 AI Governance practices

**Deloitte** (UK / IE / DE / FR / NL all have AI governance offerings), **PwC** (UK Responsible AI, US Responsible AI), **EY** (EY.ai, EY.ai Confidence), **KPMG** (KPMG Trusted AI, KPMG EU AI Act Centre of Excellence).

**Form:** Public companies (or partnerships). AI governance practice revenue is typically reported as part of "consulting", not separately disclosed. **needs primary research** for any specific 2026 AI governance practice revenue.

**Positioning:** End-to-end AI governance programme delivery — assessment, gap analysis, framework design, operating-model design, vendor selection, programme assurance, regulator advisory.

**Modules / services:**
- EU AI Act readiness assessment
- NIST AI RMF implementation
- ISO/IEC 42001 implementation support
- High-risk system classification
- Risk-management framework design
- Provider / deployer / importer / distributor obligations
- Notified body preparation
- Governance operating-model design
- Sectoral readiness (financial services, healthcare, public sector)
- AI literacy programme design (Article 4)
- Post-market monitoring programme design
- Serious-incident reporting playbook

**Pricing:** Day-rate. Typical senior consultant day rates €2,500–€4,500. Programme engagements range €250k–€5M+ depending on scope and sector.

**USP:**
- C-suite access and procurement relationships.
- Regulator credibility (Big 4 staff second to regulators).
- Cross-border delivery capability.
- Multi-framework programme delivery.
- Single-point-of-accountability for the client.

**Weaknesses / vulnerabilities:**
- **Project-by-project.** No continuous software platform.
- **Not a "live" governance tool.** Their output is a PDF + Excel + PowerPoint deck, not a continuous monitor.
- **Day-rate model punishes the long tail.** A 5k-employee EU mid-market manufacturer cannot afford a Big 4 engagement.
- **No per-call x402.**
- **No MCP / A2A surface.**
- **No sovereign software plane.**
- **No signed artifacts.**
- **Conflicts of interest** — Big 4 audit firms (Deloitte, PwC, EY, KPMG) cannot simultaneously audit and implement AI governance under independence rules. This creates a soft conflict that CSOAI (independent software vendor) does not have.

**Exploitable gaps:**
- **CSOAI is the **software substrate** the Big 4 implementation lands on.** Every Big 4 EU AI Act assessment produces a CSOAI implementation roadmap. Sell *into* Big 4 as the platform.
- **Per-call x402 + Article 4 SME** — Big 4 cannot serve the long tail; CSOAI can.
- **Signed artifacts + sovereign plane + MCP/A2A** — Big 4 cannot ship these as software.
- **Independence** — CSOAI is independent software, not an audit firm; no conflict.

## Why CSOAI's biggest services competitor is not a competitor

The services vendors are *how* the buyer's AI governance programme gets built. CSOAI's commercial motion should be **a software platform the services vendors standardise on.** The deal model:

- Big 4 / boutique firm wins a €1M AI governance assessment deal.
- The deliverable is "implement CSOAI per the gap analysis".
- CSOAI captures the recurring licence revenue.

This is the **same model** as ServiceNow + Accenture, Salesforce + Deloitte, Snowflake + Capgemini.

---

## What CSOAI must NOT do

- Do not directly compete with Big 4 on services. The day-rate model is unbeatable at the top end.
- Do not undercut boutiques on advisory fees. Their credibility is the sell.
- Do not appear to "own" the methodology. The methodology is the buyer's; the platform is CSOAI's.
