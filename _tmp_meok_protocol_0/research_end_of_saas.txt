1. THE END OF SaaS THESIS
1.1 Who Is Saying It — Key Quotes and Sources
Satya Nadella, CEO Microsoft
“I think, the notion that business applications exist, that’s probably where they’ll all collapse, right in the agent era, because if you think about it, right, they are essentially CRUD databases with a bunch of business logic. The business logic is all going to these agents, and these agents are going to be multi repo CRUD… They’re going to update multiple databases, and all the logic will be in the AI tier. And once the AI tier becomes the place where all the logic is, then people will start replacing the back ends.”
— Satya Nadella, on the BG2 Podcast with Bill Gurley and Brad Gerstner, December 2024
Nadella elaborated that Microsoft is “aggressively” working to “collapse” backends in its Dynamics platform: “We are going to go pretty aggressively and try and collapse it all right, whether it’s in customer service, whether it’s in CRM, but even our finance and operations, because people want more AI-native biz apps.”
Nadella also predicted 2025 as “the year of abundance of large language models” where AI agents will disrupt traditional SaaS models by automating backend business logic.
Ben Thompson, Stratechery
“The public markets indicate as of January 2026 that SaaS is canceled. Are they right? I think it’s probably a mix… People saying they’re going to zero are wrong, but if the assumption is you’re fine, but you’re not going to be growing indefinitely, like that shift from thought of as being a growth company to being a stable… That’s a haircut.”
— Ben Thompson, on the Stratechery Podcast, February 2026
Thompson’s analysis, delivered in conversation with John Collison (Stripe), identifies the core structural problem: SaaS companies built on per-seat pricing face a fundamental disruption when AI agents can do the work that previously required multiple human seats. He distinguishes between systems of record (Workday, ERP) which are safer, and workflow/collaboration software which is most at risk.
Thompson also coined the concept of “just-in-time UI” — interfaces that don’t exist until an AI agent generates them for a specific task — rendering traditional persistent UIs obsolete.

2. WHAT REPLACES SaaS
2.1 Agent-as-a-Service (AaaS)
Definition: A cloud-based delivery model where autonomous AI agents are provisioned, managed, and operated on behalf of a business to complete specific tasks, workflows, or goals without constant human direction.
Core insight: SaaS gives you tools. AaaS gives you workers.
Market size: $5.1B (2024) to $47.1B by 2030 (44.8% CAGR)
Five core components of AaaS: 1. Reasoning Engine — LLM-powered decision layer 2. Memory — Short-term and long-term context 3. Tool Access — APIs, databases, enterprise systems 4. Planning Module — Goal decomposition into executable steps 5. Real-Time Communication Layer — WebRTC for voice/video agent interaction
Example pricing models: - Per-task pricing (pay per agent action) - Outcome-based pricing (pay per qualified lead, resolved ticket, completed call) - Compute-based pricing (pay for infrastructure used) - Hybrid models
2.2 Outcome-as-a-Service (OaaS)
Definition: Pay for results, not seats. The customer pays only when the AI agent delivers a measurable business outcome.
Evidence this is real:
“Enterprise buyers pay for outcomes, not seats. CFOs, Chief Risk Officers, and COOs now hold material AI budgets and measure value in basis points recovered, hours saved, and false-positives reduced — not seats.” — Anthemis (VC firm), May 2026
“Per-resolution is the right model for most teams. You pay for outcomes, not seats. The unit cost scales with success, which is the right alignment.” — eCorpIT, AI Customer Service Analysis, 2026
2.3 Conversation-as-a-Service (CaaS)
Definition: The interface IS the product. Rather than navigating complex software UIs, users simply state what they want in natural language, and AI agents execute.
Evidence: - Ant Digital Technologies (November 2025): Launched “conversation-as-a-service” mobile banking — customers use natural language to request services, AI completes transactions (transfers, repayments, pension inquiries). Results: 10% lift in mobile banking conversion rates at Bank of Shanghai. - Agricultural Bank of China (2025 Annual Report): Built a new interactive model of “Conversation-as-a-Service” with intelligent agents. 276 million MAU of mobile banking. - Salesforce State of Service: 89% of service professionals say conversational AI increases self-service resolution rates. - Google: AI Mode queries are 3x longer than traditional searches; 1 in 6 use voice/images — showing users are adapting to conversational interfaces.
2.4 Protocol-as-a-Service (PaaS)
Definition: Open protocols replace proprietary app interfaces. Agents communicate through standardized protocols rather than navigating individual app UIs.
The x402 Protocol (Stripe, Coinbase, Vercel):
x402 is an HTTP-native payment protocol designed for machine-to-machine transactions. It uses the long-reserved HTTP 402 “Payment Required” status code.
How it works: An AI agent can autonomously pay for API calls, compute, data, or services — as low as $0.01 USDC per transaction — without human intervention, pre-registration, or account setup.
“The agent didn’t need to pre-register an account or ask a human to complete a purchase. Payment is part of the conversation.” — Circle Labs x402 tutorial
Key partners: Stripe (for fiat on/off ramps and compliance), Coinbase (x402 protocol infrastructure), Vercel (x402-next middleware for API route paywalls), Circle Labs (USDC settlement).
x402 enables: - Pay-per-API-call without subscriptions - AI agent commerce (autonomous purchasing) - Micropayments for content (per-article, per-minute-viewed) - Machine-to-machine payments without human authorization
Anthropic’s MCP (Model Context Protocol): An open standard that enables AI agents to securely connect to external data sources and tools. Described as the “USB-C port for AI applications.”
2.5 Intelligence-as-a-Service (IaaS)
Definition: Raw AI capability delivered as a metered utility. Rather than buying software that contains AI features, you buy intelligence directly — reasoning, analysis, content generation, code writing — as a cloud service.
Market: $17B (2025) to $230B+ (2034)
Shift from CapEx to OpEx: AI shifts from capital expenditure (building models, buying hardware) to controlled operating expense (consuming intelligence via API).
2.6 Which Model Is Winning? Which Is Hype?
Most likely winning model: Hybrid Outcome-Based Pricing
The evidence suggests no single model dominates — instead, a hybrid:
Hype: Pure “conversation-as-a-service” as standalone. Most enterprise buyers want outcomes, not conversations. Reality: The pricing model shift is real — from per-seat to per-outcome is the most durable trend.

3. REAL COMPANIES MAKING THE SHIFT
3.1 Salesforce — Agentforce
The Pivot: From CRM to “AI Agent Platform”
Key milestones: - October 2024: Agentforce launched at Dreamforce 2024; 10,000+ agents created in 3 days - Internal use: Salesforce’s own AI agents resolve 84% of support cases without human help; Agentforce handled 2.8 million interactions in Slack, saving 500,000+ employee hours - Every one of Salesforce’s Top 10 Q4 customer wins included Agentforce Sales, Agentforce Service, and Data 360 - 60%+ of bookings came from existing customer expansion
Wiley case study: Upgraded to Agentforce-powered service agents — 40% case resolution improvement, 50% faster seasonal agent onboarding, 213% ROI, $230,000 in documented savings.
Verdict: Agentforce is the fastest-growing product in Salesforce history. But it’s important to note: Agentforce is priced at $2 per conversation — an outcome-based model, not per-seat. This is the shift.
3.2 Microsoft — Copilot Ecosystem
The Pivot: From Office/Cloud to “Copilot Everywhere”
The Strategy: - Nadella explicitly stated Microsoft is building toward an “AI tier” that will collapse traditional SaaS backends - Copilot becomes the organizing UI layer — agents in Excel, Word, Dynamics all coordinate through Copilot - Enterprise Agreement restructuring (November 2025): Eliminated A-D price tiers — most significant pricing change in 25 years - Microsoft stopped reporting AI-specific revenue after FY25 Q2 — when metric stops improving, companies stop reporting it
The Problem: Independent data shows competitive challenges: - Recon Analytics (January 2026): When users had access to Copilot, ChatGPT, and Gemini, only 8% chose Copilot as preferred tool - Copilot’s paid subscriber share dropped from 18.8% to 11.5% (July 2025 to Jan 2026) — 39% contraction in market position - 70% initially preferred Copilot (it’s in Office), but after trying alternatives, only 8% stayed
3.3 Google — AI Agents in Workspace and Cloud
The Pivot: From Search/Ads/Cloud to “Gemini Everywhere”
Google Workspace: - Gemini for Google Workspace integrated across all 15 products with 500M+ users - Enterprise customers save average 105 minutes per user per week - 75% of daily Gemini Workspace users say it improves quality of their work - 95% of top 20 SaaS companies and 80% of top 100 use Gemini
Key Google quote: > “75% of the company’s new code is AI-generated” — Sundar Pichai, April 2026
3.4 Stripe — x402 Protocol for Agent Payments
The Pivot: From human-to-business payments to machine-to-machine payments
What x402 does: - Uses HTTP 402 “Payment Required” status code - Enables AI agents to autonomously pay for API calls, compute, data - Transactions as low as $0.01 USDC - No account registration required - Payments land directly in Stripe balance, settle in fiat
The Ecosystem: - Stripe: Fiat on/off ramps, compliance, reporting - Coinbase: x402 protocol infrastructure - Vercel: x402-next middleware for API paywalls - Circle Labs: USDC settlement - Solana: High-throughput micropayments - Lightning Network: Instant microtransactions
The Significance: x402 is the payment layer for the agent economy. If agents can’t pay, the agent economy can’t function. Stripe is positioning itself as the financial infrastructure for AaaS.
3.5 Vercel — EVE (Agent Framework)
The Pivot: From web deployment platform to “Next.js for Agents”
EVE (launched June 2026): - Open-source framework (Apache 2.0) treating each agent as a directory - Described as “Next.js for agents” - Agents trigger 29% of deployments on Vercel (up from <3% a year ago) - Expected to reach 50% of deployments - Vercel runs 100+ agents internally on eve
Components: - AI Gateway for model provider fallbacks - Markdown-based system prompts - TypeScript tool files (filename becomes tool name) - MCP servers for external tool connections - Durable workflow execution (survives crashes) - Sandboxed code execution for security - Human approval gates for sensitive actions - OpenTelemetry tracing for observability
The Bigger Picture — “Agent Stack”: Vercel is building the deployment infrastructure for AaaS — the equivalent of what AWS was for SaaS.
3.6 Klarna — The Cautionary Tale
The Pivot: From human customer service to AI-first, then back to hybrid
What went wrong (May 2025): - Complex cases degraded CSAT - Hallucinations on edge cases (compliance risk in fintech) - “We replaced 700 agents” framing was misleading (they avoided hiring, didn’t fire) - Customers wanted humans for sensitive financial matters
The lesson: AI agents work for routine work, but full replacement is premature in regulated, high-trust contexts. The hybrid model — AI handles volume, humans handle complexity — is the practical near-term outcome.

5. IMPLICATIONS FOR CSOAI/MEOK
5.1 If SaaS Dies, What Does the Governance Stack Look Like?
Current governance assumes: - Humans use software through UIs - Actions are logged by applications - Compliance is enforced by app features - Audits review human actions in software
Agent-native governance requires: - Agents take actions autonomously - Every agent decision must be logged, auditable, explainable - Human-in-the-loop checkpoints for high-risk actions - Real-time monitoring of agent behavior (drift, bias, errors) - Cross-system audit trails (agents span multiple SaaS backends) - Attribution: “Who approved that agent decision?” must have a clear answer
