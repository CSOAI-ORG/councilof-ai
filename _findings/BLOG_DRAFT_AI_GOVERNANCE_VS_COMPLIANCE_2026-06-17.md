## The words are not interchangeable

AI governance and AI compliance are related, but they are not the same thing. Confusing them leads to buying the wrong tools and missing the wrong risks.

## AI governance

Governance is the ongoing system of oversight. It includes:

- Roles and responsibilities
- Decision rights and escalation paths
- Policies and principles
- Risk appetite
- Board and executive accountability
- Stakeholder engagement

Governance asks: *Who decides what is acceptable, and how do they decide it?*

## AI compliance

Compliance is the demonstration that specific obligations are met. It includes:

- Mapping systems to regulatory requirements
- Documenting controls and evidence
- Producing attestations and audit trails
- Responding to regulator requests
- Meeting deadlines like the EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligation

Compliance asks: *Can we prove we meet the rules that apply to us?*

## Why the distinction matters

You can have good governance and still fail compliance if you cannot produce evidence. You can pass a compliance check and still have bad governance if decisions are opaque or unaccountable.

Most organisations need both. But the tools are different.

## Governance without compliance is a talking shop

A well-written AI ethics policy is governance. It is necessary but not sufficient. When a regulator asks for technical documentation, risk management records, and human oversight evidence, the policy alone will not satisfy them.

## Compliance without governance is a checkbox exercise

A vendor that generates documentation without involving your risk owners, board, or operational teams creates compliance theatre. It might pass an audit today, but it will not survive a serious incident or a change in leadership.
