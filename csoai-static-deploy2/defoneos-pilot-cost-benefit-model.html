1 · Why a model

Sovereign procurement reviews are won on numbers, not ideals. Every MOD/Gov buyer asks: "What will this cost me for 30 days, and what is that cost relative to the US-cloud alternative?"

This page answers that question conservatively. Every line is open: vendors, assumptions, hardware and exit credits are listed and reproducible.

Important: These figures are a self-modelled estimate, not a competitive bid. They are intended to give procurement officers a first-pass orientation. Final figures for a real contract require a discovery meeting and a tailored BOM.

3 · The equivalent US-cloud stack

For each tier, the US-cloud alternative is the closest equivalent fully-loaded public-cloud stack:

  • T1 — AWS GovCloud + Claude API + Slack Enterprise + Okta.
  • T2 — Same + Azure AD + Splunk Cloud + PagerDuty + Datadog + CircleCI.
  • T3 — Same + dedicated workloads on dedicated tenancy + 24×7 SOC retainer.

These are the stacks DEFONEOS most directly displaces. The numbers below use published list price as the upper bound; negotiation typically lands 25–40% below.

8 · Exit credits

L11 — Exit credit — the only line that often surprises reviewers — is the appreciated transfer of the deployed artefacts to the next deployment or to the buyer's own permanent operation.

  • MacBooks retain 70–85% resale value over 12 months.
  • MCPs are exported as static-binary bundles, callable from any compatible substrate.
  • SIGIL ledger becomes a long-lived audit asset owned by the buyer.
  • BFT-witness review work becomes a transferable competency.

DEFONEOS is happy to not win the next deployment. The artefacts travel with the buyer.
