What is Article 86?

Article 86 of the EU AI Act grants affected persons the right to explanation of individual decision-making made by high-risk AI systems. When a high-risk AI system outputs a decision that produces legal effects or similarly significantly affects a person's health, safety, or fundamental rights, that person has the right to obtain:

  • Clear and meaningful explanation of the decision's output
  • The main elements of the decision logic — what factors were weighed
  • The rationale behind the decision as it applies to them specifically
  • The ability to contest the decision through a defined redress process

This right intersects with GDPR Article 22(3) (right to obtain human intervention, express one's point of view, and contest the decision) and Article 15 (right of access, including the logic of automated processing). DEFONEOS implements this through a structured, multi-tier explanation framework backed by the SIGIL chain's immutable audit trail.

5-Tier Explanation Framework

1
Tier 1 — Plain Language Summary

The decision is expressed in plain English at a reading age of 12. No technical terms. No acronyms. Example: "Your application for housing priority was ranked below 14 other households because your current accommodation meets the statutory minimum standard and your household size places you in Band C."

2
Tier 2 — Decision Factors

The specific factors that influenced the decision are listed with their individual contribution weights. Each factor is traceable to the source data and the governance rule that authorised its use. Example: "Factor: Household size (weight: 0.23). Source: Housing register entry dated 12 Jun 2026. Governance: Housing Allocation Policy §4.2."

3
Tier 3 — Decision Logic Trace

A step-by-step trace showing how the factors combined to produce the output. This includes the model's reasoning path, any human override points, and the BFT council vote if one was held. Each step carries a SIGIL hash for tamper-evidence.

4
Tier 4 — Counterfactual Explanation

What would have needed to be different for the decision to go the other way? This is generated automatically from the model's decision boundary analysis. Example: "If your household included a dependent under 16 with a registered medical need (Band A factor), your application would have been ranked in the top 5."

5
Tier 5 — Full Technical Audit Trail

Complete SIGIL chain entry with Ed25519 signature, model version, training data provenance, inference timestamp, all factor weights, confidence score, calibration metric, BFT vote tally, and human review logs. Available to regulators, independent auditors, and the data subject's legal representative on request.

Integration with DEFONEOS Architecture

Every DEFONEOS decision is logged to the SIGIL chain with the following fields, enabling instant explanation generation:

  • decision_id — Unique Ed25519-signed hash
  • model_version — Exact model checkpoint and training data provenance
  • factors — Dict of {factor_name: {value, weight, source, governance_rule}}
  • threshold — Decision boundary threshold
  • confidence — Calibration score (Brier score ≤ 0.15)
  • bft_vote — Council vote tally (if triggered) with per-voter rationale
  • human_reviewer — Identity of reviewer (if triggered) and decision
  • red_line_check — 7 red lines checked, all must be CLEAR
  • sigil_hash — Hash-chained to previous entry, tamper-evident

⚠️ Honesty Register

The explanation framework described here is the designed system. DEFONEOS has not been deployed in a live public-service context where affected persons have exercised their Article 86 right through this system. The 72h/14d/30d SLAs are design targets, not measured performance. The counterfactual generation algorithm is implemented as a prototype but has not been validated against the EU AI Office's forthcoming guidance on "meaningful explanations." The BFT council escalation (Step 6) is functional in the sovereign substrate but has not been tested with a live redress case. The "plain language at reading age 12" claim is a design specification, not a tested outcome — layperson UX testing is a documented human gate. No DPO has been formally designated for DEFONEOS under GDPR Art 37.
