Meeting objective

The validation meeting is not a product demo. It is a short evidence session that decides whether a 90-day sovereign pilot has a real route. The agenda keeps the conversation safe, useful and procurement-aware.

  • Validate the buyer problem in their words.
  • Validate the data boundary: public, synthetic, owner-approved, never classified in this public pilot.
  • Validate the evidence route: which artefacts answer governance, security, technical and commercial questions.
  • Validate acceptance criteria: what a pass looks like by Day 30, Day 60 and Day 90.
  • End with one owner, one next action and one date.

Technical validation questions

These questions keep the meeting grounded in acceptance criteria rather than platform hype.

  • What decision would this pilot help you make in 90 days?
  • Which evidence do you need first: governance, security, data flow, price, route, or demo?
  • What data is permissible for a public-source validation?
  • Which system boundary must we not cross without NDA or formal accreditation?
  • Who owns the acceptance criteria?
  • What would cause the pilot to fail safely?
  • Which procurement or innovation route is realistic if the validation passes?

Post-call output

The meeting must produce a clean artefact within 24 hours.

  • A six-bullet meeting summary in buyer words.
  • One selected pilot route or an explicit no-route decision.
  • Draft acceptance criteria for Day 30, Day 60 and Day 90.
  • A link bundle of no more than three public artefacts.
  • A CRM update with signal score, proof object and next date.
  • A SoW draft only if the buyer confirmed route, owner and use case.
