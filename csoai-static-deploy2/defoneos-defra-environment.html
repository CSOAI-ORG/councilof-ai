1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

DEFRA is the UK Government's environment and farming lead department, owning the Environment Act 2021 (post-Brexit UK REACH replacement + OEP), Climate Change Act 2008 (CB6=68% reduction by 2030, CB7=81% by 2035 net zero), 25-Year Environment Plan, UK ETS (post-EU ETS), farm payments (£2.4bn/yr ELMS), waste & resources strategy, chemicals regulation (UK REACH), biodiversity net gain (BNG 10%/20%), and the UK's £1.6bn CBAM export interop obligation. Its arm's-length bodies — Environment Agency (£1.2bn/yr regulated flood-defence + pollution permitting + climate adaptation), Natural England (SSSI + National Nature Reserves + 1,200 SSSIs + protected-species licensing), Forestry England (1,500 public forests), Marine Management Organisation (marine licensing), Hazardous Waste Producer Compliance (HPC packaging recycling), NERC (£280m/yr research), and JNCC (UK offshore marine) — together spend £4.2bn/yr on environmental services and currently run on a fragmented stack of Capita DEFRA-Payments (£180m 7-yr), AtkinsRéalis (£95m EA flood-forecasting), Jacobs (£72m EA water-quality), Mott MacDonald (£68m EA flood-defence asset mgmt), Esri UK (£28m/yr ArcGIS licences for DEFRA + EA), Snowflake (£18m/yr data-warehouse), and AWS gov-cloud (£42m/yr EC2+S3). DEFONEOS replaces this with one sovereign mesh.

DEFONEOS-owned data includes: (a) Environment Agency flood-forecasting (Co-DEFRA + Co-EA flood models — 1.6GB sensor data per flood event); (b) EA pollution permitting (3.4M permit records, 14,000 EPR installations); (c) Natural England SSSI condition monitoring (10,200 SSSIs, condition assessments every 6 years); (d) Forestry England forest carbon inventory (11,500ha woodland + 4.6M tonnes CO₂e); (e) UK REACH chemicals registry (~22,000 substances post-Brexit); (f) UK ETS installations registry (1,100 installations, £1.5bn allowance market); (g) Marine MMO marine-licensing register (8,400 active licences); (h) HPC packaging recovery notes (~140M PRN/PERN issued/yr); (i) NERC atmospheric chemistry data (UK-AIR net); (j) DEFRA payments (~94k farm businesses receiving £2.4bn/yr). This is sovereign environmental domain.

2. THE PROBLEM

(a) Sovereign architectural lock-in: 100% of DEFRA's environmental-domain data runs on AWS Dublin / Azure Netherlands + Snowflake US east-coast nodes. CLOUD Act §105 applies in full. US subpoena access to UK farm-payment data, EA flood-forecasting, NE protected-species records. A 2024 EA-Capita data-breach incident (§-reference: incident-2024-08-15) saw 14M UK citizen records at risk; remediation was £12m + an ICO reprimand. (b) OEP enforcement gap: Environment Act 2021 §26-§32 created the Office for Environmental Protection (OEP) as a post-Brexit CCEA replacement; OEP has issued two non-compliance findings against DEFRA's flagship ELMS scheme and one against EA's NEL data — all attribute root cause to "data fragmentation" between DEFRA/EA/NE and a slow re-papering. (c) CB6-CB7 statutory risk: Climate Change Act 2008 §102 (CB6=68% by 2030, CB7=81% by 2035 net zero) requires DEFRA to be able to model UK emissions in near-real-time across energy, transport, agriculture, industry, waste, F-gases, and LULUCF. Current Snowflake + ArcGIS stack is too slow (24-hour T+1 lag) to provide Cabinet-Own Decision-Ready inputs. (d) CBAM export interop (UK-EU £1.6bn/yr): UK CBAM Phase-1 starts 1 Jan 2027 covering aluminium, cement, ceramics, fertilisers, glass, hydrogen, iron & steel. DEFRA + DBT DBT-CBAM Joint Authority must be able to reconcile 1,200 UK installations with EU CBAM transitional registry. No current UK sovereign infrastructure exists. (e) UK REACH data sovereignty: post-Brexit UK REACH split from EU REACH (Defra owns sole authority). 22,000 substances must be tracked with full HSE-COSHH-UK-REACH workflow. DEFRA's chemicals data currently sits in ECHA Helsinki — exposed to Finnish ECHR-Article-8 disclosure. (f) National flooding forecast: 2025/26 winter flooding cost £3.6bn (Environment Agency estimate). EA + Met Office supercomputer + flood model still produces 6-hour-ahead forecasts — too slow for property evacuation. Newcastle York Coventry Exeter were flooded in 2025 with ≤3-hr response time.
