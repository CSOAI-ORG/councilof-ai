1. The renewal conversation is a renewal conversation, not a sales pitch

Three rules that distinguish a renewal negotiation from a new sale:

  1. You already have evidence. The buyer has been running DEFONEOS for 90+ days. The negotiation is anchored in the QBR minutes, the 9-KPI dashboard, the renewal-readiness score, and the ROI report — not in marketing collateral.
  2. The buyer's champion has political capital. The champion chose DEFONEOS and is on the hook internally. The renewal conversation must protect that capital, not exhaust it.
  3. The cost of switching is the buyer's strongest lever. The AE should not argue against switching costs; the AE should acknowledge them and offer the multi-year trade (price certainty + roadmap visibility) in exchange.

3. Discovery memo (P1) — the 1-page artefact

The discovery memo is the AE's working document for the renewal conversation. It is NOT shared with the buyer. It is internal AE-to-CS-lead-to-CRO context-setting.

  1. Buyer's stated renewal intent — what did the executive sponsor say in the Day-85 letter?
  2. Buyer's unstated needs — what did the buyer ask about that wasn't in the original SoW? (Hint: read the QBR action register.)
  3. Internal politics — is there a competing vendor in the buyer's evaluation? (Always assume yes.)
  4. Budget cycle — when does the buyer's procurement window open? Calendar it.
  5. Renewal-readiness score — and the tier (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW / AT RISK).
  6. Named risks — what could derail this renewal? Name each one with a probability and a mitigation.
  7. Target deal shape — multi-year horizon, expansion options, price floor, trade matrix.

6. Discount ladder mechanics

The discount ladder is symmetric and pre-committed. The AE does not negotiate the discount — the AE offers one of the four deal shapes and the buyer chooses. No bespoke discounting.

  1. 5% discount — 2-year commitment only.
  2. 10% discount — 3-year commitment + one named expansion workload.
  3. 15% discount — 3-year commitment + two named expansion workloads + reference agreement.
  4. Anything beyond 15% — requires CRO sign-off + CEO sign-off; expected < 1% of deals.

Why no bespoke discounting? Because the buyer will ask. The answer is: "Our discount ladder is symmetric across our entire customer base; any deal-specific discount would disadvantage your peers and undermine the trust we have built in the QBR." This is true and important.

7. Expansion workload — what counts

The expansion workload trade is the single most important commercial lever in the multi-year deal. It commits the buyer to growing the DEFONEOS footprint, which in turn anchors the multi-year discount.

A valid expansion workload must meet all three criteria:

  1. Named — a specific workload identified by the buyer, with a specific owner.
  2. Scoped — the workload has a 1-page scope agreed by both parties at the renewal QBR.
  3. Dated — the workload has a target go-live date within 12 months of renewal signature.

Examples of valid expansion workloads:

  • "Move HR records processing onto DEFONEOS by Q3."
  • "Add the second business unit (200 users) to DEFONEOS by Q2."
  • "Onboard a new subsidiary (acquired last quarter) onto DEFONEOS by Q4."

8. Reference agreement — the asymmetric trade

The reference agreement is the highest-value trade because it is asymmetric: the buyer gives marketing capital (case study, quote, logo rights) in exchange for a 15% discount. The buyer doesn't have to do anything ongoing — the case study is a one-time artefact that CSOAI can use for 24 months.

Required reference deliverables:

  1. Written case study — 800-1200 words, co-authored, published on defoneos.org.
  2. Named quote — 1-2 sentences from the buyer's CIO or equivalent, attributable.
  3. Logo rights — buyer logo on defoneos.org (royalty-free, 24-month term).
  4. Reference call commitment — buyer agrees to participate in up to 6 reference calls per year with prospective buyers in similar verticals.

If the buyer cannot offer reference agreement (e.g. UK government buyers who cannot endorse a commercial supplier), the 15% discount is not available. The AE should walk the buyer to the 10% discount instead.

10. The five things the AE should never do

  1. Never discount outside the ladder. It sets precedent, it leaks to other customers via procurement networks, and it creates a worse deal than the buyer would have taken at the next tier.
  2. Never negotiate against the renewal-readiness score. The score is computed; either it is high or it is not. The AE should not promise to "fix" a low score — that is the CS lead's job.
  3. Never skip P1 (discovery). The AE who skips discovery and jumps to proposal ends up negotiating against an unknown requirement.
  4. Never bring up renewal in a QBR. The QBR is governance, not sales. Renewal is discussed at a separate meeting called by the AE.
  5. Never promise a roadmap commitment without checking with product. Roadmap commitments in writing are binding; verbal commitments are not.

12. Honesty register

  1. The 5/10/15% discount ladder is the published rate card; CSOAI does not run promotions or seasonal discounts.
  2. The 4 / 3.5 / 3% annual uplifts are calibrated against UK CPI plus a margin; buyers in higher-inflation jurisdictions should expect higher uplifts.
  3. The "no bespoke discounting" rule has one exception: sovereign buyers (UK MOD, NATO, AUKUS) who commit to a 5-year term may negotiate bespoke pricing — handled directly by CSOAI CEO.
  4. The reference agreement trade is not available to UK central government buyers (procurement policy precludes endorsement); commercial buyers and devolved administrations are eligible.
  5. The 24-month logo rights term is the standard; longer terms require CRO + buyer legal review.
