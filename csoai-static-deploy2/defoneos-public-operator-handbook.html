Public Operator Handbook

Eight bounded workflows for running a sovereign DEFONEOS instance — observe, authenticate, admit, route, respond, rotate, disclose and review.

1. Operator posture

DEFONEOS is operated as a governed substrate, not as an autonomous authority. The operator watches evidence, confirms identity, admits known artefacts, routes data through declared boundaries, and records decisions. The system may recommend a safe action, but a material action remains subject to the documented quorum, the human-owner boundary, and the immutable red lines.

This handbook is public because operational clarity is part of assurance. It explains the reusable workflow without exposing private keys, personal data, customer data, or NDA-only assets. It also makes the limits explicit: the public operator surface does not provide kinetic targeting, personal surveillance, face recognition, phone location, or individual tracking workflows.

  • Observe before acting.
  • Authenticate every principal and artefact.
  • Quarantine uncertainty rather than guessing.
  • Leave an auditable SIGIL receipt for every material transition.
Operating boundary: Evidence, owner authority and red-line safety are separate checks. A green technical probe never substitutes for a Nick-only action.

3. Start-of-shift procedure

At the start of each shift, the operator checks the canonical host, the local service health view, the most recent SIGIL receipt, clock synchronisation, certificate freshness, and the owner-gate queue. The operator does not copy secrets into a chat, paste customer data into an external model, or accept a green status from an unverified dashboard.

The shift record contains the timestamp, operator identity, deployment digest, current ring status, open incidents, expiring credentials, and any deferred action. A missing receipt is treated as a logging fault and investigated before material operations continue.

4. Identity, admission and trust rings

Every principal is treated as a typed identity: human, agent, MCP, model or data source. Admission requires provenance, manifest integrity, capability scope, certificate state and red-line scan. The three-ring model separates core sovereign controls, defence-adjacent sensors and civil/open data. Cross-ring traffic is mediated by the declared gateway; direct unmediated mixing is a policy failure.

A rejected artefact is not destroyed as a first response. It is quarantined, its evidence hash is recorded, and a human or council review decides whether the issue is a false positive, a repairable defect, or a permanent rejection. This preserves chain of custody and avoids hiding uncertainty.

5. Routing and monitoring

Routing is a policy decision, not an optimisation shortcut. The operator verifies the requester's ring, data classification, destination jurisdiction, tool capability and current health before allowing a route. If a route cannot satisfy the sovereignty or red-line policy, the correct result is a safe refusal with a SIGIL receipt.

Monitoring focuses on explainable deviations: request volume, error rate, provenance failures, certificate failures, admission changes, quorum anomalies, and unexpected data movement. A threshold crossing proposes a bounded response; it does not authorise an unsafe operation. Rate limiting, circuit breaking, quarantine and key rotation are the preferred containment vocabulary.

6. Incident response and human override

For an incident, the operator follows Detect, Triage, Contain, Eradicate, Recover, Post-Incident Review and Disclose. SEV1 events target five-minute acknowledgement and thirty-minute containment, subject to the actual deployment's staffing and evidence. BFT quorum gates the material system action; the human owner retains the final safe-state boundary and the ability to pause or override the system.

The public handbook does not describe offensive action. Containment means quarantine, credential revocation, route isolation, rate limiting, evidence capture, restoration from a known-good snapshot, and notification. The red-line policy is an explicit non-action boundary: no actor can turn an assurance workflow into a kinetic or personal-surveillance workflow.

7. Key, certificate and evidence rotation

Rotation is a normal operational workflow, not a panic ritual. The operator checks the secret class, its declared cadence, current witnesses, overlap window, dependent services and rollback reference. The new value is distributed through the internal custody mechanism, tested against the service, signed in a rotation receipt, and only then is the previous material destroyed under the declared quorum.

For certificate rotation, the internal CA hierarchy, end-entity validity, application pin and revocation path are checked together. For a failed rotation, retain the old material only for the documented overlap window, pause the route if necessary, and escalate rather than copying a secret into an unapproved channel.

8. Disclosure, closeout and review

Closeout is a control transition, not the deletion of an incident. The operator confirms containment, validates recovery, records the affected scope, preserves evidence, identifies notification obligations, and schedules a post-incident review. UK GDPR Article 33, contractual notification, NCSC expectations, JSP 440 handling and buyer-specific schedules must be mapped to the actual incident; the handbook does not replace legal advice or the owner's reporting duty.

The post-incident record includes the root cause, contributing factors, controls that worked, controls that failed, evidence gaps, red-line review, and corrective actions with owners and due dates. Public minutes may be redacted for legal or security reasons; the existence of the governance event should not be confused with public release of sensitive detail.

10. The anti-patterns this handbook defeats

  • **No blind green dashboard.** A status colour without a receipt is an observation to verify, not a permission to act.
  • **No shared credentials.** Human, agent and MCP identities remain distinct and scoped.
  • **No direct ring crossing.** Route through the declared gateway or reject the request.
  • **No external secret paste.** Keep keys, personal data and customer evidence inside the approved sovereign custody path.
  • **No quorum theatre.** Record actual witnesses and thresholds; do not imply a DEFONEOS-SEAL vote from a page publication.
  • **No offensive drift.** Assurance, resilience and public-service operation do not become kinetic targeting or personal surveillance.

11. Operator next actions

A new operator should read the evidence index, run the start-of-shift checks, and perform a non-production admission and rotation rehearsal. A buyer should choose one workflow for a pilot and agree its acceptance test. An owner should review the Nick-only queue before any external submission or credential issuance.

The handbook is successful when a second operator can follow it without guessing which action is allowed, which action is gated, and which evidence must be preserved. That is the standard for the next DEFONEOS expansion tick.
