What is Article 27?

Article 27 of the EU AI Act requires deployers of high-risk AI systems (public bodies and private entities providing services of public interest) to conduct a Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA) before putting a high-risk AI system into use. The FRIA must evaluate:

  • The specific context of use and the persons or groups likely to be affected
  • The specific risks of harm to fundamental rights that may emerge
  • The mitigation measures applied — including governance, safeguards, and human oversight
  • The consultation process with affected groups, where feasible
  • The reasoning for deploying the system despite identified risks

For DEFONEOS, the FRIA is not just a compliance document — it is the foundation of the entire rights-by-design architecture. Every red line, every oversight mechanism, every explanation tier, and every BFT governance rule traces back to a FRIA finding.

5-Phase FRIA Methodology

1
Phase 1 — Scope & Context Definition

Define the AI system's intended purpose, deployment context, affected populations, and decision categories. Identify which fundamental rights are engaged. For DEFONEOS public services: housing allocation, benefits assessment, child safeguarding triage, health prioritisation, and immigration casework.

2
Phase 2 — Rights Mapping & Impact Identification

Systematically evaluate the AI system's impact on each of the 12 fundamental rights domains. For each right, assess likelihood and severity of harm, considering both direct impacts (the decision itself) and indirect impacts (accumulated effects, chilling effects, discriminatory outcomes).

Phase 3 — Risk Scoring & Severity Assessment

Each identified impact is scored using a Likelihood × Severity matrix. The scoring methodology is aligned with ISO 14971 (risk management for medical devices) and the EU AI Act's risk-based approach.

5
Phase 5 — Consultation, Publication & Review

The FRIA must be informed by consultation with affected groups and relevant authorities. DEFONEOS requires:

  • 30-day public consultation — Draft FRIA published, feedback collected from civil society, affected communities, and equality bodies
  • Regulator pre-notification — FRIA submitted to relevant MSA / DPA 90 days before deployment
  • Equality Impact Assessment — Public Sector Equality Duty (s.149 Equality Act 2010) integrated into FRIA
  • Children's Rights Impact Assessment — UNCRC-aligned assessment when children are affected
  • Data Protection Impact Assessment — GDPR Art 35 DPIA conducted in parallel
  • Publication — Final FRIA published in plain language summary + full technical version
  • Periodic review — FRIA refreshed every 12 months or upon material system change

Integration with DEFONEOS Architecture

The FRIA is not a one-time document. It is a living governance instrument that is continuously informed by the DEFONEOS operational stack:

  • SIGIL chain → Feeds FRIA review with real-world impact data
  • Bias monitoring → Updates risk scores with measured fairness metrics
  • Incident response → Triggers immediate FRIA review on SERIOUS+ incident
  • Right to explanation → FRIA defines which decisions require explanation tiers
  • Red line enforcement → FRIA defines what constitutes a red line breach
  • BFT council → FRIA acceptance requires quorum vote; review changes require re-vote

⚠️ Honesty Register

The FRIA methodology described here is the designed framework. No live FRIA has been conducted for a real DEFONEOS deployment — there is no live deployment. The 12-rights register, the 4-level risk scoring, and the 10 mitigation categories are design artefacts, not validated outcomes from real-world consultation. The 30-day public consultation has not occurred. No national competent authority has been notified. No civil society feedback has been collected. The integration between FRIA and the operational stack (SIGIL chain, bias monitoring, incident response) is architecturally designed but has not been tested with real impact data. The claim that "FRIA is the foundation of the rights-by-design architecture" is aspirational — the architecture was designed first, and the FRIA framework was built to formalise and document the rights protections that the architecture already provides. A genuinely rights-led process would have started with the FRIA and designed the system around it — DEFONEOS did both simultaneously, which is defensible but should be acknowledged. No DPO has been formally designated. No equality body has been consulted.
