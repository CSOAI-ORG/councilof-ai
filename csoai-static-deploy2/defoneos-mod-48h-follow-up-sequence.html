48-hour operating principle

A first MOD or public-sector conversation is not won by sending a large deck. It is won by returning the buyer to the exact problem they named, with a small proof object and a clean next step.

  • One thread per buyer. Do not split evidence across side channels.
  • One ask per email. The buyer should be able to reply with yes, no, or send this to my colleague.
  • Every claim must link to a live page, a control table, or an explicitly marked draft.
  • If the buyer mentions security, send the evidence room. If they mention budget, send the pricing card. If they mention pilot, send the SoW converter.
  • If the buyer says not now, move to a 30-day useful update cadence and stop the pursuit language.

Reply classification

Classify every reply before sending anything else. This prevents the owner from over-talking after a useful first signal.

  • GREEN: buyer asks for date, route, price, security detail, procurement vehicle, or internal stakeholder version.
  • AMBER: buyer asks for a deck, says interesting, or asks to circle back without date.
  • RED: buyer says not a fit, no budget, no route, no authority, or procurement restriction.
  • UNKNOWN: out-of-office, generic acknowledgment, or auto-response. Do not score as interest.

Boundary rules

These rules keep the pursuit sovereign, useful, and non-spammy.

  • Maximum four follow-ups over 90 days unless the buyer engages.
  • Never imply MOD, NATO, AUKUS, DAIC, DASA, or Dstl endorsement.
  • Never send sensitive technical detail that would belong behind an NDA.
  • Never add personal data to the CRM beyond role, public email, organisation, and consented meeting notes.
  • Never use fear language. Use public-service resilience language.
