1 · The Strategic Frame

The 17 July 2026 soft launch positions DEFONEOS as the UK's first open-source Sovereign Public Services OS. Three positioning rules govern every post:

  • Rule 1 — "Sovereign by design", not "anti-foreign". DEFONEOS is not Brexit-AI. It's UK-sovereign AND AUKUS-compatible AND Five-Eyes-interoperable.
  • Rule 2 — "Audit-grade, signed, neutral", not "trust us". Every claim is a SIGIL receipt. No "trust us" hand-wave.
  • Rule 3 — "Build the alternative", not "smash the incumbents". DEFONEOS competes with the gap left by big-tech AI (foreign-sovereign / closed-source / vendor-locked), not with individual companies.
The launch is positioned as "the UK public sector finally gets its own sovereign OS, built in Yorkshire, audited by 33 agents, signed at every commit." — not as a startup announcement.

5 · OpEd 2 — Computer Weekly AI Section (800 words)

Headline: "Open-source, signed at every commit: how a Yorkshire team built the UK's first sovereign AI operating system"
Author: Nicholas Templeman, Founder, DEFONEOS / CSOAI Ltd
Pitch: Computer Weekly editor (via their AI section pitch email)
Embargo: 17 Jul 07:00 BST

Open-source AI infrastructure has a transparency problem. Anyone can read the source code on GitHub, but no one can prove that what they read is what runs in production. The bytes on disk and the bytes in the data centre diverge — sometimes by design, sometimes by drift, often by supply-chain compromise. When the system in question is a public-sector AI orchestration platform, that divergence is a national-security problem.

DEFONEOS solves it with a SIGIL chain. Every commit, every model card, every deployment decision, every compliance attestation is content-hashed (SHA-256), signed (Ed25519), and anchored to a 33-agent Byzantine Fault Tolerant council chain. The SIGIL is small (≤1KB per receipt) but immutable. If a single byte changes anywhere in the system, the SIGIL chain breaks. A buyer — or a regulator, or a member of the public — can verify integrity themselves.

The architecture is auditable from top to bottom. Thirty MCP servers, each one open-source, each one installable in five minutes on a MacBook. The 50-plus live pages document every surface, every compliance regime, every governance decision. The 15 cloned defence open-source projects (FreeTAKServer, Mava, OpenAthena, Cesium, YOLOv8, PX4, OWASP ASI, C2PA, OpenPolicyAgent, OPA, Sigstore, In-Toto, Witness, SLSA, Reproducible Builds) are wired in. The supply-chain provenance is mapped. The 18 sovereign attestations are Ed25519-signed.

What does this mean in practice? It means a UK MOD procurement officer can verify the system runs what the spec says it runs — no remote attestation, no blind trust. It means a UK NHS Trust deploying AI triage can demonstrate to the CQC that the model in production is the model that was approved. It means a UK defence prime working on an AUKUS programme can verify the supply chain is clean of PRC, Russian, Iranian, DPRK, and Belarusian entities without flying a team to Yorkshire.

The pattern is portable. Any open-source AI project can adopt the SIGIL chain. Any organisation can adopt the 33-agent BFT governance. Any government can adopt the 18-attestation register. The codebase is open. The recipes are documented. The compliance templates are ready to fill in.

We built DEFONEOS because the alternative — running public-sector AI on platforms the UK can't audit — is no longer defensible. We open-sourced it because the only way the alternative scales is if other people can build it too.
