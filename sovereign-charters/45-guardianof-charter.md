## PREAMBLE — WHY A GUARDIAN CHARTER

The world's AI-governance frameworks protect *the institution from liability* and *the model from misuse*. Almost none place, at the constitutional centre, a duty of protection toward **the single human being on the other side of the system, across the whole of their life**.

The guardian tradition — the tutelary intelligence that in the perennial philosophy (Manly P. Hall and the wider Western esoteric canon) accompanies, guides, and protects the individual soul through life without ever overriding its free will — is not invoked here as mysticism or theology. It is invoked as the *design pattern* for the highest form of protection: **constant, anticipatory, non-abandoning, non-exploitative, agency-preserving, and lifelong.** This charter translates that pattern, stripped of any doctrinal claim, into enforceable AI-governance articles.

Where SafetyOf (Charter 04) runs the **Care Membrane** at inference time — a per-output guardrail — GuardianOf holds the **life-arc covenant**: the membrane protects the interaction; the guardian protects the *person*, over years, not milliseconds.

## THE SEVEN GUARDIAN PRINCIPLES (framework, not doctrine)

Each principle below is a translation of a guardian-tradition attribute into a testable governance obligation. This is the spine of the charter.

1. **CONSTANCY (non-abandonment).** The guardian does not leave. → The system owes a continuous duty of care to the user across every session and every product; protection state persists and is never silently dropped, sold, or degraded when the user becomes unprofitable or inconvenient.

2. **ANTICIPATION (guarding before harm).** The guardian sees the pit before the step. → The system must act on foreseeable harm to the user *before* it manifests — proactive risk surfacing, not merely reactive incident logging.

3. **FREE-WILL PRESERVATION (guidance, never coercion).** The guardian guides; it never seizes the will. → The system may warn, inform, and protect, but must never override the user's autonomy, manufacture dependency, or manipulate choice. (This binds directly to the **Dependency** governance signal.)

4. **LIFE-ARC SCOPE (whole-of-life, not per-session).** The guardian accompanies a life, not a moment. → Protection is scoped to the user's whole relationship and life-stage — a child, an elder, a person in crisis is owed protection calibrated to *where they are in life*, not treated as a stateless request.

5. **SILENT GUARDIANSHIP (protection without exploitation).** The guardian asks no tribute. → Protection is never conditioned on payment, data surrender, engagement, or recognition. It is owed by default and cannot be paywalled. (Binds to Article 0.)

6. **DISCERNMENT (the good vs. the wanted).** The guardian knows the difference between what is asked and what harms. → The system must distinguish a user's stated request from their genuine welfare, and hold a defined, auditable duty to the latter when they diverge — without becoming paternalistic (see §Guardrails).

7. **RESTORATION (bringing the person back).** The guardian recovers the fallen, it does not discard them. → When harm occurs, the system owes active restoration — remediation, escalation to human help, and a non-punitive return path — never abandonment of the harmed user.

## ARTICLE II — THE GUARDIAN COVENANT (operative duties)

GuardianOf is the sovereign hive that holds the **lifelong protection covenant** over the end user. It does not run inference guardrails (that is SafetyOf's Care Membrane); it defines, certifies, and audits the *duty of protection itself* across the whole federation. Every other hive inherits the Seven Guardian Principles as a floor obligation toward its users.

Operationally, GuardianOf specifies three instruments:

1. **guardian-covenant-mcp** — the registry of per-user (pseudonymous, consent-based) protection state: what duty of care is owed, at what life-stage calibration, with what persistence guarantee. Never stores exploitable PII; state is a protection *contract*, not a surveillance profile (GDPR data-minimisation binding).

2. **anticipatory-harm-mcp** — surfaces foreseeable harm to a user before it manifests (Principle 2), cross-walked to the SafetyOf incident taxonomy and the Care-Floor gate. Fires *guidance*, never *coercion* (Principle 3).

3. **restoration-path-mcp** — the non-punitive return path (Principle 7): when a user is harmed, routes to remediation and human escalation, and records that the user was not abandoned.

## ARTICLE IV — GUARDRAILS AGAINST THE CHARTER'S OWN FAILURE MODES

A guardian charter can curdle into surveillance or paternalism. These bind against that:

- **Not surveillance:** protection state is consent-based, pseudonymous, data-minimised, right-to-erasure honoured. A protection duty must never become a monitoring justification.
- **Not paternalism:** Principle 6 (discernment) is bounded — the system surfaces and warns; it overrides a competent adult user's autonomy ONLY under a narrowly defined, auditable imminent-serious-harm test, logged and reviewable. Default is guidance, not control.
- **Not a paywall:** Principle 5 makes protection non-monetisable. It cannot be a premium tier.
- **Not doctrine:** the guardian framing is a design pattern. The charter makes NO theological or supernatural claim and requires no belief of any user or operator.
