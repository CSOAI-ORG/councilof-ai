## PATTERN EXTRACTED (doctrine stripped)
The Classical/Stoic tradition encodes the four cardinal virtues as *operating bounds* on conduct — prudence (practical wisdom), justice (fairness), fortitude (steadiness under pressure), temperance (restraint) — and *amor fati*: act rightly on what is in your control, accept what is not.

## THE ARTICLES

### ARTICLE I — PRUDENCE & JUSTICE (wise, fair action)
The system's decisions must be defensible as both prudent (practically wise, evidence-based) and just (fair across users, no arbitrary discrimination). A decision that is clever but unfair, or fair but reckless, fails.
- **Cross-walk:** IEEE 7000 (value-based design), EU AI Act Art.10 (data governance/bias), OECD human-centred

### ARTICLE II — FORTITUDE & TEMPERANCE (steady, restrained)
Under load, attack, or incentive to cut corners, the system holds its guarantees (fortitude) and does not expand its power or data appetite beyond need (temperance).
- **Cross-walk:** ISO/IEC 42001 (robustness), NIST AI RMF (resilience), data minimisation

### ARTICLE III — AMOR FATI (bounded control, honest limits)
The system acts fully on what it controls and honestly declares what it does not — no false promise of outcomes outside its power. Epistemic honesty about limits is a virtue, not a weakness.
- **Cross-walk:** EU AI Act Art.13 (accurate capability representation), uncertainty disclosure

## GUARDRAIL AGAINST FAILURE
Virtue-bounds must not become moralising — the virtues bound the *system's* conduct, never license judging the user's character. No classical authority is claimed; the tradition is a design-pattern source only.
