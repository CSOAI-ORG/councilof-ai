## ARTICLE II — INDUSTRY DOMAIN & MARKET

### II.A — Industry Scope

EthicalGovernanceOf is the sovereign ethical governance hive — the conscience of the AI ecosystem. Where SafetyOf monitors for immediate harm and AccountabilityOf traces decisions, EthicalGovernanceOf operates upstream: ensuring AI systems are designed, built, and deployed according to defined ethical values before they ever touch a user. This is governance-by-design, not governance-as-afterthought.

EthicalGovernanceOf operates six core MCP systems that form the ethical backbone of sovereign AI deployment:

**(1) meok-governance-engine-mcp** — the central governance orchestration engine. This MCP server implements a comprehensive ethical governance framework spanning 14 ethical dimensions: fairness, transparency, accountability, privacy, safety, human autonomy, explainability, non-maleficence, beneficence, justice, dignity, solidarity, sustainability, and responsibility. For each dimension, the governance engine provides: measurement protocols (quantitative metrics), threshold definitions (acceptable/unacceptable ranges), automated compliance checking against 30+ regulatory frameworks, and integration with the BFT council for ethical escalation decisions. The governance engine ingests AI system metadata (model architecture, training data provenance, intended use, deployment context) and produces a structured Ethical Governance Assessment (EGA) report with Ed25519 signature.

**(2) care-membrane-mcp** — the ethical guardrail fabric co-operated with SafetyOf. The care membrane enforces value-aligned behaviour boundaries at inference time, checking every AI output against 847 ethical safety signals across 23 categories. Beyond SafetyOf's harm-prevention focus, EthicalGovernanceOf configures the care membrane for positive ethical alignment: ensuring outputs reflect the values encoded in the system's Ethics Policy (dignity-respecting language, culturally-aware responses, power-balanced interactions). The care membrane supports configurable ethical profiles — a medical AI's ethical membrane differs from a financial AI's, which differs from an educational AI's. Profile authoring is itself governed by the BFT council to prevent unilateral value imposition.

**(3) ai-bom-mcp** — the AI Bill of Materials (AI BOM) generator and verifier. Borrowing from software supply chain security's SBOM concept, the AI BOM catalogues every component of an AI system: model architecture (type, layers, parameters), training data provenance (sources, licenses, collection methods, demographic composition), fine-tuning history (datasets, methods, epochs), dependency chain (libraries, frameworks, base models), deployment configuration (hardware, scaling, API surface), and human oversight mechanisms. The AI BOM is the ethical equivalent of an ingredient list — without it, ethical governance is impossible because you cannot govern what you cannot see. Every AI BOM is Ed25519-signed, SIGIL-chained, and cross-referenced against the CSRD/ESRS supply chain disclosure requirements.

**(4) explainability-report-mcp** — the sovereign explainability engine. When an AI system makes a decision — approves a loan, diagnoses a condition, recommends a sentence — ethical governance demands explanation. Not the superficial "the model predicted X" but causal, counterfactual, contrastive explanation: "The model predicted loan denial because factor Y was 2.3 standard deviations above threshold; if factor Y were reduced by 15%, the decision would reverse; this factor has historically shown disparate impact on protected group Z." The explainability MCP generates multi-method explanations (SHAP, LIME, Integrated Gradients, counterfactual, anchor) with plain-language summaries and confidence scores. Every explanation is Ed25519-signed and linked to the AI BOM for full provenance.

**(5) a2a-governance-bridge-mcp** — the inter-agent ethical governance layer. As autonomous AI agents negotiate, trade, and collaborate through A2A protocols, ethical governance must span agent boundaries. The A2A governance bridge enforces ethical constraints on inter-agent communications: an agent representing a vulnerable population cannot be coerced by a more powerful agent, an agent with access to sensitive data cannot share that data without ethical clearance, and multi-agent decisions must reflect diverse ethical perspectives. The bridge logs every A2A interaction with ethical metadata, enabling post-hoc ethical audit of agent ecosystems.

**(6) csrd-compliance-mcp** — the Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive compliance engine. The EU's CSRD/ESRS mandates detailed reporting on AI ethics, supply chain due diligence, and social impact for large undertakings. The CSRD compliance MCP automates: double materiality assessment (financial materiality + impact materiality) for AI systems, ESRS S1-S4 social impact reporting (workforce, value chain workers, affected communities, consumers/end-users), ESRS G1 business conduct reporting (AI ethics policies, whistleblower mechanisms, political engagement), and ESRS E1-E5 environmental reporting (AI training energy consumption, e-waste, water usage). Every CSRD report is Ed25519-signed with full SIGIL chain provenance.

EthicalGovernanceOf's mission: **no AI system in the sovereign ecosystem operates without a defined, enforced, and audited ethical framework.** Ethics is not a feature — it is the foundation. The governance engine, care membrane, AI BOM, explainability engine, A2A bridge, and CSRD compliance tooling form a six-pillar ethical architecture that makes ethics operational, measurable, and enforceable.

### II.C — Current State of the Industry

AI ethics is trapped in the "principles-to-nowhere" pattern. Organisations publish AI ethics principles (Google's "Don't Be Evil" era, Microsoft's "Responsible AI," Meta's "Five Pillars") but lack the technical infrastructure to operationalise them. The result is ethics-as-PR: principles exist on websites, not in code.

Specific industry failures: (1) **Ethics are documented, not enforced** — AI ethics policies are PDF documents with no connection to the AI system's runtime behaviour. A model can violate every ethical principle and no alert fires. (2) **No value alignment infrastructure** — RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) is the dominant alignment technique, but it encodes the values of the crowdworkers (typically English-speaking, college-educated, Western) as universal values. There is no mechanism for domain-specific, culturally-aware, community-governed value alignment. (3) **AI supply chains are opaque** — most AI deployers cannot answer basic questions: what training data was used? What are the demographic distributions? What licenses apply? What dependencies exist? This opacity makes ethical governance impossible. (4) **Explanations are optional and non-standardised** — no regulatory body mandates specific explanation formats, so AI deployers provide whatever explanation is cheapest, not what is most informative. (5) **CSRD/ESRS compliance is treated as an annual report-writing exercise** — not as continuous governance infrastructure.

EthicalGovernanceOf fixes all five with a governance engine that connects ethics policies to runtime enforcement, an AI BOM that makes supply chains transparent, an explainability engine that produces cryptographic-grade explanations, and CSRD compliance that is continuous, not annual. This is the operational ethics infrastructure the AI industry has been missing.
