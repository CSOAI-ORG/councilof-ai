### UK & EU Regulatory Framework
**UK Research Integrity Office** (UKRIO) — Code of Practice for Research (3 principles: honesty, rigour, transparency, accountability across individual/institutional/publisher axes). **Concordat to Support Research Integrity** (Universities UK 2012, refreshed 2019) — five commitments: maintain highest standards, enforce with rigour, use transparent processes, foster learning, share best practice. **UKRI Open Access Policy** (1 April 2021 — full implementation) — all peer-reviewed research articles arising from UKRI funding must be made immediately open access, with no embargo, under CC-BY public copyright licence (CC-BY 4.0 for most journals, CC-BY-SA 4.0 for some). Three compliant routes:
- **Gold OA** — published in fully OA journal (DOAJ indexed), APC funded from UKRI (£150m annual pot from 2013, ~£10m/year across UK since 2021 allocations)
- **Green OA** — author-archive after publisher embargo (zero or 12-month for STEM, sometimes longer for humanities); UKRI preference for zero embargo starting 2024
- **Diamond OA** — no APC, no subscription; community-supported journals (e.g., Sociological Science, Discrete Mathematics, Mind publications); funded by institutional membership / library consortia / learned society endowments
**Zero-embargo Rights Retention Strategy (RRS)**: since 2021, UKRI grant-holders can assert CC-BY licence on the manuscript of any journal-submitted article pre-review via a clause in the grant agreement, allowing green OA self-archiving of the AAM (author-accepted manuscript) irrespective of publisher later embargo. Sample text: *"This research was funded, in whole or in part, by UK Research and Innovation. For the purpose of Open Access, the author has applied a CC BY public copyright licence to any Author Accepted Manuscript version arising from this submission."*

**Horizon Europe Ethics Guidelines** (Article 19, Annex III) — ethics self-assessment mandatory for all proposals; human subjects (+EU data sharing: GDPR compliant), human cells/tissues (informed consent), animals (3R compliance), non-EU countries (Helsinki-equivalent protection), environment/biodiversity (Nagoya Protocol ABS), dual-use (EU 2021/821 export control). **Declaration of Helsinki** (2013 revision) — 37 paragraphs covering: risks/benefits, study design review, volunteer recruitment, informed consent, placebo use, post-trial access, data sharing, COI disclosure, vulnerable populations.

### International Open Science Standards
**Plan S (cOAlition S, 2018-)** — 10 principles, full implementation 1 July 2021 for new funder-supported grants: (1) all scholarly publications resulting from research funded by public grants must be fully and immediately open; (2) authors retain copyright under CC-BY 4.0; (3) authors or funders can submit metadata to repositories; (4) recommended open repositories; (5) review of funded research must be open or hybrid; (6) authors own reuse rights; (7) no embargo; (8) hybrid journals not compliant (transformative agreements sunset 2024); (9) journal flips to full OA supported; (10) Member funders support costs on a fair basis. **2022 update**: cOAlition S verified Transformative Journal agreements sunset 31 December 2024 — partial OA per-paper now non-compliant.

**COPE** (Committee on Publication Ethics) — code of conduct for journal editors (4 sections: editing decisions, peer review, author relations, publishing ethics), retraction guidelines, post-publication discussion (PubPeer), research integrity audit templates. **FAIR Data Principles** (Wilkinson et al. 2016, Scientific Data) — F = Findable (DOI, metadata, indexed); A = Accessible (standardised, authenticated protocols); I = Interoperable (vocabularies, qualified references); R = Reusable (clear licences, provenance, community standards).

### Registered Reports Landscape (as of 2026)
The Registered Report (RR) format — peer review happens before data collection, accepted-in-principle reduces publication bias. **Foundational papers**: Chambers 2013 on Registered Reports; Nosek & Lakens 2014 — pilot at Cortex. **Currently offered at 300+ journals**, including high-impact venues: Nature Human Behaviour (2020+), PLOS Biology (Reginato et al.), eLife (institutional RR with preregistered-reward mechanism), Royal Society Open Science, BMJ Open (replication studies), AIMS Neuroscience, BMC Medicine, Cortex, Collabra Psychology, Communications Psychology, Journal of Cognition, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, Psychological Science (2-year trial ending 2026), PNAS (institutional incentive), PRS, Research Synthesis Methods, Royal Society, Royal Society Interface, Scientific Reports, Social Psychological and Personality Science.

**Foundational RR types**: Stage 1 = hypothesis and methods, no data, peer review 2-4 weeks. If accepted in principle ("Stage 1 IPA"), data collection or analysis proceeds regardless of result. Stage 2 = full study including results, peer-reviewed against Stage 1. Final publication guaranteed regardless of result. Variants: **Stage 1 Registered** (data collection complete but analysis pipeline pre-registered), **Stage 2 Registered** (full final preregistration including analysis).

### Replication Studies Findings (evidence base)
The replication crisis is now quantified by major coordinated efforts:

- **Open Science Collaboration (2015)** "Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science": 100 studies from 2008 Psychological Science, 270+ coauthors. Result: 36% replicated by significance; 47% by subjective confidence; consistent with publication bias + QRP. Effect size r = 0.22 between original and replication effect sizes (when original < new studies); original effect size shrunk by 50% on average (non-trivial shrinkage).
- **Many Labs 2** (Klein et al. 2014 → 2018 PNAS): 28 classic psychology findings (anchoring, ego depletion, facial feedback, mortality salience), 125 samples worldwide, 50,000+ participants, 220+ coauthors. Replication: 50% of effects had replicable sign AND significantly replicated; 36% gave effects that were statistically "large" by meta-analytic standards. Effect-size meta-analytic correlations tightest for cognitive priming effects (R + 0.91), looser for self-report (R ≈ 0.40).
- **Many Labs 3** (Ebersole et al. 2016 → 2020 PNAS Cognitive Self-Inflation): 89% replication of cognitive self-inflation; large sample (n=17,000+, 90 labs), tighter replication due to adequate power.
- **Many Labs 4** (Coles et al. 2022 PNAS Replication of Facial Feedback Hypothesis): n=1,934 × 6 labs (US, EU, AU), weak support for facial feedback (Vasopressin vs Anesthesia IX — failed).
- **Cancer Biology Reproducibility Project (2015, ongoing)** — Begley & Ellis (2012 Nature) "Raise standards for preclinical cancer research" — estimated 90% of preclinical cancer findings do not replicate. RR replication of 30 papers: ~50% replicated (Errington et al. 2021 eLife).
- **Machine Learning reproducibility** (Sculley et al. 2018 ML hype + McDermott et al. 2021 ML reproducibility evaluation) — 60-70% reproducibility for ICLR/NeurIPS 2018-2020.
- **Social Sciences Replication Project (SSRP)** — failed 12/21 studies, ~half subject to deferred-discussion.

### Statistical Fraud Detection Tools (technical detail)
- **GRIM test** (Brown & Heathers 2016 Research Integrity and Peer Review): checks if reported means are consistent with the underlying integer data (e.g., if mean = 6.00 from n=58, the sum must be 348, integer — many reported means fail this simple check). ~67% of psychology papers surveyed had at least one GRIM test failure on a reported mean. "GRIM.SPRITE.R" R package — Heathers (2018) implementation.
- **SPRITE test** (Heathers 2018): analogous check for reported standard deviations — checks if the standard deviation is consistent with reported mean + sample size. Useful for catching impossible variance.
- **statcheck** (Nuijten, Hartgerink, van Assen, Epskamp, Wicherts 2016 — Behavior Research Methods): an OCR + natural-language processor that scans journal articles for reported p-values and tests them against the reported test statistics. Used to verify 50% of psychology papers contain statistical inconsistencies. Nuijten et al. (2017) found >70% of papers in social psychology contain at least one statistical reporting error (with ~12% of p-values unverifiable, and ~1% definitely inconsistent).
- **p-curve / z-curve** (Simonsohn et al. 2014, Schimmack 2021): p-curve analyzes the distribution of p-values to differentiate evidential value (right-skew → real effect), p-hacking (flat distribution), and fraud (left-skew — impossibly many p-values just below .05). z-curve extends with rejection rates and overall "evidential value" scoring.
- **RPP (R-Index for Detecting Questionable Research Practices)** (Schimmack & Carlsson 2019): high R-Index = high "diagnosticity" = many significant results + minimal bias. R-Index ≈ 50% across surveyed social psychology journals, indicating pervasive QRP.
- **Calin-Jageman Caldwell (2019)** "Single Paper fraud Detector" (SPfD): composes GRIM + SPRITE + check-digit analysis into a single tool. rPP (reproducibility-protocol package).
- **Suspiciousness Calculator** (Forensic Statistics, Heathers): Bayesian estimation of the probability of fraud given evidence, vs the probability of the evidence under H0.
- **Doublet detection**: identify data points repeated within dataset (for one participant across supposedly independent questionnaires), via fingerprint hashing (Lynam & Miller 2021).
- **Image forensics**: Adobe Error Level Analysis (ELA), Forensically (16+ tools — clone, Shearlet, gradient analysis), Image Twin (commercial), ForensicIRIS for medical imaging.
- **Forensic meta-analytic tools**: **RPP package** (Schimmack), **metafor** R package (Viechtbauer), **statcheck R package**, **GRIMMER** (extension of GRIM to larger data structures — 2020, Heathers).

### AI-Generated Research Detection (current tools)
- **GPT-2 Output Detector** (OpenAI 2019): original detector, 87% accuracy on long outputs (>50 tokens), drops to ~50% on text passages after paraphrasing or with prompts. Deprecated by OpenAI 2023 due to high false-positive rate.
- **GLTR (Giant Language model Test Room)** (Gehrmann, Strobelt, Rush 2019): highlights words that are likely vs unlikely under a GPT-2 trained model. AI text uses very common words with very narrow distributions; human text uses more rare, lower-ranked words. Visual highlighting.
- **GPTZero** (Tian 2023+): perplexity score (low perplexity → likely AI) + burstiness (variance in sentence perplexity — AI is uniform, human varies). Claims 98% accuracy on long-form academic text.
- **ZeroGPT** (Edward Tian equivalent): 0-100% AI score based on perplexity + burstiness.
- **Originality.ai**: commercial (paid), 0-100 score with high accuracy on hybrid human + AI text.
- **Copyleaks AI Content Detector**: cross-language, claims 99% accuracy.
- **Perplexity scoring (general)**: lower perplexity score against GPT-2/3/4-base → more likely AI-generated. Threshold varies, usually <60 flags AI on academic prose.
- **Stylometric analysis** (Brennan, Afroz, Greenstadt 2012): classic "Stylometry" — function word distributions, sentence length distributions, syntactic complexity. AI text exhibits "mode collapse" around central stylistic distributions.
- **Water-marking** (Scott Aaronson-style post-hoc): SynthID (Google DeepMind, 2023) embedded in generated text via token sampling, machine-readable. C2PA / C2P digital provenance.
- **Citation verification** (Day 2023, Nature 624:435): ChatGPT generates references with 100% confident fact-patterns — DOIs are wrong 10-15% of the time, author names are wrong 18-20% of the time. Crossref DOI verification (curl https://api.crossref.org/works/{DOI}) is reliable automated check.
- **Inconsistency in claims across the paper**: ChatGPT/Hard prompt → AI text uses very consistent hedging ("suggest", "indicate", "exhibit"), absent or minor inconsistency about "I" vs impersonal — mismatch with rest of section. Authors' earlier papers are non-AI, recent papers AI? Discontinuity is detection signal.
