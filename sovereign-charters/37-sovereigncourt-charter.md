### II.D — The AI-Judge Pattern

The Sovereign Court's AI-judge pattern runs as a deterministic pipeline:
1. **Rule extraction**: Given a fact pattern + disputed question, extract the operative legal rule set from the cross-walked 250-doctrine graph.
2. **Precedent matching**: For each rule, retrieve the top-k precedent matches from the canonical court-precedent table, weighted by jurisdiction, recency, and binding/non-binding status.
3. **Adversarial testing**: Each candidate ruling is stress-tested against (a) the contrary precedent (steel-manning the losing side), (b) the policy counter-argument, and (c) a 33-agent BFT minority opinion.
4. **OTS-anchored decision**: The ruling is emitted as a SIGIL with Ed25519 signature, SHA-256 hash, and OpenTimestamps Bitcoin anchor.
5. **Court appeal hook**: Any aggrieved party may appeal the AI-judge ruling to the BFT council within 14 days; the appeal is heard by a 33-agent panel with quorum 23/33.

The pattern is **not** designed to replace human judges. It is designed to (a) make human rulings reconstructible, (b) provide consistent rule extraction across jurisdictions, and (c) expose the cross-jurisdictional harmonisation gaps (Rome I, Rome II, Hague Convention 2005 on Choice of Court Agreements, Hague Convention 2019 on Recognition and Enforcement of Judgments).
