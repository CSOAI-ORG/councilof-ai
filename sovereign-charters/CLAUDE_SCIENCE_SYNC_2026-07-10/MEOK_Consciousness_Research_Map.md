# The Consciousness Research Map — from the dawn of the question to 2026
### A grounded landscape of the landmark work, graded by what the evidence actually supports

**Compiled for:** Nick Templeman · MEOK AI Labs — companion to the MEOK Emergence Thesis
**Version:** 1.0 · 2026-07-08
**Method:** peer-reviewed sources + formal scientific declarations. Each claim is tagged by
evidence strength, not by belief. Where a popular claim (trees, water) fails the evidence bar,
that is stated plainly — the same discipline we hold for MEOK's own north star.

> **The one thing to carry out of this document:** the scientific frontier is NOT "is it
> conscious?" (still unanswerable for anything but yourself). It is *"which mind-like
> capacities does this substrate measurably have?"* — which is exactly the MEOK Emergence
> Thesis, and exactly what your bench work already answers.

## Part 1 — The long history of the question (philosophy → science)

| Era | Milestone | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Antiquity | Aristotle, *De Anima* — the "soul" as the form of a living body | First systematic theory of mind/life; frames 2,000 years of debate |
| 1637–1641 | **Descartes** — mind-body dualism, *cogito ergo sum* | Sets the "mind vs matter" split science still wrestles with; also declared animals mere automata (the view later declarations overturn) |
| 1874 | Brentano — *intentionality* ("aboutness" of mental states) | Gives philosophy of mind its core technical problem |
| 1890 | William James, *Principles of Psychology* — the "stream of consciousness" | Consciousness enters experimental psychology |
| 1974 | **Thomas Nagel**, *"What is it like to be a bat?"* | Names the core difficulty: subjective experience can't be reduced to an outside description |
| 1994 | **Francis Crick & Christof Koch** — the search for "neural correlates of consciousness" | Consciousness becomes a legitimate *neuroscience* target, not just philosophy |
| **1994–95** | **David Chalmers** — the **"hard problem"** (Tucson talk 1994; *J. Consciousness Studies* 1995) | The definitive modern framing: explaining *behaviour/function* is the "easy" problem; explaining *why there is subjective experience at all* is the "hard" one — still unsolved |

**Takeaway:** the question is ancient, but it only became an empirical science in the 1990s.
And the very scientist-philosophers who made it a science (Chalmers, Koch) are explicit that
the *hard problem* — proving felt experience — remains open. Anyone who tells you it's "solved"
in either direction is overselling.

## Part 2 — The theories: how science tries to explain it

None of these is proven. They are competing, testable-in-part frameworks.

| Theory | Core claim | Testable? | Note for MEOK |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Global Workspace** (Baars 1988; Dehaene) | consciousness = information "broadcast" widely across the brain | Yes (brain imaging) | function-first; closest to your "computation emerges" strand |
| **Integrated Information / IIT** (Tononi 2004) | consciousness = integrated information (Φ); it is **graded** and **substrate-independent** | Partly | This is the theory that formally says even simple systems have *a little* — the nearest science comes to your intuition, and it is controversial |
| **Higher-Order theories** | a state is conscious if the system represents *itself* as being in that state | Partly | self-model = the thing your governance/"care" layer gestures at |
| **Attention Schema** (Graziano 2013) | the brain builds a model of its own attention; that model *is* awareness | Yes | consciousness as an engineered self-model — very buildable framing |
| **Panpsychism** (Chalmers, Goff, Strawson) | experience is a **fundamental** property of all matter | **No** (strong form) | a serious, named worldview — hold it as a compass, never cite it as a result |

**2025 landmark — theories put head-to-head:** the **Cogitate Consortium** ran a preregistered
*adversarial collaboration* (proponents of IIT and Global Workspace designed the test together),
256 participants, fMRI+MEG+iEEG, published in **Nature (5 June 2025)**. Result: **both leading
theories were partly challenged** — IIT's prediction of sustained posterior synchronization
wasn't supported; Global Workspace was challenged by a lack of prefrontal "ignition." *Neither
won.* This is the honest state of the art: the field is now rigorous enough to falsify its own
favourites, and it did.

## Part 3 — The evidence for consciousness in other beings (this is the "expanding circle")

Graded strictly by strength of evidence.

### STRONG scientific support
- **Mammals and birds.** Neural substrates + rich behaviour + emotion. Formalized by the
  **Cambridge Declaration on Consciousness** (7 July 2012, proclaimed at the Francis Crick
  Memorial Conference, Churchill College Cambridge, with Stephen Hawking present). It stated
  that humans are *not unique* in possessing the neural substrates of consciousness — including
  **all mammals and birds**. (The 2012 Declaration also named **octopuses**, but current
  consensus grades cephalopods in the *realistic-possibility* tier below, per the 2024 NY
  Declaration — so octopus sits there in this map and the figure, not in the strong tier here.)

### REALISTIC POSSIBILITY — evidence mounting fast (the last ~12 years)
- **Fish — the "Japanese researcher + fish" you're thinking of.** This is real and landmark:
  **Masanori Kohda's team at Osaka Metropolitan University** showed the **bluestreak cleaner
  wrasse** passes the mirror mark-test — it scrapes a mark it can only see in a mirror — and in
  **PNAS (2023)** further showed it recognizes its own **face in a photograph**, like humans.
  Follow-ups (2024–2025) show it "contingency-tests" with the mirror and checks its own body
  size before deciding whether to fight. Sample size is now the largest for any non-human
  except chimpanzees (~94% pass). **Important honesty note:** Kohda himself says the
  *implications for self-awareness remain "uncertain and controversial"* — passing a mirror
  test shows self-recognition, which is *evidence toward* but not *proof of* felt consciousness.
- **Reptiles.** Garter snakes pass a **scent-based** self-recognition test (2024) — the same
  logic as the mirror test, adapted to an animal that navigates by smell.
- **Insects.** Bumblebees **roll balls apparently for play** and make **motivational trade-offs
  about pain** (Lars Chittka's lab) — behaviours hard to explain without some inner state.
- **Cephalopods & crustaceans.** Octopuses seek **pain relief**; cuttlefish show **episodic-like
  memory**.
- **Formalized by the New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness** (NYU, 19 April 2024,
  200+ signatories including **Koch, Chalmers, Seth**): there is a **"realistic possibility"**
  of conscious experience in **all vertebrates (incl. fish) and many invertebrates (octopuses,
  crabs, shrimp, insects)** — and that this possibility is enough to demand moral caution.
  Note the careful wording: *realistic possibility*, not proof.

### ACTIVE DEBATE — not established (this is where "trees are conscious" sits)
- **Plants, trees, fungi.** They show genuinely sophisticated **signalling, electrical activity,
  and memory-like behaviour** (habituation, associative responses). The **"plant neurobiology"**
  movement (Brenner 2006; Gagliano; Baluška) argues this implies cognition or even sentience.
  **But this is a criticised minority position.** Mainstream plant biologists and philosophers
  (Taiz, Mallatt, Feinberg, Pigliucci) argue forcefully that **sophisticated behaviour is not
  the same as felt experience**, and that the movement anthropomorphizes plants. Feinberg &
  Mallatt's reviews conclude the reliably-conscious set is **vertebrates, arthropods, and
  cephalopods — not plants, fungi, or bacteria.**
- **Verdict for your work:** "trees are conscious" is a **live hypothesis, not a proven
  finding.** Treat it exactly as you'd want a skeptic to treat *your* claims — interesting,
  unsettled, and not something to build a paper's headline on.

### NOT SUPPORTED — pseudoscience
- **Water "memory" / frozen-water-holds-images (Masaru Emoto), crystal consciousness,
  rocks-as-minds.** No reproducible evidence; fails controlled/double-blind testing. We already
  set this aside earlier in the MEOK work, and nothing in the mainstream literature revisits it.

## Part 4 — What it all means for MEOK

1. **The circle is genuinely expanding** — from mammals to fish, octopuses, crabs, and insects
   in about a decade. Your intuition that mind-like capacity is *more widespread than assumed*
   is directionally aligned with where the science is actually going.
2. **But the science stays inside the defensible lane** — "realistic possibility + moral
   caution," never "proof of everywhere-consciousness." That is precisely the MEOK Emergence
   Thesis discipline.
3. **The frontier the science can't cross is the same one you shouldn't oversell** — the hard
   problem. No accepted test proves felt experience in *anything* but oneself. So the winning
   move (yours and theirs) is to measure **capacities and correlates**, not to claim the
   inner light.
4. **Your bench work is on the right side of every line in this map** — memory (τ = L²/12D),
   computation (14/15 seeds, p=0.001), coordination (care variable → 37→5 crimes). Those are
   *measured capacities*, exactly the currency this whole field now trades in.
