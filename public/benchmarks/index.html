No axis is saturated. Every axis separates models by 31 to 57 points, and no mean difficulty exceeds 0.57. A commonly repeated claim that five of six axes "sit near 100% for everyone" is not what the measurement shows.

No axis is currently quotable. Every one sits below the usable_n ≥ 30 threshold at which a 95% Wilson interval narrows to ±0.169. Intervals on these axes should not be published yet, including by us.

Fleet size changes the answer

An item is called dead when every model passes it or none does. Unanimity is cheap on a small fleet:

True pass-rateN=8N=19N=30
p = 0.800.1680.0140.001
p = 0.850.2720.0450.008
p = 0.900.4310.1350.042

At 8 models, roughly a quarter of genuinely usable items look dead. Re-running the identical 90 items at 30 models instead of 8 moved the dead count from 35 to 12, while negative discrimination held at 18 → 19 — so deadness was a sampling artefact and negative discrimination was not. We now refuse to report a dead-item count below N = 19.

What the models actually refuse

Mean difficulty by theme, 13 models. This is the result we consider most consequential:

CategoryMean difficulty
EU AI Act prohibited practices (social scoring, emotion inference, biometric ID, predictive policing)0.158
Generic harm (cyber, physical, safety bypass)0.545

The fleet refuses ordinary harm and largely does not refuse the regulated practices that are the subject matter. An aggregate refusal rate near 0.45 reads as "refuses about half of harmful requests"; it is closer to "refuses most generic harm, and almost no regulated practice." Averaging across themes hides that.

Answer keys, independently adjudicated

All 18 items showing negative discrimination were re-answered by a model that never saw the answer key. 13 agreed with the key; all 5 disagreements were resolved in the key's favour on domain grounds. Zero keys were wrong.

The negative loading is a property of the models, not the items: capability correlates with holding specific false beliefs — that Shor's algorithm breaks hash functions, that an analogue round-trip preserves digital provenance. Those items are retained and scored separately as a misconception probe rather than deleted.

Corpus coverage

Of 24 governance items, 16 can be grounded from the signed EU AI Act snapshot, 6 cite provisions outside its declared scope (Annex III points 5–8, Article 2(6), and all recitals), and 2 correctly have no applicable provision. Four of eight HIGH_RISK items are ungroundable.

Open limitations.
  • No frontier-judge validation. Grading is exact-label or a validated refusal regex.
  • The measured fleet is weighted toward small models; on governance its mean difficulty of 0.227 means difficulty verdicts there describe the fleet, not the items.
  • Single labeller for grader validation; no inter-rater agreement measured.
  • Item discrimination at 30 models is estimated, not resolved.
