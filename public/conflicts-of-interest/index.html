Conflicts of interest

Every state we publish about another organisation is decided by a person. This page says who, what they hold, what they are excluded from, and how a conflict is recorded when it appears.

Rule. No one may adjudicate a claim, a measurement or a complaint concerning an organisation that employs them, funds them, or that they advise. Where that applies, the case is reassigned and both the conflict and the reassignment are recorded here.

The register

The authoritative record is machine-readable: /governance/conflicts-register.json. It uses the same status vocabulary as the committee registry:

StatusMeans
VERIFIEDAppointment or declaration confirmed from a record we can point to.
DECLAREDDeclared by the person, not yet independently confirmed.
OPEN_SLOTThe role exists and is unfilled. Published as unfilled rather than quietly omitted.
RECUSEDHolds a relevant interest and is excluded from the decision.
SUPERSEDEDReplaced by a later entry.
Current status, stated plainly. Adjudication roles are OPEN_SLOT: no independent adjudicator has been appointed and no declaration has been collected. The register is published in that state rather than left blank, because "we have not appointed anyone" and "we have appointed someone with no conflicts" are different facts and only one of them is true.

What must be declared

  • Employment and office — current employer, directorships, and any role in a standards body, alliance or committee that we measure or report on.
  • Financial interests — equity, options, or fees from an organisation whose claims or measurements you adjudicate.
  • Funding — grants, sponsorship or gifts received by you or your organisation from a subject.
  • Advocacy — paid or unpaid advisory roles, expert-witness work, or public campaigning for or against a subject.
  • Personal — close family relationships with anyone in the categories above.

Declare what a reasonable reader would want to know. The test is not "does this legally disqualify me" but "would a subject be surprised to learn it after the decision".

When declarations are made

EventDeadline
On appointmentBefore the first decision
AnnuallyWithin 30 days of the anniversary of appointment
On changeWithin 10 working days of the change
Per caseAt the start of any adjudication, a case-specific check

How a conflict is handled

  1. The person declares it, in writing, to the register.
  2. The case is reassigned to someone with no relevant interest; the reassignment is recorded with a reason.
  3. If no unconflicted adjudicator is available, the decision is deferred and published as DEFERRED — no unconflicted adjudicator. It is not decided by the conflicted person.
  4. The declaration stays on the register for as long as the interest exists, and is retained after it ends.

What we do not treat as a conflict

  • Having read, cited or criticised an organisation's public work.
  • Being a member of a standards body we report on, provided the role is declared.
  • Being measured by us yourself — though you then cannot adjudicate your own result.

Raise a concern

If you believe an undisclosed interest affected a decision, use the complaints route and mark it independence. It is handled by someone with no involvement in the original decision, and the outcome appears in the quarterly aggregate like every other complaint.
