Research note · tokenised assets

Pontes launched. What can an outside reader check?

Evidence checked 24 September 2026 · Council of AI · Claim boundaries (JSON) · Source receipt (JSON)

An announcement can establish a programme's status. It does not establish that a particular instrument settled, that its backing is verified, or that a separate jurisdiction has authorised it.

What the named sources say

The European Central Bank announced Pontes' launch on 21 September. The ECB says it enables wholesale transactions in tokenised assets to settle in central bank money and that an initial group of market participants and DLT operators completed onboarding and was ready to start using it. That is a Eurosystem announcement. It is not, by itself, a public record of one instrument's completed asset and cash legs.

On 22 September, TAS and BlockInvest said their first joint use case was in the Pontes pilot phase. Their release says the institutional partner and project details will follow. The statement supplies a testable next question; it does not yet supply a named instrument or transaction for independent replay.

The UK has a separate evidence boundary. The FCA and Bank of England Digital Securities Sandbox provides staged testing and live-activity routes for eligible firms. The FCA's tokenised-gold Call for Input closes on 23 October. Neither page makes an ECB pilot into UK authorisation or verifies the reserves, custody, title and redemption rights of a named gold token.

Current state:

Pontes launch and the TAS–BlockInvest pilot statement are publisher statements observed. A named Pontes settlement and a named UK gold-token permission or reserve chain remain unmeasured here. Missing public evidence is neither a pass nor an accusation.

Six controls for one named instrument

  1. Identity: exact issuer, instrument, platform and identifier, plus the organisation making the claim.
  2. Authority and time: the primary source that applies to that claim, its jurisdiction and its effective date.
  3. Asset and cash legs: a public record linking issuance or transfer to the claimed settlement path. Onboarding alone is not a transaction.
  4. Reserves and custody: dated backing, custodian, auditor, legal-rights and redemption documents, with issuer assertions separated from independent corroboration.
  5. Negative control: an identifier mismatch, stale reserve statement, unavailable source or absent transaction that would contradict the claim.
  6. Uncertainty: a field-by-field PASS, FAIL or UNCHECKABLE result, preserving inaccessible evidence as inaccessible.

Council of AI's living measurement board contains bounded financial fact instruments and a separately signed effect-binding run. Those observations cover their stated subjects; they are not a Pontes transaction sample or a UK regulatory determination.
