The headline

Machine-readable marking (Art. 50(2)): 2 of 15 sites pass — one Article-50 toolkit vendor and our own. None of the AI-governance, assurance or evaluation vendors carries real synthetic-content marking on its own home site; several that ship AI-assistant surfaces show no disclosure marker in scanned HTML. The companies selling AI-transparency compliance are, on this measurement, not yet demonstrably doing the transparency the law requires.

Method & honesty register

  • Probe v0 is a deterministic heuristic over one page per site (home page), static HTML. It is measurement observation, not certification, grading or sealing.
  • “ABSENT” means checked, none found in scanned HTML — not a legal finding. JS-mounted widgets and client-side markers can evade a static scan.
  • “PARTIAL” on 50(2) means JSON-LD structured data only, without synthetic-content marking.
  • Every verdict in the raw dataset carries its evidence snippet or the explicit note “checked, none found”.
  • We measured ourselves with the same probe and publish our row alongside everyone else’s. Neutrality includes us.
  • No prices appear anywhere on this page: we state mechanism and scope only.

Article 50 probe sweep — frequently asked questions

  • How many AI-governance vendors actually comply with Article 50(2) machine-readable marking? On 8 October 2026, a deterministic probe of 15 AI-governance, assurance and AI-tooling vendor websites found machine-readable marking (Article 50(2)) on just 2 of 15 sites — one Article-50 toolkit vendor and CSOAI's own site, measured with the same probe and published alongside everyone else's. Full evidence per verdict is in the open dataset.
  • Does Article 50(2) require C2PA content credentials? No. Article 50(2) requires outputs to be machine-readable and detectable as AI-generated; it does not name a specific standard. C2PA content credentials are the leading technical approach alongside watermarking and metadata methods — the legal test is machine-readable detectability, not any one technology.
  • When did the EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations start to apply? Article 50 applies in full from 2 August 2026, with enforcement by the AI Office and national authorities starting the same day. This probe sweep (8 October 2026) measures the state of the live web after that date.
  • Is this certification or a compliance guarantee? No. This is independent measurement with evidence per verdict — never certification, grading or sealing. Probe v0 is a deterministic heuristic over the static home page of each site; ABSENT means checked and none found in scanned HTML, not a legal finding.
  • Did CSOAI measure itself in the sweep? Yes. We ran the same probe against our own site and publish our row in the same table as every other vendor — councilof.ai passed 50(1), 50(2) and 50(4) under the same deterministic checks. Neutrality includes us.
  • Where can I get the raw data from the Article 50 probe sweep? Every verdict with its evidence snippet is published as open JSON at councilof.ai/interop/art50-probe-sweep-2026-10-08.json, and the same sweep is mirrored as an open dataset on Kaggle (nicktempleman/art50-transparency-probe-sweep).
