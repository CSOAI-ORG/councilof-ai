What Is the Code of Practice?

The EU AI Act Code of Practice is a detailed framework developed by the AI Office to translate the high-level obligations of the Act into practical, verifiable commitments for providers and deployers of general-purpose AI models. While not directly legally binding, it serves as the primary reference for demonstrating compliance — and following its measures creates a presumption of compliance with corresponding Act obligations.

Why this matters for deployers: The Code of Practice bridges the gap between "what the Act says" and "what you actually need to do." If you follow the Code, you have a strong regulatory defence. If you don't, you bear the burden of proving alternative measures are equivalent.

Who It Affects

  • Providers of general-purpose AI models (GPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.)
  • Deployers using these models to generate content for end users
  • Distributors making AI-generated content publicly available
  • Importers bringing AI-generated content into the EU market
  • All entities in the AI value chain touching content generation

What Changed in the 2nd Draft

The 2nd draft represents a significant evolution from the 1st draft. The AI Office has responded to industry feedback, tightening requirements in key areas while providing more technical specificity. Here are the most important changes for deployers.

🔵 NEW — Specific Technical Standards Reference
The 2nd draft now explicitly references C2PA 2.1 and W3C Web Annotation as recognised formats for machine-readable content marking. This removes ambiguity about what counts as "adequate" technical marking. Deployers should target C2PA compliance as the gold standard.

🟡 CHANGED — Deployer Verification Obligations
Expanded obligations for deployers to verify that downstream content marking remains intact through distribution chains. Deployers can no longer pass responsibility upstream to providers — they must actively check and maintain markings.

🟢 NEW — Transparency Report Templates
Detailed templates for transparency reports are now specified, including required sections on: content types generated, marking methods used, verification procedures, exception usage, and compliance metrics. MEOK Pro includes automated transparency report generation.

🟡 CHANGED — Multi-Modal Content Handling
Clarified that content containing multiple modalities (e.g., a video with AI-generated audio track) requires separate marking for each modality. A single "AI-generated" label on the container is not sufficient — each synthetic element must be individually marked.

🔴 KEY — Copyright & Opt-Out Requirements
New section on copyright compliance requires deployers to respect machine-readable opt-out signals (e.g., robots.txt, ai.txt) when sourcing training data. While this primarily affects providers, deployers using fine-tuned models must ensure their training pipelines respect opt-out signals.

🔵 NEW — Synthetic Content Scope Expansion
The definition of "synthetic content" has been expanded to explicitly include AI-generated code, data synthesis outputs, and structured data, not just human-readable media. This broadens the scope for deployers in technical and data-heavy domains.

What This Means for Deployers

1. Transparency Is Your Responsibility

The 2nd draft makes unequivocally clear that deployers cannot outsource transparency compliance to model providers. Even if your AI provider does not offer built-in content labelling, you must implement it yourself at the point of output delivery to end users.

2. Technical Standards Are Now Mandatory

With C2PA and W3C standards now explicitly referenced, deploying non-standard marking methods carries significant regulatory risk. Adopting recognised standards like C2PA is the safest path to compliance. MEOK's C2PA integration is built for exactly this purpose.

3. Documentation Requirements Are Real

The transparency report templates signal that regulators expect comprehensive, auditable documentation — not just technical measures. Deployers need systems that automatically log compliance activities, generate reports, and maintain audit trails. Manual approaches will not scale.
