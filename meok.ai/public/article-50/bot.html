Article 50(1) — Human Interaction

Bot Interaction Disclosure

When an AI system interacts with a human, it must clearly disclose its artificial nature. Requirements for chatbots, voice assistants, and AI avatars under EU AI Act Article 50.

Audit Your AI Interactions → View Scenarios

The Core Obligation

Article 50(1) of the EU AI Act states: "Deployers of an AI system that interacts with natural persons shall inform those persons that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious from the circumstances and context of use."

Key principle: The burden is on the deployer to ensure disclosure is given. If there is any doubt about whether a user would reasonably recognise they are interacting with AI, disclosure must be made. The "obvious from context" exception is narrowly construed and should not be relied upon without careful assessment.

This obligation covers every AI system that communicates with humans in a conversational, assistive, or interactive capacity — whether through text, voice, visual interface, or any combination of modalities. The disclosure must be clear, timely (at the start of the interaction), and unambiguous.

Interactive Scenarios

Three primary interaction modes, each with specific disclosure requirements under Article 50.

💬

Chatbot

Text-based conversational AI across web, messaging, and app interfaces.

  • First-message disclosure: "I'm an AI assistant"
  • Persistent "AI" badge near the chat interface
  • Disclosure in initial system prompt visible to user
  • API response header: X-AI-Interaction: true
  • No misleading human names or personas
🎤

Voice

AI voice systems including phone bots, voice assistants, and smart speakers.

  • Verbal disclosure at call start: "This call is with an AI system"
  • Disclosure before any substantive conversation
  • Language-matched to the user's spoken language
  • Audio watermark embedding for verification
  • Recording/transcript audit of disclosure delivery
👤

Avatar

Digital humans, virtual agents, and AI-generated on-screen personas.

  • Persistent "AI" label visible near the avatar
  • Visual badge that cannot be hidden or minimised
  • Initial verbal disclosure if the avatar speaks
  • No human name, photo, or deceptive persona
  • Machine-readable marker in streaming metadata

Implementation Requirements

Timing & Placement

  • Disclosure must occur before any substantive interaction begins
  • For chatbots: first message must clearly state AI nature
  • For voice: disclosure at call connection before menu or conversation
  • For avatars: visible label before any communication
  • Disclosure must remain available throughout the interaction

Content of Disclosure

  • Clear phrasing: "You are interacting with an AI system"
  • No euphemisms, jargon, or ambiguous language
  • Language appropriate for the end user
  • Font size and contrast accessible (WCAG 2.1 AA)
  • Audio disclosure volume and clarity sufficient for comprehension

Forbidden Practices

  • Using human names, photos, or biographical details for AI agents
  • Claiming or implying the system can feel emotions or consciousness
  • Failing to disclose AI nature even when asked directly
  • Burying disclosure in terms of service or privacy notices
  • Using context that could mislead (e.g. "Ask me anything" without AI label)

Audit & Compliance

  • All interaction disclosures must be logged with timestamps
  • Records retained for regulatory inspection (minimum 3 years)
  • Disclosure delivery verification (was it spoken/displayed?)
  • User acknowledgment capture where appropriate
  • Regular compliance testing of disclosure systems

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the bot disclosure requirement under Article 50?

Article 50(1) requires that deployers of AI systems that interact with natural persons must inform those persons that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is obvious from the circumstances and context of use. This applies to chatbots, voice assistants, virtual agents, conversational AI, and any system that presents itself in a human-interactive capacity.

When does an AI system need to disclose it is AI during interaction?

Disclosure must occur at the start of the interaction, before the human user engages substantively with the system. For chatbots, this means the first message should clearly state the AI nature. For voice assistants, an initial verbal disclosure is required. For avatars, persistent visual indicators should be present. The disclosure must be clear, unambiguous, and in a language the user understands.

Are there exceptions to the bot disclosure requirement?

Article 50(1) provides an exception where it is "obvious from the circumstances and context of use" that the interaction is with an AI system. For example, a user calling a clearly named customer service hotline called 'AIVoice' in a context where AI-only service is expected may not require additional disclosure. However, this exception is narrowly interpreted — when in doubt, disclose. Deployers bear the burden of proving the context made AI interaction obvious.

What are the requirements for AI voice systems?

AI voice systems (including phone bots, voice assistants, and conversational IVR) must provide a verbal disclosure at the beginning of any interaction. The disclosure should be in the same language as the conversation, clearly stating the AI nature of the system. For outbound calls, the disclosure must occur before any substantive conversation. Recording and audit capabilities should capture that the disclosure was made.

What about AI avatars or digital humans?

AI avatars, digital humans, and virtual agents must be clearly identified as AI throughout the interaction. This includes: a persistent visual indicator (e.g. 'AI' badge or label near the avatar), an initial verbal disclosure if the avatar speaks, and machine-readable metadata in the underlying communication protocol. The avatar should not use a human name, photo, or persona that could mislead users about its artificial nature.
