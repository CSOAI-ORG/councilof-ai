The Compliance Cliff

Enforcement Date
2 August 2026
From this date, all deployers of AI systems that generate or manipulate image, audio, or video content must have deepfake disclosure measures in place. Non-compliance means fines of up to €15 million or 3% of annual turnover.

What Must Be Disclosed

  • AI-generated video that resembles real people or events
  • AI-manipulated audio (voice clones, altered statements)
  • Synthetic images that appear photographic or documentary
  • AI-generated scenes mimicking real locations or objects
  • Any content falsely appearing authentic or truthful

Who Must Disclose

  • Deployers distributing AI-generated content to EU end users
  • Social media platforms hosting AI-manipulated media
  • News and media organisations publishing synthetic content
  • Advertising and marketing teams using AI-generated assets
  • Any entity making deepfake content publicly available

Legal Requirements in Detail

Article 50(3) — Deepfake Disclosure

"Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deepfake shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated."

The disclosure must be:

  • Prominent — clearly visible or audible, not hidden in terms of service or fine print
  • Persistent — survives content sharing, copying, and basic transformations
  • Accessible — understandable to the average end user, including those with disabilities
  • Context-appropriate — proportionate to the content type and distribution channel
  • Non-deceptive — not undermined by contradictory messaging or design choices

Article 50(4) — Content Manipulation Disclosure

"Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates text published with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest shall disclose that the text has been artificially generated or manipulated."

This extends beyond deepfakes to any AI-generated text used in public-interest communications, including press releases, government announcements, news articles, and public notices.

Note on exceptions: The EU AI Act provides limited exceptions for content that is clearly artistic, satirical, or fictional where the AI generation is obvious from context, and for authorised law enforcement activities. Deployers should not rely on exceptions without legal advice — the burden of proof rests on the deployer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Article 50 say about deepfakes?

Article 50(3) specifically requires that any AI-generated or manipulated image, audio, or video content that appreciably resembles real persons, objects, places, or events (a "deepfake") must be clearly disclosed as artificially generated or manipulated. The disclosure must be prominent, persistent, and accessible to the end user before they engage with the content. This applies regardless of whether the content is harmful or benign.

When does the deepfake disclosure requirement become enforceable?

The deepfake and AI-generated content transparency obligations under Article 50 become enforceable from 2 August 2026. This is the key compliance cliff for deployers. After this date, any deployer distributing AI-generated or manipulated content without proper labelling faces penalties of up to €15 million or 3% of annual worldwide turnover.

What content qualifies as a deepfake under the EU AI Act?

Under Article 50, a deepfake is AI-generated or manipulated image, audio, or video content that appreciably resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities, or events. The key criterion is that it would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful. This covers synthetic media, face swaps, voice clones, manipulated footage, and AI-generated scenes that mimic real locations or events.

Are there any exceptions to the deepfake disclosure requirement?

Yes. Exceptions include: content that is clearly artistic, creative, or satirical in nature where the AI generation is obvious from context; content that has undergone substantial human editorial review and control; content lawfully created or processed for the purpose of preventing or detecting crime; and content where labelling would be technically impossible after reasonable efforts. However, these exceptions are narrowly construed and deployers bear the burden of proof.

What are the penalties for failing to disclose deepfakes?

Non-compliance with Article 50 deepfake disclosure obligations can result in administrative fines of up to €15 million or 3% of the undertaking's total annual worldwide turnover, whichever is higher. Regulators can also issue public warnings, order content removal, require corrective disclosures, and impose daily penalty payments for continuing violations.
