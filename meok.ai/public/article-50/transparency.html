What Article 50 Requires

Article 50 of the EU AI Act establishes a comprehensive transparency framework for AI-generated and manipulated content. The core obligation is simple: any output created or modified by an AI system must be clearly identifiable as such — both to humans and to automated systems.

Key deadline: The transparency obligations for deployers of AI systems that generate or manipulate content become enforceable from 2 August 2026. Providers of general-purpose AI models must ensure their systems support downstream compliance from the date the model is placed on the market.

Scope of Coverage

  • AI-generated text (articles, marketing copy, reports)
  • AI-generated images and graphics (including synthetic media)
  • AI-generated audio (voice synthesis, music, sound effects)
  • AI-generated video (deepfakes, synthetic footage, animation)
  • AI-manipulated content (edited photos, altered recordings)
  • AI-system-to-human interactions (chatbots, voice assistants)

Who Must Comply

  • Providers of general-purpose AI models that generate content
  • Deployers who use AI systems to create or modify content for end users
  • Distributors who make AI-generated content available to the public
  • Importers bringing AI-generated content into the EU market
  • Authorised representatives acting on behalf of non-EU providers

The Two-Layer Marking System

Article 50's transparency framework operates on two distinct but complementary layers. Both must be present for full compliance.

Layer 1
Visible Marking — for human readers
Layer 2
Machine-Readable Marking — for automated systems

🔵 Layer 1: Visible Marking

A perceptible label or indicator that clearly communicates to any human viewer that the content is AI-generated. Must be:

  • Clearly legible and understandable to end users
  • Prominently placed (not hidden, buried, or easily removed)
  • Appropriate to the medium — overlay on images, splash text on video, announcement in audio
  • Persistent — not removable through cropping, transcoding, or screenshotting
  • Language-appropriate for the target audience

🟡 Layer 2: Machine-Readable Marking

A technical marker embedded in the content's metadata, file headers, or digital signature that automated systems can detect and verify. Must be:

  • Embedded in a standardised format (C2PA, XMP, IPTC)
  • Persistent through common transformations (re-encoding, resizing, format conversion)
  • Tamper-evident — alterations to the content should invalidate the marker
  • Backwards-compatible with existing content management pipelines
  • Verifiable without requiring proprietary tooling

Implementation Guidance

For Text Content

AI-generated text outputs must include a clear statement that the text was AI-generated. This can be a header/banner reading "AI-Generated Content" or an inline disclosure at the start or end of the text. Machine-readable metadata should be embedded using schema.org/creativeWork encoding or XMP metadata in exported formats (PDF, DOCX).

For Image & Video Content

Images require a visible watermark or overlay (e.g. "AI-Generated" badge in the corner) plus C2PA-compliant provenance metadata embedded in the file. Video content additionally needs labelling at the start and end of playback, with a persistent logo overlay during the content.

For Audio Content

Synthetic or AI-manipulated audio must include a verbal disclosure at the beginning of the audio track ("This audio was generated by artificial intelligence"). Machine-readable audio watermarking should be applied using frequency-domain watermarking that survives compression and format transcoding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Article 50 of the EU AI Act?

Article 50 of the EU AI Act governs transparency obligations for providers and deployers of AI systems. It requires that AI-generated or manipulated content (text, image, audio, video) be clearly labelled as AI-generated, that deepfakes be disclosed, and that AI systems interacting with humans disclose their artificial nature. These obligations apply across the entire AI value chain, from foundation model providers to downstream deployers.

What is the two-layer marking requirement?

Article 50 mandates a two-layer approach: (1) a visible marking that humans can see — such as a label, watermark, or overlay reading "AI-generated" — and (2) a machine-readable marking embedded in the content's metadata or file headers that automated systems can detect. This dual-layer ensures both human awareness and automated compliance monitoring across the content lifecycle.

Who is responsible for AI labelling?

Responsibility is shared. Providers of general-purpose AI models must ensure technical capabilities for labelling exist. Deployers (the entities using the AI to generate or manipulate content) are primarily responsible for applying the visible and machine-readable labels to outputs. Both parties must cooperate to ensure compliance, and deployers cannot shift liability back to providers if they fail to implement labelling.

Does Article 50 apply to all AI-generated content?

Article 50 applies broadly to AI-generated or manipulated text, images, audio, and video content that could be mistaken for human-created or authentic content. Exceptions exist for content that is clearly AI-generated due to context (e.g., obviously fictional works), content that has undergone substantial human review and editorial control, and content that is lawfully authorised for legitimate purposes. However, these exceptions are narrowly construed.

When does Article 50 come into effect?

Article 50 obligations begin phasing in from May 2025. The deepfake and AI-generated content labelling requirements for deployers become enforceable from 2 August 2026. All deployers of AI systems that generate or manipulate content should implement compliance measures well before this date to avoid penalties of up to €15 million or 3% of annual worldwide turnover.
