The Fintech Article 50 Problem

The EU AI Act's Article 50 imposes mandatory transparency obligations on organisations deploying AI systems that interact with natural persons — and the FCA is watching. For fintech operators like Monzo, this means every customer-facing AI feature (chatbots, credit-decision models, fraud detection explanations, personalised nudges) must carry clear, auditable records of:

  • What model was invoked — including version, provenance, and training data lineage.
  • Why a decision was made — explainability records traceable to specific inputs and model parameters.
  • When and how a customer was informed — proof that the AI interaction was disclosed, with consent audit trails.
  • How the system was governed — risk classification, human oversight logs, and conformity documentation.

The challenge: Monzo (like most fintechs) operates dozens — sometimes hundreds — of micro-MCPs (Model Context Protocol servers) handling specific AI tasks across engineering, risk, customer operations, and compliance. Manually attesting each one is impossible at scale.
