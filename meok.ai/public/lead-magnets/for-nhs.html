The Healthcare AI Transparency Challenge

NHS Foundation Trusts are increasingly deploying AI across clinical pathways — from radiology triage and discharge summaries to genomics, surgical planning, and patient-facing chatbots. But the EU AI Act (Article 50), combined with CQC well-led and NHS DSP Toolkit requirements, demands transparent, auditable records for every AI interaction touching patients or their data.

  • Article 50(1): Any AI system interacting with a natural person (patient, carer, clinician) must disclose that interaction is AI-generated — with exceptions for clinical override.
  • Article 50(2): Emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation systems (used in some mental health & dementia care pilots) face enhanced transparency obligations.
  • CQC alignment: The Well-Led domain increasingly requires evidence of AI governance — board-level visibility into which AI systems are in use and how they are assured.
  • NHS DSP Toolkit: The Data Security and Protection Toolkit now includes AI-specific questions under Standard 7 (continuity) and Standard 8 (training).

The problem: Trusts often run AI models procured from third parties, built by in-house data science teams, or accessed via cloud APIs — each with different provenance, risk profiles, and documentation standards. Manually tracking compliance across 50+ AI services is unsustainable.
