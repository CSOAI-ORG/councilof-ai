What the Code Requires

Three Pillars of Compliance

The Code of Practice codifies three binding requirements for all general-purpose AI operators in the EU. Each must be documented, attested, and maintained.

🏷️

Transparency Marking

AI-generated content must be clearly labelled. Operators must disclose AI use in user-facing interactions, maintain output records, and ensure end-users can always distinguish synthetic from human-created material — in line with Article 50.

🛡️

Risk Management

A systematic risk framework covering model evaluation, bias testing, incident reporting, and continuous monitoring. Operators must classify risk levels, document mitigation measures, and conduct periodic reviews — per Articles 9–15.

👁️

Human Oversight

Meaningful human control over AI outputs, including override mechanisms, escalation workflows, and clear accountability structures. Operators must designate responsible persons and maintain audit trails — as mandated by Article 14.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about the EU AI Act Code of Practice and how MEOK helps you comply.

What is the EU AI Act Code of Practice? +
The EU AI Act Code of Practice, effective 2 August 2026, sets binding requirements for operators of general-purpose AI models. It mandates transparency marking (Article 50), risk management (Articles 9–15), and human oversight (Article 14) to ensure safe, accountable AI deployment across the European Union.
Who does the Code of Practice apply to? +
Any organisation that provides, deploys, or uses general-purpose AI models within the EU — including sovereign AI operators, enterprise AI teams, and independent developers — must comply by 2 August 2026 or face regulatory action.
What is transparency marking under Article 50? +
Transparency marking requires operators to clearly label AI-generated content, disclose the use of AI in user-facing interactions, and maintain records of model outputs so that end-users can always distinguish AI-generated material from human-created content.
